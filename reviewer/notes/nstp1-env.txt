THE STATE OF THE BROWN ENVIRONMENT
⚙ I. Human health depends on a healthy environment: clean water, clean air, good sanitation, and an adequate supply of food.
⚙ II. Environmental degradation:
⚙ Destructive and resource-extractive economic activities of man.
⚙ Air, water, and land pollution and natural resource depletion.
⚙ These continuously affect mankind, and vice versa.
★ Environmental degradation: damage to the environment through pollution and using up natural resources.
★ Resource depletion: using up natural resources faster than they can be replaced.

A HEALTHY COMMUNITY
★ A healthy community: one that creates and improves the physical and social environment, expands measures that let people support each other, and lets people perform all the functions of life and develop their maximum potential.
Healthy community principles:
⚙ A broad definition of health.
⚙ A shared vision from community values.
⚙ A collaborative, consensus-based approach to problem solving.
⚙ Address quality of life for everyone.
⚙ Diverse citizen participation and widespread community ownership.
⚙ Focus on systems change.
⚙ Build capacity using local assets and resources.
⚙ Benchmark and measure progress and outcomes.
★ Consensus: agreement reached by the whole group.
★ Benchmark: a standard used to measure progress.
Characteristics of a healthy community:
⚙ A clean and safe environment.
⚙ A diverse and vibrant economy.
⚙ Good housing for all.
⚙ People who respect and support each other.
⚙ A place that promotes and celebrates its cultural and historical heritage.
⚙ A place where citizens and government share power.
⚙ Affordable health care for all.
⚙ A good school.
⚙ Access to quality preventive and curative health care services for all.
Health determinants:
⚙ Peace, shelter, education.
⚙ Social security, social relations, social justice.
⚙ Food, income.
⚙ Empowerment of women.
⚙ A stable ecosystem, sustainable resources.
⚙ Respect for human rights, equity.
★ Health determinant: a factor that affects how healthy people are.

THE 5S PRINCIPLES
★ 1. Seiri (Sort, waste elimination): remove unnecessary items and dispose of them properly.
⚙ Seiri practices: disposal tags, a designated storage area, a disposal procedure.
★ 2. Seiton (Systematize, orderliness): arrange necessary items in good order so they can easily be picked up for use, and prevent loss and waste of time.
⚙ Seiton practices: label items and their locations, systematically mark everything, put frequently used items nearer to the user.
★ 3. Seiso (Sweep, cleanliness): clean your workplace completely and keep it safe, and prevent machinery and equipment from deteriorating.
⚙ Seiso practices: a big Seiso (clean-up) day, 3 to 5 minutes of clean-up daily, preventing causes of dust and dirt.
★ 4. Seiketsu (Standardize): maintain high standards of housekeeping and workplace organization at all times, maintain cleanliness and orderliness, prevent mis-operation, and make it easy to find abnormalities.
⚙ Seiketsu is the condition we keep when we maintain the first three S's.
★ 5. Shitsuke (Self-discipline): do things spontaneously without being told, enhance autonomous management, and maintain discipline. It means making a habit of keeping the set procedures.
⚙ Shitsuke practices: wash hands after going to the toilet, and before and after meals.
The slides spell it "SHIKUTSE"; the Japanese word is Shitsuke. The table also writes "SEIRE" for Seiri.
EASIER: Sort out what you don't need, set the rest in order, sweep it clean, standardize the habit, and sustain it with self-discipline.
3S among the 5S (the first three):
⚙ Seiri (Sort): take away unnecessary items, make work easy by removing obstacles, and prevent accumulation of unnecessary items.
⚙ Seiton (Systematize): arrange items in good order, prevent loss and waste of time, ensure first-come first-served, make work smooth and easy.
⚙ Seiso (Sweep): clean the community, make abnormalities easy to check, prevent equipment from deteriorating, keep the community safe and easy to work in.
Other benefits of 5S:
⚙ Improves creativity, communication, and teamwork; enhances comradeship; gives vitality to people.
Benefits of 5S:
⚙ 1. Opportunity to give creative input on how your workplace should be organized.
⚙ 2. A pleasant workplace.
⚙ 3. Work satisfaction.
⚙ 4. Removes obstacles and frustrations.
⚙ 5. Easier to communicate with everyone you work with.

PROPER WASTE MANAGEMENT
★ RA 9003: the Ecological Solid Waste Management (SWM) Act of 2000, which creates a multi-sectoral body tasked to institute a national framework for waste management.
The slides write "of 2003". RA 9003 was signed in January 2001 and is officially titled the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000. Use the slide's year only if a question copies the slide.
⚙ Success of RA 9003 lies in cooperation among sectors: consumers, advocacy groups, industries, recycling enterprises, LGUs, and others.
★ Ecological solid waste management: a systematic administration of activities that provide for segregation at source, segregated transport, storage, transfer, processing, and treatment of waste.
The 3 R's of solid waste management:
★ Reduce: reduce the amount of waste produced.
★ Reuse: reuse containers and materials for other purposes.
★ Recycle: convert waste materials, such as paper, into decor and other things.
Difference: reuse keeps the item as it is for a new use; recycle turns the material into something new.

WASTE SEGREGATION
★ Biodegradable: waste that rots naturally. Fruit and vegetable peelings, spoiled food and leftovers, vegetable trimmings, fish scales, egg shells, seafood shells, animal entrails and carcasses, corn cobs, rice hulls, peanut shells, wet newspapers and cardboard, coconut shells and husks, garden clippings, pet and livestock manure, chipped branches and sawdust.
★ Recyclables: waste that can be made into new things. Dry paper and newspapers, dry cardboard and cartons, plastic containers and materials, plastic water bottles, plastic bottle caps, tin cans, aluminum cans, iron and steel, broken glasses, glass bottles, toner and ink cartridges, computer casings, PVC and PE pipes.
★ Residuals: waste that cannot be composted or recycled. Sando bags, packing wrappers, styrofoam, cigarette butts, worn-out plastic sacks, dirt from sweepings, worn-out rags, sanitary napkins, disposable diapers, plastic straws, parlor and barber shop waste, household medicine bottles, broken ceramics, colored broken glass, coco fiber from cushions.
★ Special waste: hazardous household waste. Paint and thinner containers, household batteries, pharmaceutical waste, spray canisters, lead-acid batteries, used oil filters, broken tiles and lamps, expired cosmetics.
★ Bulky waste: large items. Destroyed TV sets, radios, stereos, washing machines, dryers, stoves, refrigerators, dishwashers, broken furniture, filing cabinets, bookcases, beds, cushions, garden debris.
EASIER: Rots: biodegradable. Can be remade: recyclable. Useless and can't be remade: residual. Dangerous: special. Too big: bulky.

PHILIPPINE ENVIRONMENTAL LAWS
★ RA 6969 (Toxic Substances and Hazardous and Nuclear Wastes Control Act of 1990): the state regulates, restricts, or prohibits the importation, manufacture, processing, sale, distribution, use, and disposal of chemical substances that present unreasonable risk or injury to health or the environment.
★ RA 8749 (Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999): the state protects and advances the people's right to a balanced and healthful ecology.
⚙ It promotes and protects the global environment to attain sustainable development.
⚙ It recognizes that the primary responsibility for cleaning the habitat and environment is area-based.
⚙ A clean and healthy environment is for the good of all and should be the concern of all.
⚙ It recognizes the primary responsibility of local government units to deal with environmental problems.
★ RA 9275 (Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004): the state pursues economic growth in a manner consistent with the protection and preservation of water.
⚙ It aims for the revival of the quality of fresh, brackish, and marine waters.
⚙ It provides a comprehensive management program for water pollution focusing on pollution prevention.
⚙ It streamlines the processes for preventing, controlling, and abating water pollution.
⚙ It promotes environmental strategies, economic instruments, and control mechanisms to protect water resources.
★ RA 9147 (Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act): the state conserves the country's wildlife resources and their habitats for sustainability.
⚙ Objectives: conserve and protect wildlife species and their habitats to promote ecological balance and biological diversity; regulate the collection and trade of wildlife; initiate and support scientific studies on the conservation of biological diversity.
★ RA 7942 (Philippine Mining Act of 1995): all mineral resources in public and private lands within the territory and exclusive economic zone of the Philippines are owned by the state.
⚙ It promotes their rational exploration, development, utilization, and conservation.
⚙ It combines the efforts of government and the private sector to enhance growth while safeguarding the environment.
⚙ It protects the rights of affected communities.
★ Brackish water: a mix of fresh and salt water, as in river mouths.
★ Exclusive economic zone: the sea area, up to 200 nautical miles from the coast, where a country has rights over resources.
★ Biodiversity: the variety of living things in an area.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Mixing up Seiri and Seiton. Seiri sorts out what is not needed; Seiton puts what is needed in order.
⚙ Mixing up Seiketsu and Shitsuke. Seiketsu standardizes; Shitsuke is self-discipline.
⚙ Calling sando bags or styrofoam recyclable. The slides list them as residuals.
⚙ Calling batteries or paint containers residual. They are special waste.
⚙ Mixing up the law numbers: 6969 toxic, 8749 air, 9275 water, 9147 wildlife, 7942 mining, 9003 solid waste.
