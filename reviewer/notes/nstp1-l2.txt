Lesson 2 Voters' Education
Why we vote, kinds of elections, how often they are held, registration, eligibility, and a voter's duties

WHY WE VOTE
⚙ The lesson opens with "Why do we vote?" and answers "Everything is political."
EASIER: Laws, prices, schools, and roads are all shaped by the officials we elect, so politics touches everyone's daily life.
"Just because you do not take an interest in politics doesn't mean politics won't take an interest in you!" (attributed in the lesson to Pericles)
★ Voters' education: a program intended to initiate political development.
⚙ Political development is best observed in the citizens' exercise of their right to vote.
⚙ The right to vote is primarily exercised during elections.
★ Political development: the active participation of all individuals in governmental activities and public affairs (from Lesson 1).

ELECTIONS
★ Election: the means by which the people choose their officials for definite and fixed periods.
★ Regular elections: national, local, barangay, BARMM, and Sangguniang Kabataan (SK) elections.
★ Special elections: plebiscite, referendum, initiative, and recall.
Difference: regular elections choose officials on a fixed schedule. Special elections decide a specific question or the fate of a specific official.

THE FOUR SPECIAL ELECTIONS
★ Plebiscite ("Do you agree with this change?"): voters decide on a proposed change or question, often involving territorial or constitutional matters.
★ Referendum ("Do you approve or reject this?"): voters directly approve or reject a proposed measure.
★ Initiative ("The people propose"): citizens propose legislation or changes through a process provided by law.
★ Recall ("We elected you, but we want to decide whether you should continue."): voters seek the removal of an elected local official under the law.
EASIER: Plebiscite is agreeing to a change, referendum is yes or no on a measure, initiative is the people writing the proposal, and recall is removing an official.
Difference: in a referendum the measure comes from the government and the people approve or reject it. In an initiative the people themselves propose it.
Difference: a plebiscite is about territorial or constitutional changes. A recall is about removing a local official.
Added example: creating a new province or city, or approving changes to the Constitution, needs a plebiscite.

HOW OFTEN ELECTIONS ARE HELD
⚙ President and Vice-President: every 6 years.
⚙ Senators, Representatives, and provincial, city, and municipal elective officials: every 3 years.
⚙ Barangay and SK: every 3 years.
⚙ BARMM: every 5 years.
★ BARMM (Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao): an autonomous region in the southern Philippines. The people elect the members of the Bangsamoro Parliament.
★ Autonomous region: a region that governs some of its own affairs, under the national government.
Lesson 1 lists ARMM elections every 3 years. ARMM was replaced by BARMM, and this lesson's slides use BARMM with elections every 5 years.

REGISTRATION
⚙ An individual who wants to take part in electoral activities must register.
★ Voter registration: the process by which a qualified Filipino citizen applies to be included in the official list of voters. It is handled by the Commission on Elections (COMELEC).
★ OEO (Office of the Election Officer): the local COMELEC office in each city or municipality.
⚙ Step 1: Go to your local COMELEC office or OEO.
⚙ Step 2: Fill out the voter registration application form.
⚙ Step 3: Submit the required identification or documentary requirements.
⚙ Step 4: Have your biometrics captured: photograph, fingerprints, and signature.
⚙ Step 5: Your application is processed, and once approved you are included in the voters' list.
★ Biometrics: body-based data used to identify a person, here the photograph, fingerprints, and signature.
★ Transfer of registration record: file the application with the COMELEC Office of the Election Officer (OEO) of your new city or municipality.

VOTING ELIGIBILITY
⚙ You must be a registered voter.
⚙ You must be a Filipino citizen.
⚙ You must be a resident of the Philippines for at least one (1) year and of the city or municipality for at least six (6) months immediately before the election.
⚙ You must not be otherwise disqualified by law.
The slides here do not repeat the age rule; Lesson 1 gives it: at least 18 years old.
EASIER: Registered, Filipino, 1 year in the country, 6 months in your town, and not disqualified.

A VOTER'S DUTIES
Before the elections:
⚙ Register.
⚙ Be informed.
⚙ Set specific guidelines in choosing government leaders.
During the elections:
⚙ Vote according to your conscience.
⚙ Protect the integrity of your own vote.
After the elections:
⚙ Be vigilant in the counting and canvassing of votes.
★ Canvassing: the official adding up of votes from different precincts to find the winners.
★ Vigilant: carefully watching for anything wrong.
EASIER: Before, get ready; during, vote honestly and guard your vote; after, watch the count.
The lesson ends with "Your vote matters."

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Mixing up referendum and initiative. Initiative comes from the people; a referendum approves or rejects a measure.
⚙ Mixing up plebiscite and recall. A plebiscite is about a change; a recall is about removing a local official.
⚙ Saying BARMM elections are every 3 years. This lesson says every 5 years.
⚙ Forgetting that barangay and SK officials are elected every 3 years, not 6.
⚙ Forgetting that biometrics means the photograph, fingerprints, and signature.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Know the five regular and four special elections.
⚙ Know the term numbers: 6, 3, 3, and 5 years.
⚙ Know the five registration steps in order.
⚙ Know the voter's duties before, during, and after elections.
