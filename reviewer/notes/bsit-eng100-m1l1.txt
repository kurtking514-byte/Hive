Module 1 Lesson 1 Principles of Communication
The core communication principles of Gamble and Gamble (2010) and Angell (1997)

TWO SETS OF PRINCIPLES
★ Gamble and Gamble (2010) stated several core communication principles.
★ Angell (1997) also stresses a set of communication principles.
EASIER: The lesson gives two lists. The first list is from Gamble and Gamble (2010). The second list is from Angell (1997). Remember each author with their year.
★ Gamble and Gamble's six principles: communication is dynamic; unrepeatable and irreversible; culturally linked; influenced by ethics; competence-based; and being transformed by media and technology.
★ Angell's seven principles: communication is a process; contextual; continuous; symbolic; culturally linked; collaborative; and ethical.
EASIER: Angell's principles help you understand how communication is done and what aspects are involved when we communicate with others.
★ Only two principles appear by name in both lists: culturally linked, and ethics (Gamble and Gamble say "influenced by ethics"; Angell says "ethical").

GAMBLE AND GAMBLE: COMMUNICATION IS DYNAMIC
★ Communication is dynamic: all its elements constantly interact with and affect each other.
★ Since all people are interconnected, whatever happens to one person determines in part what happens to others.
EASIER: Think of a group chat. One person's message changes how the others feel and reply, and their replies change the next message. Nothing in communication stands still or works alone.
Added example: If your friend arrives upset, the whole group's conversation becomes quieter and more careful. What happened to one person affected everyone else.

GAMBLE AND GAMBLE: UNREPEATABLE AND IRREVERSIBLE
★ Communication is unrepeatable: every human contact is unique. It has never happened before, and it will never happen again in just the same way.
★ A communication encounter affects and changes people, so the encounter can never happen in exactly the same way again.
★ Communication is irreversible: we can neither take back something we have said nor erase the effects of something we have done.
EASIER: Unrepeatable means you cannot replay the exact same moment. Irreversible means you cannot undo what was already said or done. Like toothpaste out of the tube, it cannot be pushed back in.
Unrepeatable is about the moment never happening the same way twice; irreversible is about not being able to take words or actions back.
Added example: You apologize after saying something hurtful. The apology helps, but it cannot erase the fact that the words were said and heard. That is irreversibility.

GAMBLE AND GAMBLE: CULTURALLY LINKED
★ Communication is culturally linked: cultural diversity, including race, ethnicity, gender, and age, influences the meanings we attribute to communication.
★ Cultural differences exist not only between persons who speak different languages but also between persons who speak the same language.
★ Every cultural group has its own rules or preferences for interaction.
★ When these cultural rules are ignored or unknown, we are likely to misinterpret the meaning of messages received and miscalculate the impact of messages sent.
EASIER: People read the same message differently depending on their culture, age, or gender. Two people who both speak English can still misunderstand each other because their groups follow different unwritten rules.

GAMBLE AND GAMBLE: INFLUENCED BY ETHICS
★ Every time we communicate, we decide implicitly or explicitly whether we will do so ethically.
EASIER: Implicitly means without saying it or even thinking about it directly; explicitly means openly and on purpose. Either way, every message carries an ethical choice.
★ Ethics includes the moral principles, values, and beliefs that the members of society use to guide behaviour.
★ Since communication has consequences, it involves judgment of right and wrong.
★ When the agreed-upon standards of behaviour are violated, the behaviour is judged unethical.
★ For example, most of us expect those with whom we interact to be honest, play fair, respect our rights, and accept responsibility for their actions.
EASIER: Society has shared rules about what is fair and honest. Breaking those shared rules when you communicate, such as lying, makes the behaviour unethical.

GAMBLE AND GAMBLE: COMPETENCE-BASED
★ We all have different communication strengths and weaknesses, but we can all benefit from getting better at communicating.
★ Communication competence is gained when we add to our knowledge and make a commitment to develop the skills to apply that knowledge across an array of communication situations or contexts.
EASIER: Competence means skill. Knowing the rules is not enough; you also practice using them in many kinds of situations, like a class report, a job interview, or a chat with family.
★ The ability to think critically is included among the skills necessary for effective communication.
★ Thinking critically is the ability to examine ideas reflectively and to decide what we should and should not believe, think, or do, given the specific set of circumstances.
EASIER: Critical thinking means you stop and check an idea before you accept it or act on it.
Added example: Before sharing a shocking post online, a student checks whether the claim is true and decides not to share it yet. That is critical thinking.

GAMBLE AND GAMBLE: TRANSFORMED BY MEDIA AND TECHNOLOGY
★ Media critic Marshall McLuhan cautioned, "The medium is the message."
★ In McLuhan's view, different channels of communication affect the way a sender encodes a message and the way a receiver responds to a message.
★ The words delivered face-to-face, on paper, or via radio or television do not constitute the same message. The channel of communication changes things.
EASIER: The medium or channel is how a message travels, such as face-to-face, a letter, the radio, or TV. The same words feel different depending on how they arrive.
Added example: "We need to talk" said kindly in person feels different from the same words sent as a late-night text. The words are the same, but the channel changes the message.
★ Not just the medium, but also its content, changes communication.
★ The content of books, newspapers, radio, television, and film also influences our cultural values.
★ Media content often reinforces the stereotypes we have of gender, race, and ethnicity, and contributes to the perceptions we have of various people and groups in society, including ourselves.
EASIER: What we watch and read shapes how we see other people and ourselves, sometimes by repeating unfair stereotypes.
★ Technology continues to speed up communication as it brings the world into our living rooms and bedrooms, offices and cars.
★ Instead of valuing sequential understanding and careful logic, we now value immediate gratification and emotional involvement.
EASIER: Because of technology, people now want quick, emotional content instead of slow, step-by-step reasoning.
★ Technology has also given us the ability to interact in more ways.

ANGELL (1997): SEVEN PRINCIPLES
★ Communication is a process: the exchange of messages is ongoing and dynamic.
★ Communication is contextual: our interactions with others occur during specific social situations, in different physical environments, and for a variety of purposes.
EASIER: Context is the situation. How you talk at a funeral is different from how you talk at a basketball game.
★ Communication is continuous: it is an ongoing process.
EASIER: Communication does not really stop; even silence or a facial expression keeps sending meaning.
★ Communication is symbolic: words or nonverbal modes may be used in communication.
EASIER: A symbol stands for something else. Words are symbols, and so are gestures like a thumbs-up or a nod.
★ Communication is culturally linked: our communication style is linked to the culture into which we are born.
★ Communication is collaborative: people work together to accomplish a goal.
EASIER: Communication is teamwork. Both sides help build the meaning.
★ Communication is ethical: ethics is a system of principles that guide the proper conduct of companies and individuals.
Process and continuous both mean ongoing; process adds that the exchange of messages is dynamic.
Gamble and Gamble describe ethics as moral principles, values, and beliefs that guide behaviour; Angell describes ethics as a system of principles that guide the proper conduct of companies and individuals.

COMMON MISTAKES
★ Do not mix up the authors and years: Gamble and Gamble is 2010; Angell is 1997.
★ Do not think cultural differences exist only between different languages; they also exist among people who speak the same language.
★ Do not think only the medium changes communication; the content of media also changes communication.
★ Do not confuse unrepeatable (no moment happens the same way twice) with irreversible (what was said or done cannot be taken back).
★ Do not say we decide to be ethical only on special occasions; we decide every time we communicate, implicitly or explicitly.

EXAM TIPS
★ Collaborative, symbolic, contextual, continuous, and process come from Angell; dynamic, unrepeatable and irreversible, competence-based, and transformed by media and technology come from Gamble and Gamble.
★ For a "medium is the message" question, the answer is Marshall McLuhan, a media critic.
★ Remember the culture factors named: race, ethnicity, gender, and age influence meaning; media stereotypes concern gender, race, and ethnicity.
