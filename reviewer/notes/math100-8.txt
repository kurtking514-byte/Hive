Module 8 Cryptography
Modular arithmetic, congruence, the Caesar cipher, cyclic coding with multiplicative inverses, and clock and calendar problems

MODULAR ARITHMETIC
★ Modular arithmetic: a system of arithmetic for integers where numbers "wrap around" after reaching a certain value, called the modulus (plural moduli).
★ The modern approach was developed by Carl Friedrich Gauss in his book Disquisitiones Arithmeticae, published in 1801.
★ $n \bmod m$: the remainder left after taking away as many multiples of m as possible. The answer is always from 0 up to m - 1.
★ Zn, the set of residues (residue classes) $\bmod n$: Zn = {0, 1, 2, ..., n - 1}.
⚙ Modulo 7 uses 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.
⚙ Modulo 5 uses 0, 1, 2, 3, 4.
⚙ In general, modulo n uses 0, 1, 2, up to n - 1.
EASIER: Think of a clock. After 12 it goes back to 1. Modular arithmetic is counting on a circle instead of a straight line.

HOW TO COMPUTE N MOD M
⚙ Step 1: If n is positive, subtract as many multiples of m as you can (or divide and keep the remainder).
⚙ Step 2: If n is negative, add multiples of m until the answer is between 0 and m - 1.
⚙ $17 \bmod 5 = 2 (17 - 15)$.
⚙ $20 \bmod 3 = 2 (20 - 18)$.
⚙ $25 \bmod 5 = 0$.
⚙ $21 \bmod 3 = 0$.
⚙ $7 \bmod 11 = 7$ (7 is already smaller than 11).
⚙ $11 \bmod 11 = 0$.
⚙ $-3 \bmod 11 = 8 (-3 + 11)$.
⚙ $-18 \bmod 7 = 3 (-18 + 21)$.
⚙ $-1 \bmod 11 = 10 (-1 + 11)$.
⚙ $-11 \bmod 11 = 0$.
⚙ $-8 \bmod 5 = 2 (-8 + 10)$.
⚙ $-25 \bmod 4 = 3 (-25 + 28)$.
Added example: a negative number $\bmod m$ is never negative. $-3 \bmod 11$ is 8, not -3 and not 3.

CONGRUENCE
★ Definition: $x ≡ y \pmod{z}$ if x - y is a multiple of z.
EASIER: Two numbers are congruent $\bmod z$ when they leave the same remainder after dividing by z.
⚙ $3 ≡ 15 \pmod{12}$, since $15 - 3 = 12$ is a multiple of 12.
⚙ $3 ≡ 27 \pmod{12}$, since $27 - 3 = 24$ is a multiple of 12.
⚙ $3 ≡ -9 \pmod{12}$, since $-9 - 3 = -12$ is a multiple of 12.
⚙ $0 ≡ 2 \pmod{2}$ and $0 ≡ -8 \pmod{2}$, since 2 and -8 are multiples of 2.
⚙ $1 ≡ 3 \pmod{2}$ and $1 ≡ -1 \pmod{2}$, since the differences are 2.

CRYPTOGRAPHY TERMS
★ Cryptography: the study of methods for sending secret messages.
★ Plaintext: the message before it is coded.
★ Ciphertext: the message after it has been coded.
★ Encryption: converting plaintext into ciphertext.
★ Decryption: converting ciphertext back into plaintext.
★ Cipher: an algorithm (a series of well-defined steps) for encryption or decryption.
★ Key: the secret value (like the shift number) used to encrypt and decrypt.
★ Substitution cipher: each letter is replaced with a letter some fixed number of positions away in the alphabet.
★ Shifted alphabet code or Caesar cipher: the same idea, shifting every letter by a fixed number.
⚙ In the past, cryptography was used mainly for government and military intelligence, and it still is.
⚙ Flow: Plaintext, then encryption with the key, then ciphertext, then decryption with the key, then plaintext again.

THE CAESAR CIPHER
★ Julius Caesar's cipher shifts each letter 3 places forward, with X wrapping to A, Y to B, and Z to C.
★ Encryption formula: $C = (P + 3) \bmod 26$.
★ Decryption formula: $P = (C - 3) \bmod 26$.
★ General shift k: $C = (P + k) \bmod 26$ and $P = (C - k) \bmod 26$.
⚙ Letter table for the Caesar cipher: $A=0$, $B=1$, $C=2$, $D=3$, $E=4$, $F=5$, $G=6$, $H=7$, $I=8$, $J=9$, $K=10$, $L=11$, $M=12$.
⚙ $N=13$, $O=14$, $P=15$, $Q=16$, $R=17$, $S=18$, $T=19$, $U=20$, $V=21$, $W=22$, $X=23$, $Y=24$, $Z=25$.
Note: the slide's text says $A = 01$ to $Z = 26$, but its table and worked examples use $A = 0$ to $Z = 25$. Use $A = 0$ for the Caesar examples.

EXAMPLE: ENCRYPT HOW ARE YOU (SHIFT 3)
⚙ Step 1: letters to numbers: 7 14 22, 0 17 4, 24 14 20.
⚙ Step 2: add $3 \pmod{26}$: 10 17 25, 3 20 7, 1 17 23.
⚙ Step 3: numbers to letters.
★ Ciphertext: KRZ DUH BRX.

EXAMPLE: DECRYPT L DP ILQH (SHIFT 3)
⚙ Step 1: letters to numbers: 11, 3 15, 8 11 16 7.
⚙ Step 2: subtract 3: 8, 0 12, 5 8 13 4.
⚙ Step 3: numbers to letters.
★ Plaintext: I AM FINE.

EXAMPLE: ENCRYPT PERICARDITIS (SHIFT 15)
⚙ $C = (P + 15) \bmod 26$.
⚙ P: $(15 + 15) \bmod 26 = 30 \bmod 26 = 4$, which is E.
⚙ E: $(4 + 15) \bmod 26 = 19$, which is T.
⚙ R: $(17 + 15) \bmod 26 = 32 \bmod 26 = 6$, which is G.
⚙ I: $(8 + 15) \bmod 26 = 23$, which is X.
⚙ C: $(2 + 15) \bmod 26 = 17$, which is R.
★ Ciphertext: ETGX RPGS XIXH.

EXAMPLE: DECRYPT QIL EMG ULN HIN QIL EBU LXU (SHIFT 20)
⚙ $P = (C - 20) \bmod 26$.
⚙ Q: $(16 - 20) \bmod 26 = -4 \bmod 26 = 22$, which is W.
⚙ I: $(8 - 20) \bmod 26 = -12 \bmod 26 = 14$, which is O.
⚙ L: $(11 - 20) \bmod 26 = -9 \bmod 26 = 17$, which is R.
⚙ E: $(4 - 20) \bmod 26 = -16 \bmod 26 = 10$, which is K.
★ Plaintext: WOR KSM ART NOT WOR KHA RDA, read as WORK SMART NOT WORK HARD.
Added example: the Lord Byron line SHE WALKS IN BEAUTY LIKE THE NIGHT becomes ODA SWHGO EJ XAWQPU HEGA PDA JECDP with a shift of 22 (the same as shifting back 4).

MULTIPLICATIVE INVERSE
★ Multiplicative inverse of $a \bmod m$: the number that, multiplied by a, gives a result congruent to $1 \bmod m$.
★ In cyclic coding, addition is undone by subtraction, but multiplication cannot be undone by dividing. You multiply by the inverse instead.
⚙ To find it: try 0, 1, 2, ... and stop when a times the number $\bmod m$ equals 1.
⚙ Inverse of $3 \bmod 5$ is 2, since $3 × 2 = 6$ and $6 \bmod 5 = 1$.
⚙ Inverse of $5 \bmod 7$ is 3, since $5 × 3 = 15$ and $15 \bmod 7 = 1$.
⚙ Inverse of $13 \bmod 9$ is 7, since $13 × 7 = 91$ and $91 \bmod 9 = 1$.
⚙ Inverse of $3 \bmod 26$ is 9, since $3 × 9 = 27$ and $27 \bmod 26 = 1$.
Note: one slide says "the multiplicative inverse of 7 with respect to modulo 7 is 3". It should say the inverse of $5 \bmod 7$ is 3.

INVERTING A CONGRUENCE
⚙ Step 1: undo the addition or subtraction (move the constant to the c side).
⚙ Step 2: multiply both sides by the inverse of the multiplier.
⚙ Step 3: write it as p ≡ (expression in c) $\bmod m$.
EXAMPLE: INVERT $C ≡ 3P$ MOD 5
⚙ The inverse of $3 \bmod 5$ is 2.
⚙ Multiply both sides by 2: $2c ≡ 6p ≡ p \pmod{5}$.
★ $p ≡ 2c \pmod{5}$.
EXAMPLE: INVERT $C ≡ (5P - 1)$ MOD 7
⚙ Add 1: $c + 1 ≡ 5p \pmod{7}$.
⚙ The inverse of $5 \bmod 7$ is 3; multiply: $3(c + 1) ≡ p \pmod{7}$.
★ $p ≡ 3(c + 1) \pmod{7}$.
EXAMPLE: INVERT $C ≡ (13P + 5)$ MOD 9
⚙ Subtract 5: $c - 5 ≡ 13p \pmod{9}$.
⚙ The inverse of $13 \bmod 9$ is 7; multiply: $7(c - 5) ≡ p \pmod{9}$.
★ $p ≡ 7(c - 5) \pmod{9}$.
★ Apart from using the inverse instead of dividing, solving a congruence is like solving an equation.

CYCLIC CODING
★ Cyclic coding uses a congruence such as $c ≡ (p + 5) \bmod 26$ to code letters.
⚙ Cyclic coding table: $A=1$, $B=2$, $C=3$, up to $Z=26$ (Z counts as 0 when the result is $0 \bmod 26$).
Difference: the Caesar examples use $A = 0$ to $Z = 25$; the cyclic coding examples use $A = 1$ to $Z = 26$. Check which table the problem uses.
EXAMPLE: DECODE OTFS I'FWH WITH $C ≡ (P + 5)$ MOD 26
⚙ Invert: $p ≡ (c - 5) \bmod 26$.
⚙ O: $15 - 5 = 10$, which is J.
⚙ T: $20 - 5 = 15$, which is O.
⚙ F: $6 - 5 = 1$, which is A.
⚙ S: $19 - 5 = 14$, which is N.
⚙ I: $9 - 5 = 4$, which is D.
⚙ W: $23 - 5 = 18$, which is R.
⚙ H: $8 - 5 = 3$, which is C.
★ Plaintext: JOAN D'ARC.
EXAMPLE: DECODE FKDNQRWVPSCXAEJ WITH $C ≡ (3P - 5)$ MOD 26
⚙ Add 5: $c + 5 ≡ 3p \pmod{26}$.
⚙ The inverse of $3 \bmod 26$ is 9, so $p ≡ 9(c + 5) \bmod 26$.
⚙ $F = 6$: $9(6 + 5) = 99$, and $99 \bmod 26 = 21$, which is U.
⚙ $K = 11$: $9(11 + 5) = 144$, and $144 \bmod 26 = 14$, which is N.
★ Plaintext (answer key): UNCOPYRIGHTABLE.

EXTENDED EUCLIDEAN ALGORITHM
★ A method for finding the multiplicative inverse when trying numbers one by one would take too long.
⚙ Inverse of $13 \bmod 22$: divide $22 = 1(13) + 9$, $13 = 1(9) + 4$, $9 = 2(4) + 1$, $4 = 4(1) + 0$. The result is 17.
⚙ Check: $17 × 13 = 221$, and $221 \bmod 22 = 1$.
⚙ Inverse of $17 \bmod 97$: $97 = 5(17) + 12$, $17 = 1(12) + 5$, $12 = 2(5) + 2$, $5 = 2(2) + 1$, $2 = 2(1) + 0$. The result is 40.
⚙ Check: $40 × 17 = 680$, and $680 \bmod 97 = 1$.

CLOCKS AND CALENDARS
★ Clocks start over after 12 or 24 hours; weeks start over after 7 days; a year has 52 weeks or 365 days.
⚙ To move forward or backward in time, divide the hours by 24: the whole part is days and the remainder is hours.
EXAMPLE: CLASS AT 2:30 PM THURSDAY
⚙ Due 145 hours later: $145 ÷ 24 = 6$ days remainder 1 hour.
⚙ Thursday + 6 days = Wednesday; 2:30 PM + 1 hour = 3:30 PM.
★ Due Wednesday at 3:30 PM.
⚙ Assigned 80 hours before: $80 ÷ 24 = 3$ days remainder 8 hours.
⚙ Thursday - 3 days = Monday; 2:30 PM - 8 hours = 6:30 AM.
★ Assigned Monday at 6:30 AM.

⚙ NCTXTYLW NLDP (shift 15): CRIMINAL CASE.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Leaving a negative answer: $-4 \bmod 26$ is 22, not -4.
⚙ Dividing to undo multiplication. Use the multiplicative inverse.
⚙ Mixing up the two letter tables ($A = 0$ for Caesar, $A = 1$ for cyclic coding).
⚙ Subtracting the key when encrypting or adding it when decrypting.
⚙ In clock problems, forgetting the remainder hours after counting whole days.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Encrypt: $C = (P + k) \bmod 26$. Decrypt: $P = (C - k) \bmod 26$.
⚙ Find an inverse by testing numbers until the product $\bmod m$ is 1.
⚙ Always check your answer by encrypting it again.

SELF-TEST
Q: What is $-18 \bmod 7$?
★ 3.
Q: Is $3 ≡ 27 \pmod{12}$?
★ Yes, because $27 - 3 = 24$ is a multiple of 12.
Q: Encrypt the letter Y with the Caesar cipher (shift 3).
★ $Y = 24$; $24 + 3 = 27$; $27 \bmod 26 = 1$, which is B.
Q: What is the multiplicative inverse of $3 \bmod 5$?
★ 2.
Q: Invert $c ≡ (5p - 1) \bmod 7$.
★ $p ≡ 3(c + 1) \bmod 7$.
Q: A paper is due 50 hours after 9:00 AM Monday. When is it due?
★ $50 = 2$ days and 2 hours, so Wednesday at 11:00 AM.
