Peace Education
What peace means, the four levels of peace, a culture of peace, nonviolent action, and the role of schools

WHAT IS PEACE
⚙ Peace is more than the absence of war.
★ Peace: a state of harmony, understanding, and tranquility, built on social justice, respect, tolerance, and the well-being of everyone.
EASIER: No fighting is not enough. Real peace means people get along, are treated fairly, and are well.
★ Peace education: teaching the values, attitudes, and skills people need to live in peace with themselves, others, and the environment.

THE FOUR LEVELS OF PEACE
★ Personal peace: peace within yourself. It includes self-respect and confidence, coping with fear, anger, insecurity, and shame, and being cheerful and optimistic.
★ Interpersonal peace: peace in our relationships with others. It includes politely saying no, listening respectfully, and humility.
★ Social or national peace: peace in the community and the nation. It includes social responsibility, interdependence, and social justice.
★ Global peace: peace among nations. It deals with unfair trade relations, racial discrimination, terrorism, militarization, environmental degradation, and xenophobia.
⚙ Interdependence example from the lecture: one household throwing garbage carelessly can clog drains and cause flooding for the whole community.
★ Interdependence: people depend on each other, so one person's actions affect everyone.
★ Social justice: fair treatment and fair sharing of rights and opportunities in society.
★ Xenophobia: fear, dislike, or prejudice toward people seen as foreigners.
★ Militarization: building up weapons and armed forces, or relying on military force.
★ Environmental degradation: damage to the environment, such as pollution and deforestation.
EASIER: The levels go from inside you, to your relationships, to your country, to the whole world.

CULTURE OF PEACE
⚙ Building a culture of peace needs a holistic response. The lecture names these areas:
⚙ Dismantling the culture of war (militarization).
⚙ Addressing structural violence.
⚙ Promoting human rights.
⚙ Cultural solidarity.
⚙ Caring for the environment.
⚙ Personal peace.
★ Structural violence: harm caused by unfair social systems that keep people's basic needs unmet. Example: children who cannot go to school because no school is accessible.
★ Holistic: looking at the whole, not just one part.
Difference: direct violence is someone hurting someone; structural violence is harm built into how society is organized.
A culture of peace is shown when people:
⚙ Understand global problems.
⚙ Have skills to resolve conflicts.
⚙ Struggle for justice nonviolently.
⚙ Respect human rights and equity.
⚙ Appreciate cultural diversity.
⚙ Respect the earth.

⚙ Understanding of a multicultural society.
⚙ Peace among young people and among nations.
⚙ Peaceful conflict resolution. Example: two classmates who both want the same book agree to take turns.
⚙ Love and concern for others.
⚙ Self-esteem.
⚙ Cooperation.
⚙ Communication skills.
⚙ Environmental awareness.
⚙ Multicultural understanding.
⚙ Global awareness.
⚙ Inner peace.
★ Multicultural: made up of many cultures living together.

FIVE STEPS OF NONVIOLENT ACTION
⚙ 1. Information gathering: find out the facts about the problem.
⚙ 2. Education: inform others about the problem.
⚙ 3. Personal commitment: decide to act and do your part.
⚙ 4. Negotiation: talk with the other side to find a fair solution.
⚙ 5. Reconciliation and healing: restore the relationship after the conflict.
⚙ Lecture example: improper garbage disposal in a community. Learn the facts, tell neighbors about it, commit to dispose of your own waste properly, talk with those involved, and rebuild good relations.
★ Nonviolent action: solving conflicts and injustice without using force or harm.
EASIER: Know the facts, tell others, commit, talk it out, then make peace.

ROLE OF SCHOOLS
⚙ 1. Use peaceful pedagogies (teaching methods).
⚙ 2. Teach disarmament education and avoid glorifying violence, such as war toys.
⚙ 3. Avoid sexist education and sexist language.
⚙ 4. Use cooperative and collaborative activities.
⚙ 5. Discuss the roots of conflicts.
⚙ 6. Promote intercultural dialogue and tolerance.
★ Pedagogy: the method and practice of teaching.
★ Disarmament: reducing or removing weapons.
★ Intercultural dialogue: open, respectful exchange between people of different cultures.

TAKEAWAYS
⚙ Peace is more than the absence of war.
⚙ Peace starts within each person and grows to relationships, the nation, and the world.
⚙ Everyone, including schools and students, has a part in building a culture of peace.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Defining peace only as no war.
⚙ Putting xenophobia or terrorism under personal peace. They are global issues.
⚙ Putting humility or politely saying no under personal peace. They are interpersonal.
⚙ Mixing up the order of nonviolent action. Information gathering is first; reconciliation and healing is last.
⚙ Thinking structural violence means physical fighting. It is harm from unfair systems.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Know the four levels of peace and what belongs to each.
⚙ Know the meaning of structural violence and xenophobia.
⚙ Know the five steps of nonviolent action in order.
⚙ Know the six roles of schools.
