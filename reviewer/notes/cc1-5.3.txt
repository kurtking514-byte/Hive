5.3 Project Management Methodology
PMBOK, ISO 21500, PRINCE2, Agile (Scrum, Kanban, XP), and the ten project management knowledge areas

KEY TERMS
★ Project management methodology: a structured approach to planning, executing, and completing projects. It gives frameworks, principles, and practices that guide managers and teams.
★ PMBOK (Project Management Body of Knowledge): a globally recognized guide of standards and best practices for project management, issued by the Project Management Institute (PMI).
★ ISO 21500: an international standard for project management, published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO).
★ PRINCE2 (PRojects IN Controlled Environments): a process-driven methodology first developed by the UK government. It stresses business justification and a controlled, flexible structure.
★ Agile: an iterative approach that divides project work into smaller, manageable pieces called iterations or sprints. Each one produces a working product or feature that is reviewed and adjusted from stakeholder feedback.
★ Iteration (sprint): one short cycle of Agile work that ends with something usable.
★ Agile Manifesto: the 2001 document by a group of software developers that states Agile's core values and principles.
★ Scrum: an Agile framework that organizes work into sprints of usually 2 to 4 weeks, each producing a potentially shippable product increment.
★ Kanban: an Agile framework that visualizes the workflow and limits work in progress, with continuous flow instead of fixed sprints.
★ Extreme Programming (XP): an Agile framework focused on technical practices such as test-driven development, pair programming, and continuous integration.
★ Knowledge areas: the ten areas of project management that must be planned and managed, from integration to stakeholders.
★ Scope creep: uncontrolled changes to the project scope.
★ PMP (Project Management Professional): the globally recognized certification connected to PMBOK and PMI.
EASIER: A methodology is like a recipe style. PMBOK is a big cookbook of best practices, ISO 21500 is the official international standard, PRINCE2 is a strict step-by-step recipe with checkpoints, and Agile is tasting and adjusting the dish as you cook.

WHY METHODOLOGIES ARE USED
Methodologies standardize processes, promote consistency, and increase the chance of project success.
By following an established methodology, managers and teams agree on processes, maintain quality, and meet objectives efficiently.
Three widely recognized methodologies are PMBOK, ISO 21500, and PRINCE2. Agile is an alternative approach.

PMBOK (PROJECT MANAGEMENT BODY OF KNOWLEDGE)
PMBOK is issued by the Project Management Institute (PMI).
★ PMBOK organizes project management into five process groups and ten knowledge areas.
⚙ Process group 1: Initiating. Define and authorize the project.
⚙ Process group 2: Planning. Establish the project scope, objectives, and procedures.
⚙ Process group 3: Executing. Complete the work defined in the project plan.
⚙ Process group 4: Monitoring and Controlling. Track and regulate project performance.
⚙ Process group 5: Closing. Conclude all project activities and formally close the project.
⚙ Knowledge areas: Integration, Scope, Time (Schedule), Cost, Quality, Resource, Communication, Risk, Procurement, and Stakeholder Management.
PMBOK's approach emphasizes consistency and is widely applicable across industries.
⚙ Benefit: gives a structured framework for projects of any size or complexity.
⚙ Benefit: its globally recognized certification (PMP) increases a project manager's credibility.

ISO 21500 STANDARD
ISO 21500 is published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO).
It aligns closely with PMBOK but emphasizes aligning projects with organizational strategy.
⚙ Core principle: Alignment with Organizational Goals. Projects should contribute to strategic objectives.
⚙ Core principle: Standardization and Adaptability. A structured approach that still allows flexibility for a project's specific needs.
⚙ Core principle: Project Life Cycle. Covers phases similar to PMBOK: Initiating, Planning, Implementing, Controlling, and Closing.
⚙ Benefit: focuses on organizational alignment through project activities.
⚙ Benefit: gives a globally accepted standard, which makes projects easier to compare and improves quality.
Difference: PMBOK (from PMI) is a guide of best practices. ISO 21500 (from ISO) is an international standard whose main emphasis is fitting projects to the organization's strategy.

PRINCE2 (PROJECTS IN CONTROLLED ENVIRONMENTS)
PRINCE2 is process-driven and was first developed by the UK government.
It emphasizes business justification and a controlled, flexible project structure.
⚙ Key component: Seven Principles. Core guidelines, including continued business justification, defined roles, and learning from experience.
⚙ Key component: Seven Themes. Important areas such as business case, risk, quality, and change.
⚙ Key component: Seven Processes. The project stages, from starting up a project to closing a project.
Added example: the full seven principles are continued business justification, learn from experience, defined roles and responsibilities, manage by stages, manage by exception, focus on products, and tailor to suit the project.
Added example: the full seven themes are business case, organization, quality, plans, risk, change, and progress.
Added example: the full seven processes are starting up a project, directing a project, initiating a project, controlling a stage, managing product delivery, managing a stage boundary, and closing a project.
⚙ Advantage: Business Case Focus. Projects must have a justified purpose.
⚙ Advantage: Process-Driven. Clear processes and roles help teams understand their responsibilities.
⚙ Advantage: Scalability. PRINCE2 can be tailored to the project's size and complexity.
PRINCE2 is highly valued in government and the public sector, and it promotes transparency and control over each phase.
EASIER: Remember PRINCE2 as "7, 7, 7": seven principles (the rules), seven themes (the topics to watch), seven processes (the steps).

AGILE METHODOLOGY
Agile is an alternative to traditional project management.
Instead of a strict sequence of steps, Agile emphasizes flexibility, iterative progress, and customer collaboration.
It works best where requirements are likely to change, such as software and game development.
Traditional methods assume the factors affecting a project are predictable, so most planning happens before the project starts. Agile does not require all planning at the beginning; the product evolves through the life cycle.
In Agile the team cycles through planning, executing, and evaluating again and again. At the end of each interval the team meets, evaluates, and reassesses what to do next.
⚙ Benefit: its iterative nature allows a rapid response to changing requirements.
⚙ Benefit: stakeholder involvement throughout the project keeps the product aligned with user needs.
The video adds that Agile's iterative nature reduces risk and gives a better end product when unexpected events happen.
Difference: traditional methods plan most things up front and follow a fixed sequence. Agile plans a little, builds, gets feedback, and adjusts in repeated cycles.

EXAMPLE: THE AGILE BURGER
⚙ Iteration 1: a chef and team make a first burger and a customer rates it 5 out of 10, saying bacon would help.
⚙ Iteration 2: the chef adds bacon and the rating rises to 6 out of 10.
⚙ Later iterations: cheese, mushrooms, and pickles are added after more feedback, and satisfaction keeps rising.
⚙ Result: through short feedback loops the burger becomes the "world's best burger", because each version moves closer to what customers want.
EASIER: Agile means delivering a small version, asking for feedback, and improving it, instead of building the whole thing once and hoping it is right.

AGILE MANIFESTO
The Agile Manifesto was introduced in 2001 by a group of software developers. Its values focus on flexibility, collaboration, and delivering working products quickly.
⚙ Value 1: Individuals and Interactions. Collaboration and communication among team members and stakeholders.
⚙ Value 2: Working Software. Deliver a functional product rather than focusing on extensive documentation.
⚙ Value 3: Customer Collaboration. Customers are actively involved throughout the project.
⚙ Value 4: Responding to Change. Scope and approach can be adjusted as requirements evolve.
Added example: the Manifesto writes them as individuals and interactions over processes and tools, working software over comprehensive documentation, customer collaboration over contract negotiation, and responding to change over following a plan.
★ Agile has twelve guiding principles, including customer satisfaction, welcoming changing requirements, frequent delivery, sustainable development, and continuous attention to technical excellence.
Difference: the Manifesto has four values and twelve principles. Do not mix the two numbers.

EXAMPLES OF AGILE METHODOLOGIES
These Agile frameworks help teams adapt quickly, stay engaged with stakeholders, and deliver improvements in increments.
★ Scrum: work is organized into sprints, short cycles of usually 2 to 4 weeks, each producing a potentially shippable product increment.
⚙ Scrum role: Scrum Master. Facilitates the process and removes obstacles.
⚙ Scrum role: Product Owner. Defines the project requirements and priorities.
⚙ Scrum role: Development Team. Completes the work and creates the deliverables.
★ Kanban: focuses on visualizing the workflow and limiting work in progress to improve efficiency.
⚙ Kanban Board: a tool with columns for the stages of work, for example To Do, In Progress, and Done.
⚙ Kanban allows continuous flow instead of fixed sprints.
★ Extreme Programming (XP): emphasizes technical practices such as test-driven development, pair programming, and continuous integration. It is designed to improve software quality and responsiveness to customer requirements.
Difference: Scrum works in fixed-length sprints. Kanban has no sprints; work flows continuously across the board.
Difference: the Scrum Master runs the process and clears obstacles. The Product Owner decides what to build and in what priority.
EASIER: Scrum is a 2 to 4 week race with a finish line each time. Kanban is a conveyor belt that never stops. XP is a set of coding habits for writing better software.

THE TEN PROJECT MANAGEMENT KNOWLEDGE AREAS
The ten key areas cover every dimension of a project, from integration to risk. Stakeholders have needs and expectations, and the knowledge areas help managers plan and run the project to meet them.
Integration sits in the center because all the other areas are connected to it.
⚙ Project Integration Management: coordinates all project elements. It creates and maintains the project plan, manages project changes, and keeps every part aligned with the overall objectives.
⚙ Scope Management: defines the work required and prevents scope creep. It involves scope planning, definition, verification, and control.
⚙ Schedule (Time) Management: creates and controls the timeline. It involves defining tasks, sequencing them, estimating durations, and building a schedule that is monitored throughout.
⚙ Cost Management: budgeting and financial control so the project finishes within the allocated budget. It involves cost estimating, budgeting, and monitoring costs.
⚙ Resource Management: uses the team and other resources efficiently. It covers human resources (team management) and physical resources (materials, equipment).
⚙ Communication Management: sets protocols for distributing information among stakeholders so everyone stays informed and aligned.
⚙ Quality Management: makes sure deliverables meet the defined quality standards. It involves quality planning, assurance, and control.
⚙ Procurement Management: acquiring goods and services from external vendors. It includes contract negotiation, supplier selection, and vendor management.
⚙ Risk Management: identifies, assesses, and mitigates risks. Being proactive reduces uncertainty and prepares the team for possible issues.
⚙ Stakeholder Management: identifies and engages the people or groups affected by the project and addresses their needs and expectations.
The video adds that good tools and techniques support each area, for example a work breakdown structure for scope and a schedule for time, and using them well across all projects leads to portfolio and enterprise success.
EASIER: Procurement is another word for purchasing or buying.

EXAMPLE: KNOWLEDGE AREAS IN A HOUSE RENOVATION
⚙ Scope: the project covers renovating the kitchen, bathroom, and deck.
⚙ Time: the old kitchen must be removed before the new one is installed.
⚙ Cost: work out the budget and keep the project within it.
⚙ Quality: if the specifications say stainless steel appliances, check that stainless steel appliances arrive.
⚙ Resource: assign workers to tasks and manage their performance.
⚙ Communication: make sure everyone communicates effectively throughout.
⚙ Risk: a worker could get injured, so plan to prevent and handle it.
⚙ Procurement: buy cabinetry, appliances, tiles, wood, and hire tradespeople.
⚙ Stakeholder: the homeowner, contractor, suppliers, tradespeople, and sometimes the local government or council.
⚙ Integration: check that the scope fits the time available, costs match the scope and quality, and the right people are in place.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Saying PMBOK is published by ISO. PMBOK is from PMI; ISO 21500 is from ISO.
⚙ Leaving out Integration when listing the ten knowledge areas. The lecture's PMBOK bullet lists only nine; Integration is the tenth.
⚙ Saying PRINCE2 means "Project Integration in Controlled Environments". It means PRojects IN Controlled Environments.
⚙ Saying Kanban uses sprints. Kanban uses continuous flow; Scrum uses sprints.
⚙ Mixing up the Product Owner (requirements and priorities) and the Scrum Master (process and obstacles).
⚙ Saying the Agile Manifesto has twelve values. It has four values and twelve principles.
⚙ Confusing Procurement Management (buying from outside vendors) with Resource Management (using the team and materials you have).

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Match the source: PMBOK to PMI, ISO 21500 to ISO, PRINCE2 to the UK government, Agile Manifesto to software developers in 2001.
⚙ "Organizational strategy" points to ISO 21500. "Business justification" points to PRINCE2. "Consistency across industries" points to PMBOK.
⚙ Numbers to remember: PMBOK has 5 process groups and 10 knowledge areas; PRINCE2 has 7 principles, 7 themes, 7 processes; Agile has 4 values and 12 principles; Scrum sprints are usually 2 to 4 weeks.
⚙ "Scope creep" points to Scope Management. "Vendors" or "contracts" point to Procurement Management.
⚙ Test-driven development, pair programming, and continuous integration point to XP.
