GENDER AND DEVELOPMENT (GAD)
★ Gender and Development (GAD): the development perspective and process that is participatory and empowering, equitable, sustainable, free from violence, respectful of human rights, and supportive of self-determination and actualization of human potentials (Commission on Population, Philippines).
⚙ GAD seeks to achieve gender equality as a fundamental value that should be reflected in development choices.
⚙ GAD holds that women are active agents of development, not just passive recipients of development.
⚙ GAD focuses on the socially constructed basis of differences between men and women.
⚙ It emphasizes the need to challenge existing gender roles and relations.
⚙ GAD is not concerned specifically with women, but with the way society assigns roles, responsibilities, and expectations to both women and men.
⚙ GAD applies gender analysis to uncover how men and women work together, presenting results in neutral terms of economics and efficiency.
EASIER: GAD is about how society treats both men and women, and making development fair to both.

EQUALITY AT WORK (ILO)
⚙ The International Labor Organization (ILO) lists seven elements of equality between women and men at work:
⚙ 1. Equality of opportunity and treatment in employment.
⚙ 2. Equal remuneration for work of equal value.
⚙ 3. Equal access to safe and healthy working environments and to social security.
⚙ 4. Equality in association and collective bargaining.
⚙ 5. Equality in obtaining meaningful career development.
⚙ 6. A balance between work and home life that is fair to both women and men.
⚙ 7. Equal participation in decision-making at all levels.
★ Remuneration: pay or salary for work.
★ Collective bargaining: workers negotiating as a group with their employer about pay and working conditions.

GENDER EQUALITY
★ Gender equality: achieved when women and men enjoy the same rights and opportunities across all sectors of society, including economic participation and decision-making, and when the different behaviors, aspirations, and needs of women and men are equally valued and favored.
★ How gender equality is measured: by looking at the representation of men and of women in a range of roles.
⚙ UNESCO: gender equality does not mean that women and men have to become the same, but that their rights, responsibilities, and opportunities will not depend on whether they were born male or female.
★ Gender equity: fairness of treatment for men and women according to their respective needs. This may be equal treatment, or treatment that is different but considered equivalent in rights, benefits, obligations, and opportunities (UNESCO).
Difference: equality is the same rights and opportunities for both; equity is fair treatment based on each one's needs, which may be different treatment.
Added example: giving every student the same exam is equality; giving a student with poor eyesight a large-print copy is equity.

SEX AND GENDER
★ Sex: the anatomical and other biological differences between females and males, determined at the moment of conception and developing in the womb and throughout childhood and adolescence.
★ Gender: a social concept; the social and cultural differences a society assigns to people based on their biological sex.
★ Gender roles: a society's expectations of people's behavior and attitudes based on whether they are female or male.
"An extremely important part of an individual's identity, and one that begins at least at birth, is gender." (Dr. Kenneth Cushner, Human Diversity in Education: An Intercultural Approach)
⚙ Gender roles can be affected by race, ethnicity, social class, religion, and geographical region (Cushner, 2012).
The module spells the name as "Crushner" in the citation; the author is Cushner.
Sex:
⚙ Categorized as male or female.
⚙ Biological.
⚙ Fixed at birth.
⚙ Does not change across time and space.
⚙ Equally valued.
Gender:
⚙ Masculinity and femininity.
⚙ Socially, culturally, and historically determined.
⚙ Learned through socialization.
⚙ Varies over time and space.
⚙ Unequally valued (masculinity as the norm).
EASIER: Sex is the body you are born with; gender is what society expects because of it.

GENDER STEREOTYPES
⚙ Gender stereotypes can be a result of gender socialization: girls and boys are expected to act in certain ways, taught from birth.
⚙ People who do not conform are often ostracized (excluded) by peers for being different.
★ Gender stereotype: a form of prejudgment, bias, or limitation given to roles and expectations of males and females.
⚙ People are usually socialized to see gender as a masculine-feminine binary (only two options), but some people's gender identity does not match their assigned gender, so the binary is not universal.
⚙ Sociologists use gender socialization to explain why males and females behave differently. Example: girls learn to bake and clean; boys learn to mow lawns and take out the garbage.
⚙ Gender socialization happens through parental attitudes, schools, peer interaction, and mass media.
⚙ Gender roles can lead to inequality. Example: women were once restricted in politics, and United States law once prohibited women from voting.
⚙ Some researchers believe biological differences underlie some behavioral differences; others disagree.
★ Transgender: a person whose gender identity does not match their assigned sex.
★ Genderqueer: a person whose gender identity challenges the classifications of masculine and feminine; they may identify as neither, in between, a combination, or another gender altogether.
★ Gender binary: the idea that there are only two genders, masculine (male) and feminine (female).

THREE ASPECTS OF GENDER DIFFERENTIATION
⚙ 1. Social roles. 2. Capacities. 3. Traits and characteristics.
Stereotypes in social roles:
⚙ Men: provide financially for the family; work as managers, construction builders, engineers; portrayed as leaders.
⚙ Women: take care of the household and children; work as nurse, teacher, secretary; portrayed as followers.
Stereotypes in capacities:
⚙ Men: good in Math and Science; physically strong; firm decision-makers.
⚙ Women: good in arts and less intellectual pursuits; physically weaker and fragile; wishy-washy or fickle-minded in decision-making.
Stereotypes in traits and characteristics:
⚙ Men: active, aloof, aggressive, independent, brave.
⚙ Women: passive, loving, peaceful, dependent, fearful.
EASIER: Roles are what jobs people are given, capacities are what they are thought able to do, and traits are what personality they are expected to have.

GENDER SOCIALIZATION
★ Gender socialization: the process by which individuals are taught how to behave socially in accordance with their assigned gender, which is assigned at birth based on their sex phenotype.
⚙ It also means educating and instructing males and females in the norms, behaviors, values, and beliefs of group membership as men or women.
⚙ Today it is largely believed that most gender expression differences come from socialization rather than genetic and biological factors.
★ Phenotype: the observable physical traits of a person.
⚙ Areas of gender socialization: Family, Church, Mass Media, School.

FAMILY: THE FOUR PROCESSES
★ Manipulation: people handle girls and boys differently as infants.
⚙ Mothers tend to use more physical stimulation on male infants and more verbal stimulation on female infants.
⚙ Boy babies are tossed in the air; girl babies get more delicate handling.
★ Canalization: people direct children's attention to gender-appropriate objects.
⚙ Boys are given toy cars and machines; girls are given dolls and tea sets. Toys teach children what their prescribed roles in life will be.
★ Verbal appellation: telling children what they are and what is expected of them.
⚙ "Brave boy, pretty girl." "Boys don't cry, girls don't hit playmates."
★ Activity exposure: familiarizing children with their gender-appropriate tasks.
⚙ Girls help their mother with housework; boys are encouraged to play outside the house.
EASIER: Manipulation is how babies are handled, canalization is toys and objects, verbal appellation is words and labels, and activity exposure is chores and activities.

GENDER-FREE EDUCATION
⚙ 1. Shared parenting: taking care of the baby, helping the children with homework, disciplining the children.
⚙ 2. Shared decision-making: family council, giving permission to children, deciding on the children's affairs.
⚙ 3. Shared home management: fixing and cleaning the house, doing laundry, going to market, cooking, preparing the table, washing the dishes.
EASIER: Both parents share childcare, decisions, and housework.

CHURCH AND MASS MEDIA
⚙ Church: gender roles in Christianity vary considerably today, as they have during the last two millennia, especially in marriage and ministry.
⚙ Mass media examples (such as the Internet):
⚙ Media's sexualization of young female celebrities.
⚙ Marketing of unrealistic beauty standards through fashion magazines.
⚙ Video games that promote violence and negative stereotypes.
⚙ Music lyrics and music videos that promote negative stereotypes and violence.
⚙ Sex stereotypes in kids' TV programs.

SCHOOL
⚙ Three areas: Instructional Language, Classroom Management, Instructional Materials.
★ Instructional language: the words used in teaching. Avoid generic words like he, his, and man for both girls and boys, and avoid stereotyped language.
⚙ freshman → first year student
⚙ mankind → humanity
⚙ fisherman → fisher folk
⚙ chairman → chairperson
★ Classroom management: assigning groups (boys and girls together, not boys against girls), the tasks teachers give, and discipline.
★ Instructional materials: textbooks, direct teaching materials, and peripheral teaching materials.
Added: peripheral teaching materials are things around the classroom like posters, charts, and wall displays.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Saying sex and gender are the same. Sex is biological; gender is social.
⚙ Saying GAD is only about women. It is about the roles society gives both women and men.
⚙ Mixing up equality and equity. Equity can mean different treatment based on needs.
⚙ Mixing up manipulation and canalization. Manipulation is handling infants; canalization is directing attention to toys and objects.
⚙ Saying gender is fixed at birth. Sex is fixed at birth; gender varies over time and space.
