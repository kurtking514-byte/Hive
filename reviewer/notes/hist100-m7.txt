Module 7 Taxation in the Philippines
Tax concepts, history, legal bases, the tax reform program, the TRAIN Law, and types of taxes (summarized)

WHAT TAXATION IS
★ Taxation: the power and process by which government imposes compulsory charges to raise public revenue. Taxes are not voluntary donations.
★ The power to tax is an inherent power of sovereignty, but it is not unlimited; it must follow law and the Constitution.
★ Lifeblood idea: without revenue, government cannot run. But this does not justify unlimited taxation.

THE THREE INHERENT POWERS OF THE STATE
⚙ Taxation: raises revenue for public purposes (income tax, VAT, excise). Limit: legal, fair, public purpose.
⚙ Police power: regulates behavior for public welfare (health rules, traffic laws, zoning). Limit: must not be arbitrary.
⚙ Eminent domain: takes private property for public use (land for roads). Limit: public use and just compensation.
EASIER: Taxation = how government gets money. Police power = how it regulates behavior. Eminent domain = when it can take property.

WHY GOVERNMENTS TAX
⚙ Fund public services and infrastructure (roads, schools, health, courts, disaster response).
⚙ Redistribute resources through social programs.
⚙ Influence behavior (excise, health, and environmental taxes).
⚙ Support economic stability and development.
⚙ Strengthen government and create a basis for accountability.

TAXES VS OTHER CHARGES
⚙ Tax: compulsory, for public revenue, not tied to one direct service.
⚙ License or regulatory fee: for regulation or permission (business permit).
⚙ Toll: for using a particular facility (expressway toll).
⚙ Special assessment: for a property benefit from a public improvement (road or drainage).
⚙ Penalty: punishment for violating a rule (late filing surcharge).
⚙ Debt: from a contract or private relationship (a loan).
⚙ Characteristics of a tax: imposed by law, payable in money, compulsory and enforceable, and for a public purpose.

PRINCIPLES OF A GOOD TAX SYSTEM
⚙ Fiscal adequacy: raises enough revenue.
⚙ Theoretical justice: fair and based on ability to pay.
⚙ Administrative feasibility: practical to assess and collect.
⚙ Transparency: people understand why and how they pay.
⚙ Accountability: collection is tied to lawful spending.

FAIRNESS AND TAX TERMS
★ Horizontal equity: people in similar situations get similar tax treatment.
★ Vertical equity: people with greater ability to pay may pay more.
★ Progressive tax: the rate rises as ability to pay rises (graduated income tax brackets).
★ Proportional tax: the same rate for everyone (a flat rate).
★ Regressive tax: lower-income groups carry a heavier burden relative to income (consumption taxes can feel this way).
★ Tax incidence: who actually bears the cost. Statutory incidence = who legally pays; economic incidence = who ends up carrying it (for example, consumers paying VAT through prices).
★ Tax base: what is taxed. Tax rate: the percentage applied. Tax burden: the practical weight of taxes.
★ Tax avoidance: legal ways to reduce taxes. Tax evasion: illegal non-payment, like hiding income or falsifying records.
⚙ Compliance cycle: register, keep records, file returns, pay on time, respond to audits.

HISTORY OF PHILIPPINE TAXATION
⚙ Pre-colonial: datus received tribute, labor, or goods for protection and community needs.
⚙ Spanish: tribute and cedula (personal tax and a symbol of colonial authority), polo y servicios (forced labor of Filipino males), bandala (forced sales), monopolies, and customs duties.
⚙ Revolution and First Republic: a new government needed funds for soldiers, offices, and diplomacy.
⚙ American period: modern bureaucratic revenue administration; the Bureau of Internal Revenue traces its origins here.
⚙ Commonwealth, war, postwar: war disrupted taxes; after independence, taxes funded reconstruction.
Milestones:
⚙ 1977 NIRC: internal revenue laws codified.
⚙ 1988 VAT: VAT became a major consumption tax.
⚙ 1997 NIRC: the new Tax Code, base of many modern rules.
⚙ 2005 EVAT: Expanded VAT.
⚙ 2017 to present: TRAIN, CREATE, EOPT, valuation reform, and digital services VAT.
★ Taxation history is a history of power: who collects, who pays, who benefits, and who decides.

LEGAL BASES OF TAXATION
⚙ 1987 Constitution: grants and limits taxing power.
⚙ NIRC (National Internal Revenue Code): governs major national taxes.
⚙ Local Government Code: basis of local taxes, fees, and charges.
⚙ Customs laws: duties on imports.
⚙ Revenue regulations and rulings: implement tax laws.
★ Article VI, Section 28: taxation must be uniform and equitable, and Congress shall evolve a progressive system of taxation.
⚙ Revenue and tariff bills must originate in the House of Representatives.
⚙ No one may be imprisoned for debt or non-payment of a poll tax.
⚙ Constitutional limits: due process, equal protection and uniformity, progressive taxation, exemptions for charitable, religious, and educational property.
⚙ Inherent limits: public purpose, territoriality, international comity, and no tax on government instrumentalities unless the law says so.

TAX INSTITUTIONS
⚙ Congress: makes tax laws.
⚙ Department of Finance: oversees fiscal policy.
⚙ BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue): collects national internal revenue taxes.
⚙ BOC (Bureau of Customs): collects customs duties.
⚙ LGUs: collect local taxes.
⚙ Court of Tax Appeals: the specialized court for tax disputes.
⚙ Taxpayer remedies in order: assessment notice, administrative protest, decision, then judicial review at the Court of Tax Appeals.

THE COMPREHENSIVE TAX REFORM PROGRAM (CTRP)
★ CTRP: a multi-package reform to make taxes simpler, fairer, and more efficient while raising revenue.
⚙ TRAIN (RA 10963): lower personal income tax; adjusted consumption and excise taxes.
⚙ Tax amnesty (RA 11213): settle unpaid taxes, such as estate tax amnesty.
⚙ CREATE (RA 11534) and CREATE MORE (RA 12066): lowered corporate income tax and reworked incentives.
⚙ Sin taxes (RA 11346, RA 11467): higher taxes on tobacco, alcohol, heated tobacco, and vapes for health and revenue.
⚙ Real property valuation (RA 12001): standardize property valuation.
⚙ Ease of Paying Taxes (RA 11976): simpler, digital compliance.
⚙ Digital services VAT (RA 12023): VAT on digital services like streaming and online platforms.
⚙ Trade-offs: more revenue and simpler filing versus higher prices and heavier effects on low-income households.

THE TRAIN LAW
★ TRAIN: RA 10963, the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion Law, Package 1 of the CTRP. It amended the NIRC.
⚙ It lowered personal income tax for many workers and raised revenue through VAT and excise changes.
TRAIN income tax brackets (2023 onward, annual taxable income):
⚙ Not over P250,000: 0%.
⚙ Over P250,000 to P400,000: 15% of the excess over P250,000.
⚙ Over P400,000 to P800,000: P22,500 + 20% of the excess over P400,000.
⚙ Over P800,000 to P2,000,000: P102,500 + 25% of the excess over P800,000.
⚙ Over P2,000,000 to P8,000,000: P402,500 + 30% of the excess over P2,000,000.
⚙ Over P8,000,000: P2,202,500 + 35% of the excess over P8,000,000.
EXAMPLE: COMPUTING TAX UNDER TRAIN
⚙ P300,000: 15% of P50,000 = P7,500.
⚙ P600,000: P22,500 + 20% of P200,000 = P62,500.
⚙ P1,000,000: P102,500 + 25% of P200,000 = P152,500.
⚙ Excise taxes under TRAIN: petroleum, automobiles, minerals, tobacco, and sugar-sweetened beverages.
⚙ Estate tax: on property transferred after death. Donor's tax: on gifts during life.
⚙ Benefits: higher take-home pay, more revenue for infrastructure and social programs.
⚙ Criticisms: higher prices from indirect taxes; poor households feel consumption taxes even if they pay little income tax.
★ Mitigation: the Unconditional Cash Transfer program for poor households, plus fuel-related assistance.

TYPES OF TAXES
★ Direct tax: charged directly to the person who bears it (income tax, estate tax, donor's tax, real property tax).
★ Indirect tax: collected from one party but passed on through prices (VAT, excise tax, customs duties).
★ National taxes: imposed by national law, collected by BIR or BOC (income tax, VAT, excise, estate, donor's, documentary stamp tax, customs).
★ Local taxes: imposed by LGUs (real property tax, local business tax, community tax).
⚙ Income tax: on taxable income (compensation, business, professional, corporate).
⚙ VAT: tax on value added at each stage of production and distribution.
⚙ Percentage tax: for selected non-VAT taxpayers, often smaller businesses.
⚙ Excise tax: on selected goods like fuel, alcohol, tobacco, and sweetened drinks.
⚙ Withholding tax: a collection method; the payer withholds part of a payment and remits it to government. It can be creditable or final.
⚙ Capital gains tax: on gains from selected property or share sales.
⚙ Documentary stamp tax: on specified documents and transactions.
⚙ Classification map: by burden (direct or indirect), by level (national or local), by base, by purpose, and by administration.

MULTIDISCIPLINARY LENSES
⚙ Historical, Legal, Economic, Social or Equity, Public Administration, Communication.
★ Taxation is a shared system: law gives it authority, history explains its roots, economics shows its burden, administration makes it work, and citizenship demands fairness.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Know the 3 inherent powers and how they differ.
⚙ TRAIN = RA 10963, Package 1. CREATE = RA 11534. Ease of Paying Taxes = RA 11976. Digital VAT = RA 12023.
⚙ Direct (income, estate, donor's, real property) vs indirect (VAT, excise, customs).
⚙ Avoidance is legal; evasion is illegal.
⚙ Revenue bills start in the House of Representatives.
