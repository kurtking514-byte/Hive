2 Communication and Globalization
Understanding how communication connects people and places around the world

MODULE OVERVIEW

★ Learning objectives: define globalization; introduce kinds of globalization; determine positive and negative effects; define glocalization.
EASIER: Explain globalization, its effects, and how global ideas can be adapted for local communities.

DEFINITIONS

★ Globalization is integrating a country’s economies, societies, and cultures with the world through exchange of goods, services, information, and ideas among people and nations.
EASIER: Globalization connects countries through what they buy, sell, share, and how people communicate. Think of a web linking communities.
★ Globalization is driven by international trade and investment, aided by information technology.
EASIER: Trade and investment connect countries; technology helps people share information quickly.
★ Glocalization combines globalization and localization.
★ Glocalization adapts global products, services, ideas, or cultural elements to the preferences and needs of a local or regional audience.
EASIER: It is like changing a recipe to use familiar local ingredients while keeping its basic idea.

POSITIVE EFFECTS

★ Easy access to information is a major effect of globalization, according to Nikolay Daney (2017).
EASIER: People can find and share information from around the world more easily.
★ Businesses can expand to other markets, offering products and services to a larger audience.
EASIER: A business can find customers in other regions or countries.
★ The language gap gets smaller when companies communicate with customers in their native language.
★ Native-language communication helps companies connect with customers and create fairer, more personal experiences.
EASIER: Customers may understand a product better in a language they use comfortably.
★ Globalization gives companies access to a diverse pool of talents.
★ More companies operate remotely and hire globally.
EASIER: Companies can work with people in many places who bring different skills.
★ Globalization supports transfer of technology and knowledge sharing.
★ Opening borders can lead to greater innovation, breakthroughs, and growth.
EASIER: People can build on one another’s ideas when they share knowledge and technology.

NEGATIVE EFFECTS

★ Cultural homogenization happens when dominant foreign cultures influence local culture and make global culture more uniform.
EASIER: Local customs can become less visible when similar global trends spread widely.
★ Globalization can be associated with financial crises and inequality.
★ Although globalization has been shown to decrease poverty overall, this can be at the expense of local businesses and populations.
EASIER: Some benefit while local communities or businesses face costs or stronger competition.
★ The environmental toll can include decreased biodiversity and the spread of invasive species.
★ These effects can impact agriculture, wildlife, and ecosystems worldwide.
EASIER: Species spreading where they do not naturally belong can disrupt local habitats.

COMMON MISTAKES

★ Glocalization is more than spreading a global product; it requires adapting it to local or regional preferences and needs.
EASIER: If nothing changes for the local audience, it is not clearly glocalization.
★ Cultural homogenization makes cultures more uniform; glocalization adapts global elements to local audiences.
★ Three disadvantages: cultural homogenization, financial crises and inequality, environmental toll.
★ Five advantages: information access, market expansion, smaller language gap, diverse talent, technology transfer and knowledge sharing.

EXAM TIPS

★ Recall advantages as information, markets, language, talent, and technology/knowledge.
★ Recall disadvantages as cultural uniformity, financial crises/inequality, and environmental harm.
★ To explain glocalization, name the global element, local audience, and adaptation.
EASIER: Ask: What is global? Who is the local audience? What changed for that audience?
