3.3 Content and Contextual Analysis of Primary Sources
A concise guide to evidence, perspective, and historical meaning

MODULE PURPOSE
★ Analyze primary sources by examining context, content, perspective, value, limitations, and contribution to Philippine history.
★ O-C-P-V-L means Origin, Content, Purpose, Value, Limitation.
⚙ Origin: who made it, when and where, and how connected to the event.
⚙ Content: words, images, symbols, claims, actions, and details present.
⚙ Purpose: why it was created and for whom.
⚙ Value: what historians can learn from it.
⚙ Limitation: what it cannot answer, whose perspective is missing, and how creator's position shapes it.
EASIER: Use O-C-P-V-L as a checklist: who, what, why, what useful, and what missing?

SOURCE TYPES AND CONTEXT
★ A primary source was created during the period studied or by someone closely connected to the event; examples include documents, speeches, photographs, paintings, cartoons, objects, maps, films, and interviews.
★ A secondary source later interprets, explains, compares, or synthesizes past evidence.
★ Classification depends on the research question: a modern documentary is secondary about earlier events but primary evidence of modern memory.
★ Eyewitness and primary do not mean neutral, accurate, complete, or representative.
⚙ Content analysis examines words, images, claims, symbols, tone, and direct or implied meaning.
⚙ Contextual analysis examines creator background, period, audience, institutions, conflicts, and power relations.
★ Historical perspective reconstructs past conditions and choices; presentism judges the past only through current assumptions.
★ Point of view is the creator's position; bias is a tendency to select, emphasize, label, or omit perspectives. Bias does not make a source useless.

ARGUMENT AND EVIDENCE
★ Main argument is the central message the creator wants the audience to accept.
⚙ Find repeated claims, reasons, examples, emotional or legal language, tone, and what the audience is meant to believe, feel, or do.
⚙ Separate direct evidence from opinion or interpretation.
★ Historical importance comes from helping answer a meaningful question, not simply from age or fame.
★ Corroboration compares independent evidence to find agreement, conflict, and gaps.
⚙ Compare creators, dates, places, source types, and perspectives before making broad claims.
★ One source cannot stand for an entire society, region, class, or period.
EASIER: Corroboration is checking more than one witness before making a big conclusion.

SELECTED PHILIPPINE SOURCES
★ Juan de Plasencia's late-sixteenth-century Customs of the Tagalogs is a chronicle and colonial ethnographic account by a Spanish Franciscan missionary.
★ Plasencia describes barangays, datus, social classes, marriage, dowry, inheritance, laws, justice, debt, and religious beliefs, showing organized Tagalog political, legal, social, and religious systems.
★ His missionary-colonial lens, Christian categories, Spanish-language assumptions, Tagalog focus, and Spanish contact limit generalization.
⚙ Use specific evidence, make cautious inferences, and corroborate Plasencia with early accounts, archaeology, material culture, indigenous terms, later ethnography, and evidence from other regions.
★ Antonio Pigafetta's 1519-1522 First Voyage Around the World is an eyewitness travel account from Magellan's expedition.
★ Pigafetta records routes, islands, names, food, rituals, rulers, trade, ceremonies, and conflict during early European contact with Visayan communities.
★ His Cebu account records ships, banners, artillery, interpreters, negotiation, tribute, and trade; local protocols and trade existed before European arrival.
★ Translation barriers, possible misunderstanding, European labels, and expedition perspective limit Pigafetta's account.
⚙ Separate observation from cultural interpretation, compare local evidence, notice power, and avoid projecting later nationalism onto 1521.
★ Emilio Jacinto's 1890s Kartilya ng Katipunan presents membership as moral commitment: discipline, honor, reason, integrity, equality, respect, service, patriotism, and duty.
★ The Kartilya connects honorable individuals, respectful communities, and collective freedom.
★ Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista prepared the Act of the Declaration of Philippine Independence, proclaimed June 12, 1898.
★ The Declaration argues colonial abuses justified separation from Spain and claims sovereignty; it addresses local and foreign audiences during the Spanish-American War.
★ Its praise of American protection amid U.S. nonrecognition, revolutionary leadership perspective, and unequal representation of social and regional views require context and comparison.
★ Juan Luna's Spoliarium (1884) depicts defeated gladiators and won a gold medal at the Madrid Exposition.
★ Its bodies, workers, grieving figures, darkness, and lighting support interpretations of suffering, violence, spectacle, defeat, and grief; its colonial-nationalist meaning is later interpretation, not a photograph of a Philippine event.
★ Fernando Amorsolo painted The Making of the Philippine Flag in 1955, retrospectively depicting the flag sewn in Hong Kong in 1898 by Marcela Agoncillo, Lorenza Agoncillo, and Delfina Herbosa de Natividad.
★ Amorsolo's painting is evidence of later national memory, not eyewitness visual evidence of 1898.
⚙ For paintings, describe visible features first, then interpret composition, color, light, gesture, scale, and symbols in context.
★ Individual American-era cartoons from 1900-1941 are primary sources; the 1985 McCoy-Roces book is a secondary compilation.
⚙ Decode a cartoon by identifying characters and symbols, examining exaggeration, reading caption, finding irony or criticism, and connecting it to context.
★ “Wherever You Go, We Will Follow” (1917) uses Uncle Sam and Little Juan to criticize the contradiction between promoting freedom abroad and withholding independence.
★ A 1917 cartoon contrasts punishment of a poor child over a chicken with powerful “big thieves,” criticizing selective justice; exaggeration needs corroboration.
★ Cartoons reveal public opinion, colonial tensions, elite power, inequality, anxiety, and criticism absent from formal documents, but one cartoon cannot represent every view.
★ The Raiders of Sulu Sea (2008) is secondary evidence about earlier raiding and primary evidence of 2000s historical memory.
⚙ For film, distinguish historical evidence from narration, editing, reenactments, music, visuals, labels, maps, and expert selection.
★ Corazon C. Aquino addressed the U.S. Congress on September 18, 1986, after People Power and constitutional restoration; she argued democracy needed support while the Philippines rebuilt.
★ Ethos appeals to credibility, pathos to emotion, and logos to reasoning.
★ Filipino grievances against Governor-General Leonard Wood concern autonomy, executive power, and Filipino institutions.
★ The 1916 Jones Act expanded elected Filipino institutions and promised eventual independence once stable government was established.
★ Wood's stronger executive supervision and the Conley dispute helped trigger the 1923 Cabinet Crisis; a 1926 protest accused him of arbitrary acts and usurpations.
★ The Wood grievance preserves elite constitutional arguments but does not represent every group or Filipino view.

WRITING ANALYSIS
★ A source-analysis paragraph makes a claim supported by evidence and reasoning, not summary alone.
⚙ Use this order: claim, evidence, reasoning, context, qualification.
⚙ State what the source explains, provide a specific detail, show how it supports the claim, connect historical context, and name a perspective or limitation.
★ Avoid summary without a claim, calling biased sources useless, presentism, treating art as photograph, confusing compilation with original, and treating one source as whole history.
⚙ Build analysis from claim, evidence, context, perspective, and corroboration.
