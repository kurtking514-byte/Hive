Module 1 Lesson 2 Ethical and Effective Oral and Written Communication
Principles of effective speaking and writing, and the ethics of communication

ORAL AND WRITTEN COMMUNICATION
★ Communication can be oral or written.
EASIER: Oral communication is spoken (a talk, a report, a meeting). Written communication is put on paper or screen (an essay, an email, a letter).
The lecture presents two sets of principles side by side: five principles of effective oral communication and seven principles of effective written communication, called the 7 C’s.

PRINCIPLES OF EFFECTIVE ORAL COMMUNICATION
⚙ Oral principle 1: Be clear with your purpose. You should know by heart your objective in communicating.
EASIER: Before you speak, know exactly what you want your listeners to understand or do.
⚙ Oral principle 2: Be complete with the message you deliver. Make sure that your claims are supported by facts and essential information.
EASIER: Do not leave listeners with claims that have nothing behind them; back them up.
⚙ Oral principle 3: Be concise. You do not need to be verbose or wordy with your statements. Brevity in speech is a must.
★ Verbose means using more words than needed. Brevity means being short and direct.
EASIER: Say what you need in as few words as it takes, like a good text message that still makes sense.
⚙ Oral principle 4: Be natural with your delivery. Punctuate important words with appropriate gestures and movements.
★ A natural speaker exudes a certain degree of confidence even if he or she does not feel confident enough.
EASIER: “Punctuate” here means to stress or highlight: a hand movement on a key word works like bold text in speech.
⚙ Oral principle 5: Be specific and timely with your feedback. Inputs are most helpful when provided on time.
EASIER: Feedback given late, or feedback that is too general, does little good; say exactly what to fix, and say it early.

PRINCIPLES OF EFFECTIVE WRITTEN COMMUNICATION: THE 7 C’S
★ The 7 C’s of effective written communication are: be clear, be concise, be concrete, be correct, be coherent, be complete, and be courteous.
⚙ Written principle 1: Be clear. Be clear about your message. Always be guided by your purpose in communicating.
EASIER: A reader cannot ask you what you meant, so the message must make sense on its own.
⚙ Written principle 2: Be concise. Always stick to the point and do not beat around the bush. Be brief by focusing on your main point.
EASIER: “Beating around the bush” means talking around a topic without getting to it.
⚙ Written principle 3: Be concrete. Support your claims with enough facts.
★ Readers will easily know if you are bluffing or deceiving them when there is nothing to substantiate your claims.
EASIER: “Substantiate” means to prove or back up. Concrete writing gives facts, not just big statements.
⚙ Written principle 4: Be correct. Observe grammatical correctness in your writing.
★ Always have time to revise and edit your work. Even simple errors may easily distract your readers.
EASIER: A single misspelled word can pull the reader’s attention away from your point.
⚙ Written principle 5: Be coherent. Convey logical messages.
★ In coherent writing, the ideas are connected with each other and related to the topic.
★ Observe sound structure that will present a smooth flow of ideas.
★ Use transitional or cohesive devices so that the ideas will cohere (stick together) with one another.
Added example: Transitional or cohesive devices include words and phrases such as however, therefore, in addition, for example, and as a result.
EASIER: Coherent writing reads like a path with no missing steps; each idea leads to the next.
⚙ Written principle 6: Be complete. Include all necessary and relevant information so that the audience will not be left wanting of any information.
EASIER: After reading, the audience should not still be asking “but what about…?”
⚙ Written principle 7: Be courteous. The tone of your writing should be friendly.
★ A courteous writer avoids any overtone or insinuation to eliminate confusion and misinterpretation.
EASIER: An insinuation is a hidden, usually unpleasant hint. Courteous writing says things politely and plainly, without hidden digs.

ORAL VERSUS WRITTEN PRINCIPLES
★ Be clear, be concise, and be complete appear in both the oral list and the written list.
★ Be concrete, be correct, be coherent, and be courteous appear only in the written list (the 7 C’s).
★ Be natural with your delivery and be specific and timely with your feedback appear only in the oral list.
Be complete versus be concrete: being complete means including all necessary and relevant information; being concrete means backing claims with enough facts.
Be coherent versus be concise: coherent is about logical connection and flow of ideas; concise is about brevity and sticking to the main point.
Be correct versus be courteous: correct is about grammar and editing; courteous is about a friendly tone without insinuation.

COMMUNICATION ETHICS
★ Ethics is the discussion of the judgments we make about the appropriateness, the right or wrong, of our actions and policies, be those actions communicative, political, social, personal, or a mixture of areas (Johannesen, 1990).
EASIER: Ethics asks “Is this the right thing to do or say?” for any kind of action, including what we communicate.
★ Ethics helps us determine whether what we say or do is suitable or proper considering its effects on society.
The lecture asks how you would respond when a classmate encourages you to forge a signature on an important document; ethics guides that decision.
In school or in the workplace, there are situations when you must decide what to say, what not to say, or how to convey a message.
★ People may interpret messages differently because they vary in cultural background and personalities, so there is a need to consider our behaviour when we communicate with others.
★ One ethical issue in communication is plagiarism.
★ It is best to be aware of the rules on copyright and the legal issues on the use of information from various sources.
EASIER: Plagiarism is presenting someone else’s words or ideas as your own. Copyright is the legal right of an author over his or her work.

SUGGESTIONS ON ETHICAL COMMUNICATION (JOHANNESEN, 1990)
⚙ Ethical communicators are respectful of their audiences.
⚙ Ethical communicators consider the consequences of their communication.
⚙ Ethical communicators respect truth.
⚙ Ethical communicators use information properly.
⚙ Ethical communicators do not falsify information.
⚙ Ethical communicators respect the rights of others to information.
EASIER: Remember six ideas: respect the audience, think of consequences, respect truth, use information properly, never falsify, and let others have the information they have a right to.
★ Respecting others’ rights and their views may result in negotiations, effective communication, and a good working relationship.

ETHICAL DILEMMAS IN THE WORKPLACE (EUNSON, 2007)
★ Eunson (2007) listed ethical dilemmas that people are often faced with in the workplace.
EASIER: An ethical dilemma is a hard choice where the easy or tempting option may be the wrong one.
⚙ Should I embellish my resume or curriculum vitae with “half-truths” in order to get a job?
⚙ Should I “lie with statistics” when using graphs or charts in documents or presentations to make my arguments more persuasive?
⚙ Should I pass on, and perhaps embellish, rumours on the organizational grapevine?
⚙ Should I plagiarize materials to pad out documents I am writing?
⚙ Should I use dubious tactics (attack the person, divide and conquer) when negotiating with others?
⚙ Should I censor, filter, or block information getting to others?
⚙ Should I censor myself or remain silent when the group I am in is making important decisions?
⚙ Should I create “spin” or deceptive impressions when communicating with the public?
⚙ Should I use knowledge about human behaviour to more effectively manipulate others?
⚙ Should I deliberately distort the situation analysed in a report I am writing in order to create further work opportunities for myself?
⚙ Should I use knowledge about leadership styles to manipulate others?
⚙ Should I manipulate meetings so that a hidden agenda, rather than the written agenda, is followed?
EASIER: Embellish means to add details that make something look better than it is. The organizational grapevine is the informal gossip network in a workplace. Pad out means to fill a document with extra material to make it longer. Spin means presenting facts in a twisted way to create a favorable but misleading impression.
Added example: Starting the vertical axis of a sales graph at a high number so that a small increase looks huge is one way of “lying with statistics.”

MORALS AND ETHICAL COMMUNICATION
★ Communication ethics emphasizes that morals influence the behaviour of an individual, group, or organization, thereby affecting their communication.
★ One’s behaviour should be regulated by honesty, decency, truthfulness, sincerity, and moral uprightness.
EASIER: Good communication starts with good character; what you value shows in what you say.

GUIDES TO ACHIEVE ETHICAL COMMUNICATION
⚙ Guide 1: Establish an effective value system that will pave the way for the development of your integrity as a person.
★ One’s behaviour and decision-making style affect, in turn, the operations of an organization.
⚙ Guide 2: Provide complete and accurate information. Whether it is needed or not, the data you provide should always be contextualized and correct.
EASIER: “Contextualized” means given with enough background so it is not misunderstood.
⚙ Guide 3: Disclose vital information adequately and appropriately. Never conceal or hide information that is necessary, for purposes of transparency.
EASIER: Transparency means being open, so people can see what is really going on.

CODE OF ETHICS
★ Observing a code of ethics is essential because it determines the kind of behaviour that is proper and desirable over one that is displeasing and offensive.
★ A code of ethics sets the standards to be observed by a person or a company.
★ Following a code of ethics creates a good reputation or a positive image not only for an individual but also for the organization.
EASIER: A code of ethics is like a rulebook for good conduct; following it earns trust for both the person and the company.

EXAMPLE: APPLYING THE PRINCIPLES
⚙ A speaker who knows the objective of the talk, backs claims with facts, speaks briefly, uses gestures on key words, and gives feedback on time is following all five oral principles.
⚙ A writer who revises a report to fix grammar is being correct; adding transitional words so ideas flow is being coherent; removing a sarcastic remark is being courteous.
⚙ Refusing to forge a classmate’s signature follows the rule that ethical communicators do not falsify information.

COMMON MISTAKES
★ Do not list be natural or be specific and timely with feedback among the 7 C’s; they are oral principles only.
★ Do not confuse be concrete (support claims with enough facts) with be complete (include all necessary and relevant information).
★ Do not confuse be coherent (logical, connected ideas with smooth flow) with be concise (brief and to the point).
★ Do not confuse Johannesen (1990), who defined ethics and gave the suggestions on ethical communication, with Eunson (2007), who listed workplace ethical dilemmas.
★ Guide 3 says never conceal vital information; censoring or blocking information is listed as an ethical dilemma, not as a good practice.

EXAM TIPS
★ Memorize the 7 C’s: clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.
★ Memorize the five oral principles: clear purpose, complete message, concise, natural delivery, specific and timely feedback.
★ Memorize the six suggestions: respect audiences, consider consequences, respect truth, use information properly, do not falsify, respect the rights of others to information.
★ Memorize the three guides in order: value system, complete and accurate information, adequate and appropriate disclosure.

SELF-TEST
Q: Which three principles appear in both the oral list and the 7 C’s?
★ Be clear, be concise, and be complete.
Q: Who defined ethics as the discussion of judgments about the appropriateness, the right or wrong, of our actions and policies?
★ Johannesen (1990).
Q: What are the three guides to achieve ethical communication?
★ Establish an effective value system, provide complete and accurate information, and disclose vital information adequately and appropriately.
