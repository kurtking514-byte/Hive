Speech Writing and Delivery
The seven steps, the three-part structure, the four speech types, sample speeches, and how speeches are graded

THE FOUR TYPES OF SPEECH
⚙ Type 1: Informative speech.
⚙ Type 2: Persuasive speech.
⚙ Type 3: Argumentative speech.
⚙ Type 4: Humorous speech.
These speeches may discuss similar topics, but they have different purposes, structures, and approaches.
The handout's title says "Four Types of Communication", but what it lists are four types of speech.
EASIER: Informative teaches, persuasive convinces, argumentative proves, and humorous entertains.

STEPS IN WRITING A SPEECH MANUSCRIPT
★ Speech manuscript: the written, word-for-word text of a speech.
These seven steps apply to every type of speech:
⚙ Step 1: Identify the purpose. Determine what the speech aims to accomplish.
⚙ Step 2: Choose a topic. Select a relevant, interesting, and manageable topic.
⚙ Step 3: Identify the audience. Consider the audience's age, interests, and background.
⚙ Step 4: Gather information. Collect facts, examples, experiences, or supporting evidence.
⚙ Step 5: Organize ideas. Arrange the introduction, body, and conclusion.
⚙ Step 6: Write the manuscript. Develop the ideas into complete sentences and paragraphs.
⚙ Step 7: Revise and practice. Check clarity, grammar, organization, timing, and delivery.
EASIER: Why, what, who, find, arrange, write, polish.
★ Manageable topic: a topic small enough to cover well in the time given.

GENERAL STRUCTURE OF A SPEECH
Most speeches, whatever their purpose, have three major parts.
★ Part 1, Introduction: an attention-getter, a greeting and introduction of the topic, and the purpose or central idea of the speech.
⚙ An attention-getter can be a question, a quotation, a story, or an interesting statement.
★ Part 2, Body: main point 1, main point 2, main point 3, plus supporting details, explanations, examples, and evidence.
★ Part 3, Conclusion: summarize the important ideas, reinforce the central message, and end with a memorable statement.
★ Suggested manuscript length: about 250 to 350 words for a 2 to 3 minute speech, depending on the speaker's pace and pauses.
★ Attention-getter: the opening line that makes the audience want to listen.
★ Central idea: the one main message of the whole speech.

INFORMATIVE SPEECH
★ Purpose: to inform or explain.
★ Informative speech: a speech that educates the audience about a topic by presenting facts, explanations, definitions, and examples, without necessarily trying to convince them to take a position.
Steps in writing an informative speech:
⚙ Step 1: Introduce the topic and explain why it is important.
⚙ Step 2: Define or give background information about the topic.
⚙ Step 3: Present important facts and explanations.
⚙ Step 4: Give examples to help the audience understand.
⚙ Step 5: Summarize the information and end with a meaningful statement.
Suggested structure:
⚙ Introduction: introduce the topic and its significance.
⚙ Body 1: explain the meaning or background.
⚙ Body 2: discuss important characteristics or facts, with an in-text citation.
⚙ Body 3: give examples, applications, or effects, with an in-text citation.
⚙ Conclusion: summarize the information.
⚙ References: 2 to 3 references in APA 7th edition format.

PERSUASIVE SPEECH
★ Purpose: to convince or influence.
★ Persuasive speech: a speech that tries to influence the audience's beliefs, attitudes, opinions, or actions, using emotional appeals, credibility, and logical reasoning.
Steps in writing a persuasive speech:
⚙ Step 1: Introduce an issue that needs attention.
⚙ Step 2: Clearly state your position or desired action.
⚙ Step 3: Present reasons why the audience should agree.
⚙ Step 4: Support your reasons with examples, facts, or emotional appeals.
⚙ Step 5: Address the audience directly and encourage them to take action.
Suggested structure:
⚙ Introduction: present a problem or situation.
⚙ Body 1: state your position or claim.
⚙ Body 2: present reasons and supporting evidence, with an in-text citation.
⚙ Body 3: explain why the issue matters to the audience, with an in-text citation.
⚙ Conclusion: reinforce your position and give a call to action.
⚙ References: 2 to 3 references in APA 7th edition format.
★ Call to action: the closing request that tells the audience what to do, such as "choose a book and read a few pages today".

ARISTOTLE'S RHETORICAL APPEALS
Persuasive speeches often use Aristotle's three rhetorical appeals. The manuscript should label the appeals it uses.
★ Ethos: establishing credibility and trustworthiness.
★ Pathos: appealing to the audience's emotions.
★ Logos: using facts, evidence, and logical reasoning.
EASIER: Ethos is "trust me", pathos is "feel this", and logos is "here is the proof".
Difference: logos uses facts and research. Pathos uses feelings, such as imagining what you could gain.

ARGUMENTATIVE SPEECH
★ Purpose: to defend a position using evidence.
★ Argumentative speech: a speech that presents a clear claim about a debatable issue and defends it through logical reasoning and credible evidence.
★ Thesis statement: the one clear claim the speech will defend.
★ Refute: to prove an opposing argument wrong or weaker using logic and evidence.
Steps in writing an argumentative speech:
⚙ Step 1: Introduce a controversial or debatable issue.
⚙ Step 2: Present a clear thesis statement or argument.
⚙ Step 3: Give reasons and credible evidence supporting your claim.
⚙ Step 4: Acknowledge an opposing argument.
⚙ Step 5: Refute the opposing argument with logical explanations and evidence.
⚙ Step 6: Restate your position and conclude strongly.
Suggested structure:
⚙ Introduction: introduce the issue and state your thesis.
⚙ Body 1: present your first argument and evidence, with an in-text citation.
⚙ Body 2: present additional supporting arguments, with an in-text citation.
⚙ Body 3: present an opposing viewpoint and refute it, with an in-text citation.
⚙ Conclusion: restate your position and summarize your arguments.
⚙ References: 2 to 3 references in APA 7th edition format.
Difference: a persuasive speech may emphasize emotional influence and action. An argumentative speech puts more weight on examining opposing viewpoints and proving why its position is more reasonable.
EASIER: Persuasive wants you to act. Argumentative wants to win the debate with evidence, so it must face the other side.

HUMOROUS SPEECH
★ Purpose: to entertain and amuse.
★ Humorous speech: a speech that entertains through funny observations, amusing stories, relatable experiences, exaggeration, irony, or unexpected punchlines, while still having a central topic or message.
Steps in writing a humorous speech:
⚙ Step 1: Choose a relatable or entertaining topic.
⚙ Step 2: Begin with an amusing observation, question, or short anecdote.
⚙ Step 3: Develop the topic using funny experiences, situations, or observations.
⚙ Step 4: Add humor through exaggeration, irony, wordplay, or unexpected twists.
⚙ Step 5: End with a memorable punchline or witty statement.
Suggested structure:
⚙ Introduction: start with a joke, funny question, or amusing situation.
⚙ Body 1: introduce a relatable experience or situation.
⚙ Body 2: develop the humor through exaggeration or unexpected events.
⚙ Body 3: present the funniest or most surprising moment.
⚙ Conclusion: end with a punchline or humorous observation.
⚙ Reminder: a humorous speech does not need to be all jokes. Storytelling and relatable experiences can also make an audience laugh.
★ Anecdote: a short, interesting story about a real event.
★ Irony: saying or showing the opposite of what is expected, for a funny or striking effect.
★ Punchline: the final line of a joke that makes it funny.

COMPARING THE FOUR TYPES
⚙ Informative: purpose is to inform or explain; it ends by summarizing.
⚙ Persuasive: purpose is to convince or influence; it ends with a call to action.
⚙ Argumentative: purpose is to defend a position with evidence; it acknowledges and refutes the opposing view, then restates the position.
⚙ Humorous: purpose is to entertain and amuse; it ends with a punchline or witty statement.
⚙ Informative, persuasive, and argumentative speeches each list 2 to 3 references. The humorous structure has no references row, but its sample still cites sources.

EXAMPLE: INFORMATIVE SPEECH
Topic: The Importance of Digital Literacy. Title: "Beyond Clicking: Understanding Digital Literacy".
⚙ Attention-getter: questions about sharing posts without checking them or clicking suspicious links.
⚙ Definition: digital literacy is the ability to use digital technologies effectively, responsibly, and critically.
⚙ Three aspects: information literacy, digital communication, and digital safety.
⚙ Sources: UNESCO (2021) and Tinmaz et al. (2022).
★ Why it is informative: it defines digital literacy, explains three aspects, gives examples and facts, and educates rather than persuading.

EXAMPLE: PERSUASIVE SPEECH
Topic: Why Students Should Read More Books. Title: "Open a Book, Open Your World".
⚙ Attention-getter: "When was the last time you read a book simply because you wanted to?"
⚙ Reasons: reading builds vocabulary and comprehension, encourages critical thinking, and gives personal growth. These are labeled logos.
⚙ Sources: National Reading Panel (2000) and Institute of Education Sciences (2016).
⚙ Objection addressed: some say reading is boring or they have no time; the answer is to start with short stories, comics, novels, or articles.
⚙ Pathos line: imagine how much you could gain by scrolling less and reading more.
⚙ Call to action: choose a book and read a few pages today.
★ Why it is persuasive: a clear position, logical reasons, a possible objection addressed, an appeal to personal growth, and a call to action.

EXAMPLE: ARGUMENTATIVE SPEECH
Topic: Should Artificial Intelligence Be Allowed in Education? Title: "Artificial Intelligence: A Learning Tool, Not a Replacement for Thinking".
⚙ Thesis: AI should be allowed as an educational tool, if students use it responsibly and ethically.
⚙ Argument 1: AI makes learning more accessible (Miao and Holmes, 2023).
⚙ Argument 2: AI supports independent learning.
⚙ Opposing view: AI may encourage academic dishonesty; UNESCO (2023) warns that over-reliance may weaken intellectual skills.
⚙ Refutation: banning AI is not the answer; the problem is how people use it, so schools should set clear guidelines.
★ Why it is argumentative: a debatable issue, a clear thesis, supporting arguments, an opposing view acknowledged and refuted, and the position restated at the end.

EXAMPLE: HUMOROUS SPEECH
Topic: The Struggles of Being a College Student. Title: "Surviving College: A Daily Exercise in Confusion".
⚙ Opening: the "five stages of academic survival": happiness, confusion, panic, acceptance, and submission.
⚙ Humor: procrastination as "a college tradition", and "tomorrow has become a permanent resident in their schedule".
⚙ Sources: Oram and Rogers (2022) and Hensley and Munn (2020).
⚙ Message: college teaches patience, responsibility, teamwork, and how to survive academic challenges.
★ Why it is humorous: relatable student experiences, exaggeration and irony, unexpected situations, a humorous closing, and it entertains rather than proving or explaining a position.

HOW THE SPEECH IS GRADED
★ Final score: speech delivery 50 points plus speech manuscript 30 points, for a total of 80 points.
Speech delivery (50 points), 10 points each:
⚙ Voice projection and audibility: clear, audible, and well-modulated throughout.
⚙ Pronunciation and fluency: clear pronunciation and natural delivery with minimal hesitation.
⚙ Confidence and stage presence: confidence, composure, and effective audience engagement.
⚙ Expression and appropriate delivery: tone, facial expressions, gestures, and emphasis suited to the speech type.
⚙ Audience engagement and time management: keeps the audience's interest and meets the 2 to 3 minute requirement.
Speech manuscript (30 points):
⚙ Content and relevance to speech type (8): clearly shows the purpose and features of the assigned type.
⚙ Organization and structure (7): clear introduction, well-developed body, meaningful conclusion, and logical transitions.
⚙ Development of ideas and language use (10): ideas supported by relevant facts, examples, arguments, or humor, in clear and grammatical language.
⚙ Research integration and APA 7th edition referencing (5): 2 to 3 credible in-text citations with matching references in APA 7th edition.
⚙ Rating levels for each criterion: Excellent, Very Satisfactory, Satisfactory, and Needs Improvement.
★ Well-modulated voice: a voice that changes pitch and volume naturally instead of sounding flat.
★ In-text citation: a short source note inside the speech, such as "According to UNESCO (2021)".

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Mixing up persuasive and argumentative. Only argumentative must acknowledge and refute an opposing argument.
⚙ Ending an informative speech with a call to action. It ends by summarizing.
⚙ Writing a humorous speech that is only jokes with no central topic or message.
⚙ Writing far more than 350 words for a 2 to 3 minute speech.
⚙ Forgetting in-text citations and the 2 to 3 APA 7th edition references.
⚙ Mixing up the appeals: ethos is credibility, pathos is emotion, logos is logic and evidence.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Know the seven steps in order: purpose, topic, audience, gather information, organize, write, revise and practice.
⚙ Know each type's purpose and how it ends.
⚙ Know ethos, pathos, and logos.
⚙ Know the numbers: 250 to 350 words, 2 to 3 minutes, 2 to 3 references, 50 + 30 = 80 points.
