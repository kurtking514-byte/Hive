Module 3 Business Communication
Introductory Lesson: Understanding Business Communication, Communicating for Professional Success

OBJECTIVES
Define business communication and explain its importance in professional settings.
Distinguish business communication from ordinary communication.
Identify the elements and purposes of effective business communication.
Explain the role of clarity, conciseness, correctness, courtesy, and professionalism in workplace communication.
Identify common barriers to effective business communication.
Apply appropriate communication strategies to basic workplace situations.

WHAT IS BUSINESS COMMUNICATION
★ Business communication: the purposeful exchange of information, ideas, instructions, opinions, and feedback within and outside an organization to achieve professional or organizational goals.
★ Simple definition: business communication is communication with a purpose in a professional or organizational context.
★ The goal is not simply to send a message, but to ensure that the message is understood and leads to an appropriate response or action.
EASIER: Business communication is talking or writing at work on purpose, so that the other person understands and does the right thing.
It includes communication among:
⚙ Employees and supervisors
⚙ Managers and employees
⚙ Coworkers and teams
⚙ Businesses and customers
⚙ Organizations and stakeholders
⚙ Employers and applicants
⚙ Companies and the public
EASIER: "Within" an organization means inside the company (coworkers, managers). "Outside" means people beyond it (customers, applicants, the public).
It may take place through emails, meetings, reports, presentations, telephone calls, interviews, letters, memos, chat platforms, social media, and face-to-face conversations.

WHY BUSINESS COMMUNICATION IS IMPORTANT
Effective business communication helps organizations do seven things:
⚙ Share information: employees need accurate information to perform their responsibilities.
⚙ Give instructions: managers communicate procedures, expectations, deadlines, and responsibilities.
⚙ Make decisions: organizations depend on information exchanged among people and departments.
⚙ Build professional relationships: respectful communication develops trust among colleagues, customers, and business partners.
⚙ Solve problems: many workplace problems require discussion, clarification, negotiation, and feedback.
⚙ Persuade and influence: businesses communicate to persuade customers, employees, investors, and other stakeholders.
⚙ Represent the organization: a poorly written email or an inappropriate response can affect how people perceive an organization.
EASIER: Every email you send at work is also a small advertisement for your company. A rude or sloppy one makes the whole company look bad.

BUSINESS COMMUNICATION VS EVERYDAY COMMUNICATION
⚙ Purpose: everyday communication may be spontaneous; business communication usually has a specific purpose.
⚙ Style: everyday communication is often informal; business communication usually follows professional conventions.
⚙ Topics: in everyday communication personal topics are common; in business communication work-related topics are emphasized.
⚙ Rules: in everyday communication rules may be flexible; in business communication accuracy and appropriateness are important.
⚙ Audience: in everyday communication the audience may be familiar; in business communication the audience may include clients, managers, or strangers.
⚙ Consequences: in everyday communication consequences are often limited; business communication may have organizational consequences.
⚙ Language: in everyday communication informal language may be acceptable; in business communication language should be appropriate to the context.
EASIER: "Professional conventions" are the accepted habits of the workplace, like greeting properly, stating the request clearly, and saying thank you.

EXAMPLE: CASUAL VS BUSINESS MESSAGE
Casual communication: "Hey, can you send me that thing when you get a chance?"
Business communication: "Could you please send the sales report by 3:00 p.m. today? Thank you."
★ The second message is better because it is more specific, polite, and actionable.
EASIER: "Actionable" means the receiver knows exactly what to do and by when. "That thing" and "when you get a chance" leave the receiver guessing.

THE COMMUNICATION PROCESS
★ The process flows: Sender → Message → Channel → Receiver → Feedback, with Context and possible Noise affecting the entire process.
⚙ Element 1: Sender, the person or organization initiating (starting) the communication.
⚙ Element 2: Message, the information, idea, instruction, request, or emotion being communicated.
⚙ Element 3: Channel, the medium through which the message is transmitted.
⚙ Element 4: Receiver, the person or group for whom the message is intended.
⚙ Element 5: Feedback, the receiver's response to the message.
⚙ Element 6: Context, the situation in which communication occurs.
⚙ Element 7: Noise, anything that interferes with understanding.
Examples of channels: email, telephone, meeting, memo, letter, video conference, chat, and presentation.
★ Six kinds of noise: physical, technological, linguistic, cultural, psychological, and organizational.
EASIER: Think of mailing a package. The sender packs it, the message is what is inside, the channel is the courier, the receiver opens it, and feedback is the reply "I got it". Noise is anything that damages the package on the way.
Difference: the message is WHAT is sent; the channel is HOW it is sent (email, phone, meeting).
Difference: feedback is the receiver's response; noise is anything that blocks understanding; context is the situation surrounding the whole exchange.
Added example: a loud construction site is physical noise, a frozen video call is technological noise, unfamiliar jargon is linguistic noise, and a stressed or angry mind is psychological noise.

THE 7 CS OF EFFECTIVE BUSINESS COMMUNICATION
The 7 Cs are a useful framework for checking a workplace message. Each has a meaning and a sample practice.
★ Clear: easy to understand. Practice: state exactly what you need.
★ Concise: brief but complete. Practice: remove unnecessary words.
★ Concrete: specific and definite. Practice: give dates, numbers, and details.
★ Correct: accurate and appropriate. Practice: check grammar and facts.
★ Coherent: logical and organized. Practice: present ideas in a meaningful sequence.
★ Complete: contains the necessary information. Practice: answer who, what, when, where, and how when needed.
★ Courteous: respectful and professional. Practice: consider the receiver's feelings and position.
EASIER: Memory trick for the seven: "Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous."
Difference: concise means brief (no extra words); complete means nothing needed is missing. A good message is both short and whole.
Difference: clear means easy to understand; concrete means specific, with exact dates, numbers, and details.
Difference: coherent is about order and logic; correct is about accurate grammar and facts.

EXAMPLE: BEFORE AND AFTER THE 7 CS
Before: "We need the report ASAP because management needs it and there have been some problems with the numbers."
Improved: "Please submit the revised sales report by 3:00 p.m. today. Management will use the report for tomorrow's planning meeting. Please verify the figures before submission."
★ The revised version is clear, concrete, complete, and professional.
EASIER: "ASAP" is vague; "by 3:00 p.m. today" is concrete. "Some problems with the numbers" becomes a clear action: "verify the figures".

MAJOR PURPOSES OF BUSINESS COMMUNICATION
Business communication generally serves four major purposes:
★ To inform: providing facts, updates, policies, or instructions. Example: "The orientation program will begin at 9:00 a.m. on Monday."
★ To request: asking another person to provide information or perform an action. Example: "Please send the updated inventory list before noon."
★ To persuade: attempting to influence an individual's or organization's decision. Example: "Our proposed training program can reduce customer-service response time."
★ To build relationships: creating and maintaining positive professional relationships. Example: "Thank you for your continued partnership with our organization."
Difference: to inform only gives facts; to request asks someone to give something or do something.
Difference: to persuade tries to change a decision; to build relationships keeps goodwill, like a thank-you message.

FORMS OF BUSINESS COMMUNICATION
★ Written communication: business letters, emails, memoranda, reports, proposals, minutes of meetings, notices, résumés and cover letters, and business plans.
★ Strength of written communication: it provides a record of communication.
★ Oral communication: meetings, interviews, telephone conversations, presentations, briefings, and negotiations.
★ Strength of oral communication: it allows immediate interaction and clarification.
★ Nonverbal communication: facial expressions, eye contact, posture, gestures, tone of voice, physical distance, and appearance.
★ Important point: what we communicate is not determined only by the words we use. How we deliver a message can affect how it is interpreted.
★ Digital communication: email, messaging applications, video conferencing, collaborative platforms, social media, and online project-management systems.
⚙ Digital communication requires attention to professional tone, response time, privacy, permanence, and audience.
EASIER: Written is for when you need proof later. Oral is for when you need a quick back-and-forth. Nonverbal is the body and voice "saying" things without words. Digital is anything sent through apps and the internet.
Difference: tone of voice counts as nonverbal communication even though it is heard, because it is about how the words are said, not the words themselves.
EASIER: "Permanence" means digital messages can be saved, forwarded, and screenshotted, so they may last forever.
Added example: "memoranda" is the plural of memorandum (memo), a short written message sent inside an organization.

FORMAL AND INFORMAL BUSINESS COMMUNICATION
★ Formal communication: used when professionalism, documentation, or organizational protocol is important.
Formal example: "Dear Ms. Santos, Please find attached the revised project proposal for your review."
★ Informal communication: may be appropriate for routine communication among colleagues who have an established working relationship.
Informal example: "Hi Maria, Could you check the figures in the last section before we submit the report?"
★ Key principle: professional does not always mean extremely formal.
★ Effective communicators adjust their language according to the audience, purpose, relationship, channel, and situation.
EASIER: You can be friendly with a coworker you know well and still be professional. Being professional is about being appropriate, not stiff.

COMMON BARRIERS TO BUSINESS COMMUNICATION
Even when a message is sent successfully, communication can fail. The eight barriers are:
⚙ Ambiguous language: words with unclear meaning, as in "Send it soon." What does "soon" mean?
⚙ Information overload: too much information makes it difficult to identify the main message.
⚙ Poor listening: the receiver may hear the words without understanding the intended meaning.
⚙ Language differences: different levels of language proficiency can affect understanding.
⚙ Cultural differences: people may interpret politeness, directness, silence, or disagreement differently.
⚙ Emotional barriers: stress, anger, anxiety, or defensiveness can interfere with communication.
⚙ Technological problems: poor internet connections, incorrect attachments, or communication-platform problems can disrupt communication.
⚙ Assumptions: a communicator may assume that the receiver already understands something.
EASIER: "Ambiguous" means it can be read in more than one way. "Proficiency" means how well someone knows a language.
Difference: language differences are about how well people know the language; cultural differences are about how people interpret behavior such as silence or directness.
Difference: poor listening is the receiver's problem (hearing but not understanding); assumptions are the sender's problem (thinking the receiver already knows).

AUDIENCE AWARENESS
★ One of the most important principles of business communication: write and speak for your audience, not merely for yourself.
Before communicating, ask these six questions:
⚙ Question 1: Who is my audience?
⚙ Question 2: What does the audience already know?
⚙ Question 3: What does the audience need to know?
⚙ Question 4: What do I want the audience to do?
⚙ Question 5: What tone is appropriate?
⚙ Question 6: What communication channel should I use?
The same information can be communicated differently to a company president, a coworker, a customer, a supplier, or a new employee.
★ The message may remain the same, but its language, tone, detail, and format may change.
EASIER: Telling your boss and telling a new employee about the same new policy: the boss may need a short summary, while the new employee needs more detail and simpler words.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
The five principles to remember:
⚙ Principle 1: Have a purpose. Know what you want your communication to accomplish.
⚙ Principle 2: Know your audience. Adapt your message to the people who will receive it.
⚙ Principle 3: Be clear. Do not make the receiver guess what you mean.
⚙ Principle 4: Be professional. Use appropriate language, tone, and behavior.
⚙ Principle 5: Communicate for understanding. Successful communication occurs when the intended meaning is understood and can lead to appropriate action.
★ Closing thought: in business, communication is not merely about speaking or writing well. It is about creating understanding that helps people work, decide, solve problems, and build professional relationships.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Thinking the goal is only to send a message. The goal is for it to be understood and lead to the right action.
⚙ Mixing up message and channel. The report is the message; email is the channel.
⚙ Mixing up concise and complete. Concise means brief; complete means all needed information is there.
⚙ Thinking professional always means extremely formal. Informal can be fine with colleagues you work with often.
⚙ Forgetting that tone of voice and appearance are nonverbal communication.
⚙ Mixing up the strengths: written gives a record; oral gives immediate interaction and clarification.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Memorize the order Sender, Message, Channel, Receiver, Feedback, plus Context and Noise.
⚙ Memorize the 7 Cs with their meanings: clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.
⚙ Know the four purposes: inform, request, persuade, build relationships. Be ready to classify a sample sentence.
⚙ Know the eight barriers and be ready to name the barrier in a short situation.
⚙ Know the six kinds of noise: physical, technological, linguistic, cultural, psychological, organizational.
