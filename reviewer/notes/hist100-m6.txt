Module 6 The Philippine Constitution
From Biak-na-Bato to the 1987 Constitution, State Policies, the Bill of Rights, and problem solving (summarized)

WHAT A CONSTITUTION IS
★ Constitution: the highest legal and political framework of a state. It creates institutions, divides power, limits power, protects rights, and states national direction.
★ Formula: Constitution = Power Map + Rights Shield + National Direction.
⚙ Function 1, Organizes Power: sets up the executive, legislature, judiciary, local governments, and commissions.
⚙ Function 2, Limits Power: rights, checks and balances, due process, elections, accountability.
⚙ Function 3, Guides Public Life: national values like democracy, social justice, human rights, education, and ecology.
Difference: the Constitution is the highest law and harder to change. Ordinary laws are made under it, are easier to amend, and can be struck down if unconstitutional.
⚙ Historical analysis formula: Root Cause (what crisis led to it), Design Choice (what it created), Effect or Limit (what changed and what stayed unresolved).

TIMELINE OF PHILIPPINE CONSTITUTIONS
⚙ 1897 Biak-na-Bato: during the Revolution against Spain.
⚙ 1899 Malolos: the First Philippine Republic.
⚙ 1902 to 1934 U.S. Acts: American colonial rule.
⚙ 1935 Constitution: the Commonwealth and later the Republic.
⚙ 1943 Constitution: Japanese occupation.
⚙ 1973 Constitution: the Martial Law era.
⚙ 1986 Freedom Constitution: the post-EDSA transition.
⚙ 1987 Constitution: democratic restoration (the present Constitution).

1897 BIAK-NA-BATO CONSTITUTION
★ A revolutionary constitution during the fight against Spain. It created a Supreme Council with executive authority.
★ Drafters: Isabelo Artacho and Felix Ferrer. Influenced by the Cuban Constitution of Jimaguayu.
⚙ It was short-lived because of the Pact of Biak-na-Bato.
⚙ Meaning: Filipino revolutionaries were not only fighting; they were organizing a government.

1899 MALOLOS CONSTITUTION
★ The constitution of the First Philippine Republic, made by the Malolos Congress.
★ Felipe Calderon wrote the main draft; Apolinario Mabini contributed ideas on revolutionary authority.
⚙ It emphasized popular sovereignty, representative institutions, and civil liberties.
⚙ It is remembered as one of Asia's early republican constitutions.
⚙ Limitation: the Philippine-American War cut it short.

U.S. ACTS (1902 TO 1934)
These were not Philippine-made constitutions, since sovereignty stayed with the United States.
⚙ Philippine Bill of 1902: civil government, some rights, and later an elected Philippine Assembly.
⚙ Jones Law of 1916: more self-government and a bicameral legislature (Senate and House).
⚙ Tydings-McDuffie Act of 1934: allowed a Philippine constitution and a 10-year Commonwealth transition. Independence was scheduled, not immediate.

1935 CONSTITUTION
★ Written under the Tydings-McDuffie Act. It governed the Commonwealth and the postwar Republic until 1973.
⚙ Based on the American model: presidential system, separation of powers, Bill of Rights.
⚙ Original design: a unicameral (one-house) National Assembly.
⚙ 1940 amendments: restored a bicameral Congress (Senate and House) and adjusted presidential term rules.
★ Women's suffrage: in 1937, Filipino women gained the right to vote through a plebiscite.

1943 CONSTITUTION
★ Drafted during the Japanese occupation; created the Second Philippine Republic under President Jose P. Laurel (with KALIBAPI).
★ Legality: whether a constitution formally exists. Legitimacy: whether authority is truly accepted as rightful.
⚙ The 1943 Constitution was legal in form but its legitimacy was questioned because Japan held real power.

1973 CONSTITUTION
★ Replaced the 1935 Constitution and introduced a parliamentary-style framework in form, during Martial Law under Marcos.
⚙ The 1971 Constitutional Convention began drafting before Martial Law.
⚙ It was ratified through Citizens' Assemblies, which was controversial because freedoms were restricted.
⚙ Repeated amendments led to presidential dominance, a gap between the text and reality.

1986 FREEDOM CONSTITUTION
★ A temporary constitution after the EDSA People Power Revolution, issued through Proclamation No. 3.
⚙ It allowed government reorganization and created the Constitutional Commission to draft a permanent constitution.

1987 CONSTITUTION
★ The present Constitution, drafted by the 1986 Constitutional Commission of 48 commissioners.
★ Ratified by plebiscite in February 1987.
⚙ Structure: a Preamble and 18 Articles.
⚙ Government: presidential system, bicameral Congress, independent judiciary.
⚙ Anti-authoritarian safeguards: a stronger Bill of Rights, term limits, independent constitutional commissions, judicial review, and accountability (impeachment, Ombudsman, "public office is a public trust").
EASIER: The 1987 Constitution was built to stop another dictatorship.

STRUCTURE OF THE 1987 CONSTITUTION
⚙ Preamble: national ideals (justice, freedom, equality, peace, truth, democracy, common good).
⚙ Articles I to II: national territory; principles and state policies.
⚙ Articles III to V: Bill of Rights, citizenship, suffrage.
⚙ Articles VI to VIII: legislative, executive, and judicial departments.
⚙ Articles IX to XI: constitutional commissions, local government, accountability of public officers.
⚙ Articles XII to XVI: economy and patrimony, social justice, education, family, general provisions.
⚙ Articles XVII to XVIII: amendments and transitory provisions.

ARTICLE II: PRINCIPLES AND STATE POLICIES
⚙ Sovereignty resides in the people.
⚙ The Philippines is a democratic and republican state.
⚙ Civilian authority is supreme over the military.
⚙ Separation of Church and State.
⚙ State policies: social justice, human rights, labor, education, health, ecology, family, youth, women, Indigenous peoples, local autonomy, and the policy against political dynasties.
★ Democratic: the people are the source of authority. Republican: the state acts through representatives, not personal rule.

BRANCHES AND CHECKS AND BALANCES
⚙ Legislative: makes laws, controls the budget, conducts oversight.
⚙ Executive: implements laws and runs the government.
⚙ Judicial: interprets laws and checks grave abuse of discretion.
⚙ Checks: presidential veto, congressional oversight and budget power, judicial review, impeachment.

CONSTITUTIONAL COMMISSIONS
⚙ COMELEC (Commission on Elections): runs elections.
⚙ CSC (Civil Service Commission): merit and professionalism in government service.
⚙ COA (Commission on Audit): audits public funds.
★ Public office is a public trust: officials serve the people, not themselves or their families.

THE BILL OF RIGHTS (ARTICLE III)
★ The Bill of Rights is the citizen's shield: it limits government power and protects individual freedom.
⚙ Due process: government must use lawful grounds and fair procedures before taking life, liberty, or property.
⚙ Equal protection: people in similar situations must be treated alike.
⚙ Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures, and privacy of communication.
⚙ Freedom of speech, expression, press, assembly, and petition.
⚙ Freedom of religion.
⚙ Liberty of abode and the right to travel; right to information; freedom of association.
⚙ Rights of the accused: presumption of innocence, right to counsel, right against self-incrimination, fair and speedy trial, right to bail, and protection from cruel punishment, double jeopardy, and imprisonment for debt.
Difference: State Policies say what government SHOULD PROMOTE and often need laws to work. The Bill of Rights says what government MUST NOT VIOLATE and can be directly invoked.

CONSTITUTIONAL PROBLEM SOLVING
⚙ Step 1: Define the Problem.
⚙ Step 2: Identify the Root Cause.
⚙ Step 3: Choose a Constitutional Lens (a right, policy, or institution).
⚙ Step 4: Anticipate Future Scenarios.
⚙ Step 5: Recommend a Solution.
Worked examples from the lesson:
⚙ Disinformation and elections: lens is democracy, free expression, suffrage; fix with media literacy and fact-checking.
⚙ Corruption in public funds: lens is accountability and COA; fix with open contracting, audits, and citizen monitoring.
⚙ Climate and disaster risk: lens is ecology and local autonomy; fix with risk-based zoning and resilient infrastructure.
⚙ Education and digital inequality: lens is the right to quality education; fix with device access and teacher training.
⚙ Privacy and surveillance: lens is privacy and unreasonable searches; fix with clear privacy policies and oversight.
⚙ Political dynasties: lens is equal access to public service; fix with party development and an anti-dynasty law.
★ Solution quality test: Rights-Based, Rooted, Accountable, Inclusive, Future-Ready.

SELF-TEST
Q: Which constitution created the First Philippine Republic?
★ The 1899 Malolos Constitution.
Q: Which U.S. law set up the 10-year Commonwealth transition?
★ The Tydings-McDuffie Act of 1934.
Q: How many commissioners drafted the 1987 Constitution?
★ 48.
Q: Which article of the 1987 Constitution is the Bill of Rights?
★ Article III.
Q: Which commission audits the use of public funds?
★ The Commission on Audit (COA).

★ Legality is whether the structure formally exists; legitimacy is whether the authority is truly accepted as rightful.
