Lecture 4 Human Errors
From Norman, D.A. (1988). The Design of Everyday Things

1. Human vs computer
★ Humans are the result of millions of years of evolution
Humans: the guiding principle was survival, not precision
Humans: robust in the face of unexpected circumstances
Humans: have the ability to deceive
★ Computers are less than 100 years old
Computers are reliable, consistent, and based on mathematics
EASIER: People were built to survive surprises, computers were built to be exact.

2. Differences in computation
★ Human computation is slow, complex, highly parallel, subject to rapid change, error tolerant, and forgiving
★ Computer computation is fast, high speed, precise, and not fault tolerant
EASIER: People are sloppy but flexible, machines are exact but brittle.

3. The concept of error
★ Common computer view of error: the computer was given information it could not process
Blame is then shifted onto the human being
⚙ Achieving a goal should be a cooperative endeavor between human and machine
⚙ The task is not to assess blame but to complete the task
EASIER: Stop asking who is at fault and help get the job done.

4. Types of error
★ Slips result from automatic behavior
★ Mistakes stem from conscious deliberation
EASIER: A slip is autopilot going wrong, a mistake is thinking it through and getting it wrong.
Six types of slips: capture, description, data driven, association activation, loss of activation, and mode errors

5. Capture errors
★ Capture error: a frequently done activity captures the one intended
Example: mistakenly dialing telephone numbers with the same prefix
Example: 426-6001 is the Ateneo trunk line and 426-6071 is the DISCS direct line
EASIER: A habit grabs the steering wheel from the thing you meant to do.

6. Description errors
★ Description error: an action is performed on the wrong artifact
EASIER: Right action, wrong object.

7. Data-driven errors
★ Data-driven error: triggered by the arrival of sensory data
Example: typing what you are thinking, hearing, or seeing and not what is intended
Example: calling someone by the wrong name
Slide-note anecdote under this topic: a thief shouted a stick-up at a bank and the 20-year-old teller told him he was in the wrong line and to wait over there, and he waited until the police arrived
EASIER: What comes in through your senses leaks into what you do.

8. Freudian slips
★ Freudian slip: a verbal or memory mistake that usually reveals the subconscious

9. Loss-of-activation errors
★ Loss-of-activation error: forgetting to do something
EASIER: The intention faded before you carried it out.

10. Association activation errors
Association activation errors are listed as a slip type, but the slides give no definition or example for them

11. Mode errors
★ Mode error: an action sequence performed in the wrong mode
Example: typing in the password with CAPS LOCK on
Slide-note anecdote near this topic: a government secretary mistakenly sent a personal letter to all government offices
EASIER: Right keystrokes, wrong state of the system.

12. Mistakes
★ Mistake: choice of an inappropriate goal
Mistakes include a poor decision, misclassifying a situation, or failing to take all factors into account
Mental thought is not neat and orderly
The disorder leads to creativity, discovery, and great robustness of behavior

13. Failure to detect problems
★ The user's ability to detect errors is unreliable
★ Relevance bias: people seek confirmatory evidence when evaluating a hypothesis
★ Partial explanation: crude agreement between what the user expects and what he sees
★ Overlap of model and world: the user's mental model is partially consistent with the world
EASIER: Users look for proof they are right, accept a rough match, and rely on a model that is only partly true.
