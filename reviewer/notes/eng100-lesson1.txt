1 Communication Process, Principles, and Ethics
A clear guide to language, the communication process, and responsible communication

MODULE OBJECTIVES
★ The module aims to define language, dialect, and accent; explain the communication process; describe communication models; and identify ethics and principles for effective communication.
EASIER: Learn what language varieties are, how messages move between people, and how to communicate responsibly.
★ Class reminders: Be confident without being rude, respect everyone, follow the two-ears-one-mouth policy, attend class, submit activities on time, participate actively, expect oral presentations, and do not use gadgets.

LANGUAGE, DIALECT, AND ACCENT
★ Language is a system of communication made up of sounds, words, and grammar.
EASIER: A language gives people shared sounds and rules for expressing meaning, much like a shared code.
★ A dialect is a variation of a language associated with a particular region, social group, or community.
★ Dialects may differ from a standard or prestige language in pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammar.
EASIER: A dialect is a recognizable variety of a language used by a community; it is not simply an incorrect form.
★ An accent is a distinctive way of pronouncing a language, especially one associated with a particular nation, locality, or social class.
EASIER: An accent concerns how words sound. Two people may use the same words and grammar but pronounce them differently.
★ Major dialects that enrich the Filipino language include Tagalog, Cebuano, Ilokano, Ilonggo, Bicolano, Waray, Pangasinense, and Kapampangan.
★ The module states that there are over 7,000 different forms of language in the world.
★ Languages change through time.
★ All languages have grammatical categories.
★ New words enter dictionaries.
★ Language is arbitrary: words and symbols generally have meanings because communities agree on them, not because the sounds naturally resemble the things they represent.
EASIER: The word “tree” does not look or sound like a tree; English speakers have learned to connect that word with the object.
★ The module identifies pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis as the longest word in the English language.
EASIER: This is a very long technical word; remember the spelling as presented in the module.
★ The module describes communication as more of an art than a science.
EASIER: Communication involves judgment about people and situations, even though it can also be studied systematically.
★ Communication is a natural ability.
★ Great vocabulary is essential for effective communication.
★ More communication is always better.
★ Telling clearly and confidently is the secret of communication.
★ Written communication is the fastest way to get a response.
EASIER: These five statements appeared as a true-or-false activity. They are not all reliable rules: effectiveness depends on the situation, and a larger vocabulary or written message does not guarantee better or faster understanding.
⚙ Class group activity: Form groups of five and choose five languages or dialects. Groups must not repeat another group’s choices.
⚙ Give examples of five words or phrases for each chosen language or dialect, for at least 25 words or phrases total.
⚙ Give the English equivalent meaning for each word or phrase.
⚙ The activity is worth 30 points: 25 points for the words or phrases and 5 points for presentation and translation.
EXAMPLE: LANGUAGE AND DIALECT
⚙ If a group chooses five languages, it should provide at least five words or phrases from each one, translate all of them into English, and present the results.
⚙ Check that the five languages or dialects do not duplicate another group’s selections.

COMMUNICATION
★ John Adair defines communication as essentially the ability of one person to make contact with another and make himself or herself understood.
★ William Newman and Charles Summer define communication as an exchange of ideas, facts, opinions, or emotions between two or more persons.
★ Louis Allen describes communication as a bridge of meaning.
★ Newstrom and Keith Davis define communication as the transfer of information from one person to another and a way of reaching others by transmitting ideas, feelings, thoughts, facts, and values.
★ Theo Haimann defines communication as the process of passing information and understanding from one person to another.
EASIER: These definitions share the idea of connecting with others and sharing information or meaning so it can be understood.
★ Communication is dynamic, continuous, complex, irreversible, non-sequential, and unrepeatable.
EASIER: Communication changes as people and situations change; it keeps unfolding, can involve many factors, cannot be fully taken back, need not follow a straight sequence, and cannot be recreated in exactly the same way.
★ The module lists five goals of communication: to get and give information, to get action, to persuade, to change behavior, and to ensure understanding.
★ The module presents “What is communication and why do we communicate?” as a guiding question.
EASIER: People communicate to exchange information and meaning, influence action or behavior, persuade, and check understanding.
★ The module presents the statement “Communication is much more of an art than science.”
EASIER: Skilled communication requires judgment and adaptation, although theories and evidence can help explain it.

ELEMENTS OF COMMUNICATION
★ Context is the environment where an interaction happens.
★ Physical context is the place where communication happens, such as a school or restaurant.
★ Psychological context is what participants bring to an interaction, such as needs, desires, and values.
★ Social context concerns the relationship between speaker and listener, including the expectations involved.
★ Cultural context includes learned behaviors and rules that affect an interaction, such as body movements, facial expressions, and practices.
EASIER: Context is the situation around a conversation: where it happens, what people bring to it, how they relate, and which learned customs shape it.
★ The sender, or speaker, is the source of the message and encodes it through speaking or writing.
★ The sender initiates communication by sending a message of information.
★ Encoding is transforming abstract opinions and ideas into symbols such as words, pictures, signs, and marks.
★ The message is the idea, information, or topic the communicator sends to the receiver to get feedback.
★ The message is the key element of any communication process.
★ The channel is the means or way a message is sent; examples include in-person meetings, phone calls, text messages, and email.
★ Noise is a barrier that affects a message being sent, received, or understood.
★ The module lists six forms of noise: physical, physiological, semantic, psychological, technical, and cultural.
★ Physical noise is an external sound or disturbance that interferes with communication, such as traffic honking.
★ Physiological noise arises from a bodily condition that makes sending or receiving a message difficult.
★ Semantic noise occurs when words, signs, or sentence construction make a message confusing or ambiguous.
★ Psychological noise arises from a person’s thoughts, emotions, or mental state and distracts from communication.
★ Technical noise is interference or failure involving communication equipment or transmission.
★ Cultural noise arises when differences in cultural knowledge, practices, or language interfere with understanding.
EASIER: Noise is anything that gets in the way of intended meaning. It can come from surroundings, a person’s body or mind, unclear language, technology, or cultural differences.
★ Decoding is translating or understanding the message; decoding is the first stage in listening.
★ The receiver is the audience member who decodes the message to recognize its meaning.
★ Feedback is a reaction, response, or information provided by the receiver.
★ Feedback completes the process because it affirms the receiver’s interpretation of the message.
EASIER: The sender forms an idea, puts it into a message, sends it through a channel, and the receiver interprets it. The receiver’s response gives evidence of how the message was understood.
⚙ Communication process: sender → encoding → message through a channel → decoding → receiver → feedback.
⚙ Noise can affect the message as it is sent, received, or understood.
EXAMPLE: ASKING A CLASSMATE TO SHARE NOTES
⚙ The sender decides to ask for notes and encodes that idea in spoken words or a text message.
⚙ The message is the request to share notes, and the channel is speech or text messaging.
⚙ The receiver decodes the request and replies; that reply is feedback.
⚙ Background traffic, a poor phone connection, unfamiliar wording, or distraction can introduce noise.

MODELS OF COMMUNICATION
★ The linear communication model presents communication as a one-way, straight-line process: speakers speak, listeners listen, and there is no immediate feedback.
★ Linear communication is common in mass communication such as television, radio, newspapers, commercials, and print materials.
★ The module credits Shannon and Weaver with presenting the linear communication model in 1948 through A Mathematical Theory of Communication.
EASIER: A broadcast is a useful example of linear communication: one source sends a message to an audience without an immediate reply in the same channel.
★ The interactive communication model describes participants alternating positions as sender and receiver.
★ In the interactive model, participants create meaning by sending messages and receiving feedback within physical and psychological contexts.
★ The interactive model is a two-way process.
★ The module identifies the interactive communication model with Schramm (1955).
EASIER: In a conversation, one person speaks, the other responds, and then their roles may switch.
★ The transactional communication model shows that communication elements are interdependent.
★ In the transactional model, each person is both speaker and listener and can send and receive messages simultaneously.
★ Transactional communication is ongoing and continuously changing as participants and their environment change.
★ The module identifies the transactional communication model with Barnlund (1970).
EASIER: During a face-to-face conversation, people can speak, listen, react, and adjust at the same time; each person’s behavior can influence the other.
EXAMPLE: THREE MODELS
⚙ A television announcement with no immediate audience response illustrates the linear model.
⚙ A conversation in which two people take turns sending messages and responding illustrates the interactive model.
⚙ A live conversation in which both people send signals, listen, react, and adjust simultaneously illustrates the transactional model.
⚙ Distinguish the models by asking whether communication is one-way, alternating two-way, or simultaneous and interdependent.

PRINCIPLES OF COMMUNICATION: THE 7 CS
★ The module lists the 7 Cs of Communication as completeness, conciseness, consideration, concreteness, courtesy, clearness, and correctness.
★ Completeness means including the information the audience needs.
★ Conciseness means expressing the message without unnecessary words.
★ Consideration means taking the audience’s needs and perspective into account.
★ Concreteness means using specific, definite details rather than vague language.
★ Courtesy means communicating respectfully.
★ Clearness means making the meaning easy to understand.
★ Correctness means using accurate information and appropriate language.
EASIER: The 7 Cs are a checklist: include what is needed, keep it economical, consider the audience, be specific, be respectful, be clear, and be accurate.
EXAMPLE: APPLYING THE 7 CS
⚙ Before sending an announcement, check that it includes needed details, removes unnecessary wording, considers readers, gives specific information, uses a respectful tone, is easy to understand, and is accurate.

ETHICS OF COMMUNICATION
★ Ethics is an area of philosophy that determines or judges what is right or wrong.
★ Ethical communication helps people make decisions about how to structure messages and choose words appropriate to a situation.
EASIER: Ethics asks whether a message is responsible and fair as well as whether it can be understood.
★ Ethical communicators respect their audiences.
★ Respectful communication considers who the audience is, including social and economic background, experience, and current psychological and mental mindset.
★ The Golden Rule for respect is to treat others the way we want to be treated.
★ Ethical communicators weigh the consequences of their actions.
★ Every word sent can influence or affect the lives of an audience.
★ The Golden Rule for consequences is: think before you click and think before you speak.
★ Ethical communicators value truth; truth is a core value and a major currency in communication.
★ The Golden Rule for truth is to respect the truth.
★ Ethical communicators know how to use information properly, are adequately informed, and communicate accurately.
★ The module states that misinformation is one of the worst offenses by a communicator and goes against communication principles.
★ Ethical communicators do not falsify information.
★ Distorting facts and making things up are both forms of dishonesty.
★ The module calls the idea that a frequently repeated lie becomes truth the “Big Lie Philosophy.”
EASIER: Check information before sharing it, do not distort or invent facts, consider how words may affect people, and treat the audience with respect.
EXAMPLE: RESPONSIBLE MESSAGE
⚙ Before posting a claim, check whether the information is accurate, consider how the audience may be affected, choose appropriate words, and avoid changing or inventing facts.

COMMON MISTAKES
★ A dialect is a variety of a language; an accent specifically concerns pronunciation.
★ Do not assume that more communication, a large vocabulary, or written communication automatically produces effective or fast communication.
★ Do not confuse the channel with the message: the message is what is communicated, while the channel is how it is sent.
★ Do not confuse decoding with encoding: the sender encodes; the receiver decodes.
★ Do not treat noise as only loud sound; noise can also be physiological, semantic, psychological, technical, or cultural.
★ Do not confuse the linear, interactive, and transactional models: they describe one-way flow, alternating two-way exchange, and simultaneous interdependent exchange, respectively.
★ The module’s references are Josue M. (2023), Purposive Communication in Diverse Contexts; Cayaban N. (2022), Purposive Communication in the 21st Century; and Mariveles H. (2021), Purposive Communication, Second Edition.

EXAM TIPS
★ Memorize the communication scholars with their central ideas: Adair, contact and being understood; Newman and Summer, exchange; Allen, bridge of meaning; Newstrom and Davis, transfer of information and ideas; Haimann, passing information and understanding.
★ Memorize the process in order: sender, encoding, message and channel, decoding, receiver, feedback.
★ For noise questions, identify whether the cause is surroundings, bodily condition, unclear meaning, mental state, technology, or cultural difference.
★ For model questions, identify whether feedback is absent immediately, roles alternate, or participants communicate simultaneously.
★ For ethics questions, check respect, consequences, truth, accurate information, and honesty.
