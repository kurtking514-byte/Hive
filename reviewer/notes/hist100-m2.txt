Module 4 Filipino Cultural Heritage
Local and oral history, museums, NHCP sites and markers, living heritage, and responsible promotion (summarized)

THE BIG IDEA
Heritage is not only old buildings and objects. It also includes stories, memories, performances, rituals, knowledge, skills, and values passed from one generation to the next.
Heritage is evidence: it must be interpreted, not just admired.
⚙ Lesson principle: Evaluate, then Understand, then Respect, then Promote.
⚙ Evaluate: where did the claim come from, who made it, whose voice is missing?
⚙ Understand: connect local stories, museums, sites, and rituals to the bigger Philippine story.
⚙ Respect: permission, consent, context, cultural sensitivity, community ownership.
⚙ Promote: share and preserve heritage responsibly, not just as content.

TYPES OF HERITAGE
★ RA 10066: the National Cultural Heritage Act of 2009. It recognizes tangible and intangible cultural heritage.
★ Tangible heritage: things you can touch, like museums, shrines, houses, churches, artifacts, and landmarks.
★ Intangible heritage: living practices, like oral history, chants, festivals, rites, dances, crafts, and foodways.
★ Natural-cultural heritage: places where nature and culture are connected, like rice terraces, sacred mountains, and heritage landscapes.
EASIER: Tangible = touchable. Intangible = practiced and remembered.
★ Key idea: heritage matters not just because it is old, but because communities see it as part of their identity, memory, and belonging.

LOCAL AND ORAL HISTORY
★ Local history: how national events were experienced in a specific place, like a barangay, school, family, church, market, or town. It shows history does not only happen in the capital or in textbooks.
★ Oral history: memories and experiences told by people (elders, farmers, workers, residents) that may not be in written records.
⚙ What local and oral history add: Voice (missing experiences), Place (how events affected communities), Completeness (brings in ordinary people).
Memory can be selective and emotional, so oral testimony must be checked and compared, with ethical care.

THE SANA ALL LENS
A quick checklist before believing or sharing a heritage claim.
⚙ S, Source: where did it come from, who recorded it?
⚙ A, Authority: what makes it credible?
⚙ N, Narrative: what story or angle is being told?
⚙ A, Absent Voices: whose perspective is missing?
⚙ ALL, Afterlife: how is it used today (education, tourism, politics, identity, profit)?

DOING ORAL HISTORY RESPONSIBLY
★ Oral history is documented memory gathered through a deliberate and ethical process, not just informal storytelling or rumor.
⚙ Step 1: Prepare: research the topic and write respectful questions.
⚙ Step 2: Ask Consent: explain the purpose, recording, privacy, and how the story will be used.
⚙ Step 3: Listen Deeply: avoid leading questions; let the narrator speak their own way.
⚙ Step 4: Verify: compare with records, other interviews, maps, photos, and archives.
⚙ Step 5: Return Value: credit the narrators and share the output with the community.
★ Ethical principle: verification and respect are not opposites. You can honor a narrator and still check the story.

MUSEUMS
★ A museum is an interpretive space, not just a storage room. It chooses and arranges objects into stories.
⚙ Museum functions: Collect, Interpret, Educate.
★ Museum literacy: reading an exhibit as a source. Ask what is shown, what the label says, what time and people are represented, what is NOT shown, how it makes you feel, and how you should behave.
EASIER: A display can be informative and still be selective or incomplete.

SITES, SHRINES, AND LANDMARKS
★ Historic site: a place linked to an important event, movement, or development.
★ Shrine: a place of commemoration where memory, reverence, and public education meet.
★ Landmark: a structure or place with recognized historical, cultural, or architectural value.

THE NHCP
★ NHCP: National Historical Commission of the Philippines, the key agency for conserving and preserving the country's historical legacies.
★ RA 10086: the Philippine History Act of 2010, which strengthens the NHCP's powers.
⚙ What the NHCP does: Research and Document; Recognize and Mark; Conserve and Protect; Administer and Popularize.
NHCP recognition types (not interchangeable):
⚙ National Historical Landmark: tied to a major event, achievement, or turning point.
⚙ National Historical Shrine: a hallowed site tied to a national hero or very significant event.
⚙ National Historical Monument or Memorial: a structure honoring heroes, events, or national memory.
⚙ Historic Site: a place formally recognized for historical significance.
⚙ Heritage House: an ancestral house with historical, cultural, architectural, and artistic value.
⚙ Historical Marker: a public plaque explaining why a person, place, or event matters.

HOW A SITE GETS RECOGNIZED (2011 NHCP GUIDELINES)
Recognition is based on evidence, not on being old, famous, or popular.
⚙ Step 1: Significance: strong link to major events, persons, or firsts.
⚙ Step 2: Age and Authenticity: at least 50 years old and about 70% authentic.
⚙ Step 3: Research: field work, archives, and oral interviews if needed.
⚙ Step 4: Board Action: NHCP evaluates and may declare and/or mark the site.
⚙ Step 5: Registry and Care: the site enters the national registry; NHCP, owners, LGUs, and communities share care.
★ Level I: declaration plus historical marker (heritage zone, national cultural treasure, national historical shrine, monument, or landmark).
★ Level II: historical marker only. The site is significant but not declared a shrine, landmark, or monument.
★ A marker does not automatically mean the place is a national shrine or landmark. Always check the official status in the NHCP registry.

NATIONAL HISTORICAL SHRINES IN THE LESSON
⚙ Rizal Shrine, Fort Santiago (Intramuros, Manila): Rizal's imprisonment before his execution.
⚙ Rizal Shrine, Calamba (Laguna): Rizal's birthplace, childhood, and family.
⚙ Emilio Aguinaldo Shrine (Kawit, Cavite): the 1898 declaration of Philippine independence.
⚙ Pinaglabanan Memorial Shrine (San Juan City): the 1896 Battle of Pinaglabanan.
⚙ Quezon Memorial Shrine (Quezon City): President Manuel L. Quezon and the Commonwealth.
⚙ Mabini Shrine (Tanauan, Batangas): Apolinario Mabini and the First Republic.
A shrine is a place of reverence but can also be a place of inquiry: ask what was selected, left out, or simplified.

HISTORICAL MARKERS
⚙ Marker roles: Locate, Remember, Verify, Care.
⚙ Marker text should be factual and research-based, usually in Filipino.
⚙ Markers are protected government property; do not remove, alter, or destroy them.
A marker is a short summary, so read beyond it: what was left out?

WHO RECOGNIZES WHAT
⚙ NHCP: historical significance (sites, shrines, landmarks, markers).
⚙ National Museum: cultural properties and collections (archaeology, fine arts, natural history).
⚙ NCCA: arts and culture policy, programs, and grants.
⚙ UNESCO: international recognition (World Heritage and Intangible Cultural Heritage lists).
★ One place can have several recognitions from different bodies, so name the right authority.

LIVING AND INTANGIBLE HERITAGE
★ Cultural performances (dances, theater, festivals, chants, epics) are living history carried through body, voice, music, and costume.
★ Indigenous practices (weaving, rice rituals, chants, crafts) are living knowledge systems, not costumes or tourist props.
★ Respect rule: communities are not exhibits; they are rights-bearing peoples.
★ Religious rites and rituals (prayers, processions, offerings, pilgrimages) preserve identity and belonging. You do not need to share the belief, but you must interpret it respectfully: no mocking, secret recording, or sensationalizing.

APPRECIATION VS APPROPRIATION
⚙ Appreciation learns context; appropriation uses symbols without understanding.
⚙ Appreciation credits the community; appropriation claims or profits from it.
⚙ Appreciation asks consent for sacred practices; appropriation posts without permission.
⚙ Appreciation supports local makers; appropriation reduces heritage to a costume, trend, or joke.
★ Golden rule: protect meaning, not just the image.

THREATS TO HERITAGE
⚙ Development without care (demolition, displacement).
⚙ Climate and disasters (flood, fire, earthquake).
⚙ Forgetting (loss of elders, languages, skills).
⚙ Over-commercialization (souvenirs, staged authenticity).
⚙ Misinformation (viral but wrong history).
⚙ Conflict and neglect (no funds, abandonment).

PROMOTING AND MAPPING HERITAGE
⚙ Promotion strategies: Document, Verify, Design, Partner, Educate, Care.
★ Responsible promotion: make heritage shareable, but keep it truthful, contextualized, and respectful. It is not about going viral at any cost.
★ Cultural mapping: identifying, documenting, evaluating, and valuing a community's cultural assets.
⚙ Cultural mapping steps: Identify, Document, Evaluate, Plan.
⚙ Outputs: heritage inventory, annotated map, photo essay, interview archive, or mini-exhibit.

PHILIPPINE UNESCO WORLD HERITAGE PROPERTIES
⚙ Cultural: Baroque Churches of the Philippines; Historic City of Vigan; Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras.
⚙ Natural: Mount Hamiguitan Range Wildlife Sanctuary; Puerto-Princesa Subterranean River National Park; Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park.
★ Global recognition does not replace local stewardship.

INTANGIBLE HERITAGE CASES
⚙ Hudhud chants of the Ifugao: oral tradition tied to rice culture.
⚙ Darangen epic of the Maranao: an ancient sung epic carrying law, values, heroes, and history.
⚙ Punnuk tugging ritual (Ifugao): a post-harvest communal rite.
⚙ Buklog of the Subanen: a thanksgiving ritual needing urgent safeguarding.
★ Intangible heritage survives only when people keep practicing, teaching, and passing it on. Recordings alone cannot replace them.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Know SANA ALL, the 5 oral history steps, and the NHCP recognition types.
⚙ Marker is not the same as shrine or landmark. Level I = declaration plus marker; Level II = marker only.
⚙ 50 years old and about 70% authentic for NHCP recognition.
⚙ RA 10066 = National Cultural Heritage Act; RA 10086 = Philippine History Act (NHCP).
⚙ Know which shrine is where, and the 3 cultural and 3 natural UNESCO sites.
