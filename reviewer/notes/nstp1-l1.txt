Lesson 1 NSTP Laws and Policies
RA 9163, the three NSTP components, the nature of development, the elements of national security, and voters' education

THE YOUTH AND RA 8044
★ The Filipino youth are considered the most valuable resource of the country.
★ RA 8044, the "Youth in Nation-Building Act": enacted in 1995. It established the National Youth Commission and the National Comprehensive and Coordinated Program on Youth Development.
★ Youth under RA 8044: Filipinos aged 15 to 30.
⚙ The lesson gives the Philippines a projected population of 85.2 million, with the youth making up more than 40 percent.
The 85.2 million figure is an old projection; the current population is much higher. Remember the lesson's number for the exam.
EASIER: RA 8044 is the law that says who counts as youth (15 to 30) and created the National Youth Commission.

RA 9163, THE NSTP LAW
★ RA 9163: "An Act Establishing the National Service Training Program (NSTP) for Tertiary Level Students, Amending for the Purpose Republic Act No. 7077 and Presidential Decree No. 1706, and for Other Purposes".
★ RA 9163 was signed into law on January 23, 2002, amid calls from dissenting sectors to abolish or reform the old program.
⚙ It amended RA 7077 and PD 1706.
★ Tertiary level students: college students.
★ Constitutional basis: the duty of the state to serve and protect its citizens, from Article II (Declaration of Principles and State Policies) of the Constitution.
⚙ "The prime duty of the Government is to serve and protect the people. The Government may call upon the people to defend the State and, in the fulfillment thereof, all citizens may be required, under conditions provided by law, to render personal military or civil service."
The lesson cites this as Article II, Section 2, but in the 1987 Constitution this sentence is Article II, Section 4. Section 2 is about renouncing war.
⚙ This is the same principle that created and sustained the Reserve Officers' Training Corps (ROTC).
★ Primary objective of the NSTP law: to promote the role of the youth in nation-building.
⚙ It encourages the youth to become civic or military leaders and volunteers who can be called upon when the nation needs their services.
EASIER: The government's first job is to protect the people, and it can ask citizens to help. NSTP trains students so they are ready to help.

FROM ROTC TO NSTP
⚙ ROTC: specialized in military training.
⚙ E-ROTC (Expanded ROTC): gave students three options, but its implementation was limited.
⚙ NSTP: makes sure the three components, CWTS, LTS, and ROTC, get the same and equal implementation in schools.
⚙ The NSTP law also defined the different components and the duration of the program. The lesson's sentence is cut off at this point.
Difference: ROTC was only military. E-ROTC had three options but was not fully implemented. NSTP gives all three components equal implementation.

THE THREE NSTP COMPONENTS
★ Civic Welfare Training Service (CWTS): activities with social impact that contribute to the "health, education, environment, entrepreneurship, safety, recreation and morals of the citizenry".
⚙ CWTS stresses youth involvement in broad programs or activities that benefit the people.
★ Literacy Training Service (LTS): trains students to become teachers of literacy and numeracy skills to school children, out-of-school youth, and other segments of society that need their service.
⚙ LTS specializes in the education of the people, strengthening the education sector to empower people through education.
★ Military Training Service (MTS), also known as the Reserve Officers' Training Corps (ROTC): has the primary objective of preparing the youth for national defense.
⚙ ROTC kept the existence and nature it had under RA 7077, but under the NSTP law it became just one component of the program.
★ Literacy: the ability to read and write. Numeracy: the ability to work with numbers.
EASIER: CWTS helps the community, LTS teaches people to read, write, and count, and ROTC trains for defense.
Difference: CWTS covers broad community welfare. LTS is only about teaching literacy and numeracy.

NATURE OF DEVELOPMENT
★ Political development: the active participation and involvement of all individuals in all governmental activities and public affairs.
★ Economic development: the constant progress or social mobility of every individual; adequate economic resources, conservation, and proper consumption; and the maintenance of peace and order in society.
★ Socio-cultural development: the harmonious relationship of individuals from different social groups, cultures, and religions; and the preservation of the cultural virtues and values needed for moral development.
★ Social mobility: a person's ability to move up in life, such as getting a better job or income.
EASIER: Political is about taking part in government, economic is about livelihood and resources, and socio-cultural is about getting along and keeping good values.

THE ELEMENTS OF NATIONAL SECURITY
★ Cultural cohesiveness: people share the values and beliefs handed down by their forebears and have a strong attachment to the national community, despite religious, ethnic, and language differences.
⚙ It shows in common ideas, feelings, and perspectives among people of different languages, religions, lifestyles, or ethnic groups.
★ Moral consensus: people, inspired by patriotism and national pride, take an active part in pursuing the country's goals.
⚙ There is agreement on where the nation wants to go and how to get there, on what is right and wrong, and on a national vision.
★ Territorial integrity: the country's territory is intact and under the effective control of the government.
⚙ The territory is protected from illegal intrusions and destructive explorations, and no rebel or belligerent group controls any part of it where our laws cannot be enforced.
The lesson's wording reads "and rebels or belligerent group has control", which is a typo; territorial integrity means no such group has control.
★ Ecological balance: the environment can support sustainable development for the nation and the people who depend on it.
★ Socio-political stability: peace and harmony among different groups, with mutual cooperation and support between the government and the people.
⚙ Civil and human rights are respected, and just policies and rules are obeyed.
★ Economic solidarity: the economy is strong, can support national endeavors, and draws strength from people who have a stake in it through participation or ownership.
⚙ People control their own lives, livelihoods, and economic destinies, and economic democracy prevails.
★ External peace: the country has cordial relations with its neighbors and is free from any control, interference, or threat of aggression from them.
⚙ It is respected by other nations whatever its political, economic, or socio-cultural status, and can act without being hampered by any nation.
EASIER: The seven elements are shared culture, shared morals, a whole territory, a healthy environment, a stable society, a strong economy, and peace with other countries.
Difference: cultural cohesiveness is sharing values and beliefs. Moral consensus is agreeing on goals, right and wrong, and a national vision.
Difference: socio-political stability is peace inside the country. External peace is peace with other countries.
★ Belligerent group: an armed group fighting against the government.

VOTERS' EDUCATION
★ Voters' education: a program meant to start political development.
⚙ Political development is best seen in citizens exercising their right to vote.
⚙ The right to vote is mainly exercised during elections.
★ Election: the means by which the people choose their officials for definite and fixed periods.
★ Regular elections: national, local, barangay, ARMM, and SK (Sangguniang Kabataan) elections.
★ Special elections: plebiscite, referendum, initiative, and recall.
Added: a plebiscite is a vote to approve or reject a constitutional change or the creation, merger, or division of a local government unit.
Added: a referendum is a vote to approve or reject a law or local measure already passed.
Added: an initiative is when the people themselves propose and pass a law.
Added: a recall is a vote to remove an elected official before the end of the term, for loss of confidence.
EASIER: Regular elections choose officials on a fixed schedule. Special elections are called for a specific issue or person.

HOW OFTEN ELECTIONS ARE HELD
⚙ National and local elections: every second Monday of May.
⚙ President and Vice-President: every 6 years.
⚙ Senators, Representatives, and provincial, city, and municipal elective officials: every 3 years.
⚙ Barangay and SK elections: every last Monday of October, every 3 years.
⚙ ARMM elections: every 3 years from March 1993.
ARMM has since been replaced by BARMM (Bangsamoro), but the lesson lists ARMM.
Difference: the President and Vice-President have 6-year terms. Senators are listed with every-3-year elections because half of the Senate is elected every 3 years for 6-year terms.

VOTER REGISTRATION
⚙ To take part in electoral activities, a person must register.
★ Registration: accomplishing and filing a sworn application for registration by a qualified voter.
⚙ Why register: to have the opportunity to choose our leaders.
★ Requirements for registration: a Filipino citizen; at least 18 years old; a resident of the Philippines for at least 1 year and of the city or municipality for at least 6 months immediately before the election; and not otherwise disqualified by law.
★ Validation of registration: the voter's fingerprints are captured electronically, at no cost to the voter.
⚙ Where to register: the COMELEC office.
⚙ How to transfer registration, Step 1: apply with the Election Officer of your new residence for the transfer of your registration records.
⚙ How to transfer registration, Step 2: accomplish six (6) application forms.
★ COMELEC: the Commission on Elections, the government body that runs elections.
EASIER: To vote you must be a Filipino, 18 or older, living in the Philippines for 1 year and in your town for 6 months, and not disqualified.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Mixing up RA 8044 (1995, youth and the National Youth Commission) and RA 9163 (2002, NSTP).
⚙ Mixing up CWTS and LTS. LTS is only about teaching literacy and numeracy.
⚙ Mixing up the residency rules: 1 year in the Philippines, 6 months in the city or municipality.
⚙ Mixing up cultural cohesiveness and moral consensus.
⚙ Putting plebiscite or recall under regular elections. They are special elections.
⚙ Saying all elections are in May. Barangay and SK elections are on the last Monday of October.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Know the dates: RA 8044 in 1995, RA 9163 signed January 23, 2002.
⚙ Know the youth age range: 15 to 30.
⚙ Know the three components and what each focuses on.
⚙ Know the three kinds of development and the seven elements of national security.
⚙ Know the voter requirements and the election schedule.
