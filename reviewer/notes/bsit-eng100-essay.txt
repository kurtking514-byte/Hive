Essay Expressions Cause-and-Effect and Comparison-and-Contrast Expressions
Useful transitional expressions for cause-and-effect essays and comparison-and-contrast essays

KEY TERMS
★ Transitional expression: a word or phrase that links ideas and shows how they are related (for example, cause to effect, or one subject to another).
★ Cause: the reason why something happens. It answers "Why did something happen?"
★ Effect: the result or consequence of a cause. It answers "What happened as a result?"
★ Cause-and-effect essay: an essay that explains the reasons behind a situation and describes the results or consequences of particular actions or events.
★ Comparison-and-contrast essay: an essay that identifies similarities and differences between two or more subjects, ideas, situations, experiences, or phenomena.
★ Comparison: showing how subjects are alike (similarities).
★ Contrast: showing how subjects are different (differences).
EASIER: Cause is the "why", effect is the "what happened". Compare is "how they are alike", contrast is "how they are different".
These expressions help organize paragraphs and present clear, logical arguments in academic essays.

PART ONE: CAUSE-AND-EFFECT EXPRESSIONS
The cause-and-effect handout has ten parts: introducing the cause, explaining multiple causes, introducing the effect, explaining multiple effects, showing cause-and-effect relationships, showing a chain of causes and effects, comparing positive and negative effects, concluding, a sample paragraph, and a quick reference.

INTRODUCING THE CAUSE
★ Expressions for introducing the cause: used to explain the reasons why something happens.
⚙ Because of: "Because of poverty, some students struggle to continue their education."
⚙ Due to: "Due to climate change, extreme weather events have become more frequent."
⚙ Owing to: "Owing to technological advancements, communication has become faster."
⚙ As a result of: "As a result of poor time management, students may experience academic difficulties."
⚙ Since: "Since many people depend on social media, misinformation spreads quickly."
⚙ Because: "Many students perform poorly because they lack adequate preparation."
⚙ One reason for this is: "One reason for this is the lack of parental guidance."
⚙ The primary cause of: "The primary cause of air pollution is the excessive emission of harmful gases."
⚙ This can be attributed to: "The increase in online learning can be attributed to technological development."
⚙ Stemming from (stem from): "Many social problems stem from economic inequality."
EASIER: In every sample, the words right after the expression are the cause: poverty, climate change, technological advancements, poor time management.
Added example: "Because of", "due to", and "owing to" are followed by a noun phrase (because of poverty), while "because" and "since" are followed by a full clause (because they lack adequate preparation).
Difference: "As a result of" (with "of") introduces the cause: "As a result of poor time management, students struggle." "As a result" (no "of", followed by a comma) introduces the effect: "Many students lack sleep. As a result, their performance suffers."

EXPLAINING MULTIPLE CAUSES
★ Expressions for explaining multiple causes: help present two or more reasons for a particular situation.
⚙ One major cause is...
⚙ Another contributing factor is...
⚙ A further reason is...
⚙ Among the main reasons are...
⚙ This situation results from several factors, including...
⚙ There are several factors that contribute to...
⚙ The problem can be traced to...
⚙ This phenomenon is largely influenced by...
⚙ In addition to this,...
⚙ Furthermore,...
EXAMPLE: MULTIPLE CAUSES
"Several factors contribute to students' declining academic performance. One major cause is excessive social media use. Another contributing factor is poor time management. Furthermore, the lack of parental supervision may worsen the situation."
EASIER: The three causes are listed one after another with "One major cause is", "Another contributing factor is", and "Furthermore".

INTRODUCING THE EFFECT OR RESULT
★ Expressions for introducing the effect or result: used to explain what happens because of a particular cause.
⚙ As a result: "Many students lack sleep. As a result, their classroom performance suffers."
⚙ Consequently: "The company reduced its workforce. Consequently, many employees lost their jobs."
⚙ Therefore: "People consume excessive amounts of sugar. Therefore, they become more vulnerable to certain health problems."
⚙ Thus: "The environment continues to deteriorate. Thus, immediate action is necessary."
⚙ Hence: "Many families experience financial difficulties; hence, some children leave school."
⚙ For this reason: "Public transportation is limited. For this reason, traffic congestion increases."
⚙ As a consequence: "Forests are continuously destroyed. As a consequence, wildlife loses its natural habitat."
⚙ This leads to: "Excessive screen time leads to sleep disturbances."
⚙ This results in: "Poor communication results in misunderstandings."
⚙ This brings about: "Rapid urbanization brings about changes in people's lifestyles."
EASIER: In these samples the cause comes first, then the effect expression, then the result: cause, "Consequently,", effect.
Difference: "results in" points forward to the effect (Poor communication results in misunderstandings). "results from" points back to the cause (This situation results from several factors).

EXPLAINING MULTIPLE EFFECTS
★ Expressions for explaining multiple effects: help discuss several consequences of one particular cause.
⚙ One significant effect is...
⚙ Another possible consequence is...
⚙ This can also result in...
⚙ This may eventually lead to...
⚙ Among its major consequences are...
⚙ The situation may give rise to...
⚙ This has a considerable impact on...
⚙ This negatively/positively affects...
⚙ In the long run,...
⚙ Ultimately,...
EXAMPLE: MULTIPLE EFFECTS
"Excessive use of social media has several effects on students. One significant effect is reduced concentration. Another possible consequence is poor academic performance. In the long run, this habit may negatively affect students' personal and academic development."
Difference: multiple causes means several reasons lead to one situation. Multiple effects means one cause leads to several consequences.

SHOWING CAUSE-AND-EFFECT RELATIONSHIPS
★ Expressions for showing cause-and-effect relationships: establish a direct relationship between causes and their corresponding effects.
⚙ The more..., the more...
⚙ The less..., the more...
⚙ This is directly related to...
⚙ This contributes significantly to...
⚙ This has resulted in...
⚙ This has led to...
⚙ This has brought about...
⚙ This is one of the reasons why...
⚙ As a direct consequence of...
⚙ This explains why...
EXAMPLE: DIRECT RELATIONSHIPS
Sample A: "The more students practice reading, the more their vocabulary develops."
Sample B: "Poor environmental management has led to an increase in pollution."
Sample C: "Lack of financial resources contributes significantly to educational inequality."
EASIER: "The more..., the more..." shows that as one thing grows, the other grows too, like more practice giving a bigger vocabulary.

SHOWING A CHAIN OF CAUSES AND EFFECTS
★ Expressions for showing a chain of causes and effects: used when one effect becomes the cause of another effect.
⚙ This, in turn, leads to...
⚙ Consequently, this creates...
⚙ As a result, this causes...
⚙ This triggers a series of...
⚙ This creates a cycle of...
⚙ This eventually results in...
⚙ One consequence leads to another...
⚙ This sets off a chain reaction...
⚙ Over time, this may cause...
EASIER: A chain is like falling dominoes: each result knocks over the next one.
EXAMPLE: CHAIN OF EFFECTS
"Unemployment can lead to financial instability. This, in turn, may cause family conflicts. Consequently, children may experience emotional stress, which eventually affects their academic performance."
⚙ Link 1: Unemployment.
⚙ Link 2: Financial instability.
⚙ Link 3: Family conflicts ("This, in turn,").
⚙ Link 4: Children's emotional stress ("Consequently,").
⚙ Link 5: Affected academic performance ("eventually").
Difference: a direct relationship links one cause straight to its effect. A chain shows one effect turning into the cause of the next effect.

COMPARING POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE EFFECTS
★ Expressions for comparing positive and negative effects: help examine both beneficial and harmful consequences.
Each positive expression is paired with its negative partner:
⚙ Positive: One positive outcome is... Negative: One negative consequence is...
⚙ Positive: This provides several benefits... Negative: This creates several problems...
⚙ Positive: This contributes to... Negative: This gives rise to...
⚙ Positive: This promotes... Negative: This discourages...
⚙ Positive: This improves... Negative: This undermines...
⚙ Positive: This strengthens... Negative: This weakens...
⚙ Positive: This has a beneficial impact on... Negative: This has a detrimental impact on...
⚙ Positive: This creates opportunities for... Negative: This creates challenges for...
EASIER: "Beneficial" means helpful; "detrimental" means harmful. "Undermines" means slowly weakens or damages.

CONCLUDING A CAUSE-AND-EFFECT ESSAY
★ Expressions for concluding a cause-and-effect essay: help summarize the relationship between the causes and effects discussed in the essay.
⚙ In conclusion,...
⚙ To sum up,...
⚙ Overall,...
⚙ In summary,...
⚙ Considering these causes and effects,...
⚙ These factors demonstrate that...
⚙ The discussion clearly shows that...
⚙ Therefore, it is evident that...
⚙ Ultimately, understanding these causes and effects is essential because...
⚙ Addressing these underlying causes can help prevent further consequences.

EXAMPLE: SAMPLE CAUSE-AND-EFFECT PARAGRAPH
Topic: The Effects of Excessive Social Media Use on Students.
"Social media has become an essential part of students' daily lives. However, excessive use of these platforms can negatively affect their academic performance and personal development. One major cause of this problem is the amount of time students spend browsing social media instead of completing their academic tasks. As a result, they may develop poor study habits and experience difficulties managing their time. Furthermore, constant exposure to online content can reduce their ability to concentrate. Consequently, students may struggle to understand lessons and complete academic requirements. In the long run, excessive social media use may lead to lower academic achievement and reduced productivity. Therefore, students must develop responsible social media habits to maintain a healthy balance between their academic responsibilities and personal activities."
The expressions appear in this order:
⚙ Step 1: "One major cause" introduces the cause (time spent browsing).
⚙ Step 2: "As a result" introduces an effect (poor study habits, time management difficulties).
⚙ Step 3: "Furthermore" adds another point (reduced ability to concentrate).
⚙ Step 4: "Consequently" introduces a further effect (struggling with lessons and requirements).
⚙ Step 5: "In the long run" gives the long-term effect (lower achievement, reduced productivity).
⚙ Step 6: "Therefore" gives the closing recommendation (develop responsible social media habits).

QUICK REFERENCE: CAUSE-AND-EFFECT EXPRESSIONS
⚙ Introducing causes: because, since, due to, owing to.
⚙ Adding causes: another reason, furthermore, in addition.
⚙ Emphasizing causes: the primary cause, a major contributing factor.
⚙ Introducing effects: as a result, consequently, therefore, thus.
⚙ Adding effects: moreover, another consequence, additionally.
⚙ Showing direct relationships: leads to, results in, contributes to.
⚙ Showing indirect relationships: this, in turn, eventually, over time.
⚙ Showing consequences: as a consequence, ultimately, in the long run.
⚙ Concluding: in conclusion, overall, to sum up.

WRITING REMINDER FOR CAUSE-AND-EFFECT ESSAYS
⚙ Avoid using the same transitional expression repeatedly.
⚙ Choose expressions that accurately represent the relationship between ideas.
Guide questions for each kind of idea:
★ Cause: Why did something happen?
★ Effect: What happened as a result?
★ Multiple causes: What factors contributed to the situation?
★ Multiple effects: What consequences followed?
★ Chain of effects: How did one consequence lead to another?

PART TWO: COMPARISON-AND-CONTRAST EXPRESSIONS
The comparison-and-contrast handout has twelve parts: introducing the subjects, showing similarities, adding similarities, showing differences, emphasizing major differences, comparing and contrasting at the same time, degrees of similarity and difference, advantages and disadvantages, concluding, a quick reference, writing reminders, and two organizational patterns.
Most samples compare traditional classroom learning (Subject A) with online learning (Subject B).

INTRODUCING THE SUBJECTS BEING COMPARED
★ Expressions for introducing the subjects being compared: introduce the two subjects that will be examined.
⚙ Both A and B...
⚙ A and B are two examples of...
⚙ A comparison between A and B reveals that...
⚙ A and B share several characteristics.
⚙ Although A and B appear different, they have several similarities.
⚙ A and B can be examined in terms of...
⚙ When comparing A and B, it is important to consider...
⚙ This essay examines the similarities and differences between...
⚙ There are several important aspects to consider when comparing...
⚙ A closer examination of A and B shows that...
EXAMPLE: INTRODUCING SUBJECTS
"This essay examines the similarities and differences between traditional classroom learning and online learning in terms of accessibility, interaction, and learning effectiveness."

SHOWING SIMILARITIES
★ Expressions for showing similarities: highlight common characteristics, shared features, and comparable ideas.
⚙ Similarly: "Similarly, both teaching methods encourage student participation."
⚙ Likewise: "Likewise, online and traditional classes require effective time management."
⚙ In the same way: "In the same way, both approaches promote independent learning."
⚙ Both...and...: "Both teachers and students benefit from effective communication."
⚙ Just as: "Just as traditional classrooms provide interaction, online classes offer virtual communication."
⚙ Equally: "Both methods are equally important in developing students' knowledge."
⚙ In a similar manner: "In a similar manner, both systems require careful preparation."
⚙ Correspondingly: "Correspondingly, both approaches emphasize the importance of assessment."
⚙ Have in common: "These two educational approaches have several characteristics in common."
⚙ Share similar characteristics: "Both cultures share similar characteristics in their family traditions."
⚙ Are alike in terms of: "The two approaches are alike in terms of their learning objectives."
⚙ Comparable to: "The flexibility of online learning is comparable to that of independent study."
EASIER: All of these mean "and the same is true here".

ADDING MORE SIMILARITIES
★ Expressions for adding more similarities: help introduce additional points of comparison.
⚙ Another similarity is...
⚙ In addition to this,...
⚙ Furthermore,...
⚙ Moreover,...
⚙ Another common feature is...
⚙ Both subjects also demonstrate...
⚙ A further similarity can be observed in...
⚙ Another point of agreement is...
⚙ Aside from this,...
⚙ Not only do they..., but they also...
EXAMPLE: ADDING SIMILARITIES
"Both traditional and online learning encourage students to acquire knowledge. Furthermore, both approaches require discipline and active participation. Another common feature is their reliance on instructional materials and assessment activities."
Difference: "Similarly" and "Likewise" show a similarity. "Furthermore" and "Moreover" only add one more point; they can add a cause, an effect, or a similarity depending on the essay.

SHOWING DIFFERENCES
★ Expressions for showing differences: emphasize contrasting characteristics, opposing ideas, and distinctions between two subjects.
⚙ However: "Traditional classes require physical attendance. However, online classes can be attended remotely."
⚙ In contrast: "Traditional learning is classroom-based. In contrast, online learning takes place through digital platforms."
⚙ On the other hand: "Traditional classes offer face-to-face interaction. On the other hand, online classes rely heavily on virtual communication."
⚙ Whereas: "Traditional learning requires students to attend school, whereas online learning allows remote participation."
⚙ While: "While one approach emphasizes physical interaction, the other emphasizes technological connectivity."
⚙ Unlike: "Unlike traditional classrooms, virtual classrooms do not require students to be physically present."
⚙ Conversely: "Some students prefer structured classroom instruction. Conversely, others prefer flexible online learning."
⚙ Nevertheless: "Online learning offers flexibility. Nevertheless, it may limit face-to-face interaction."
⚙ In comparison: "In comparison, traditional classrooms provide more opportunities for direct interaction."
⚙ Differ from: "The two approaches differ in terms of accessibility and instructional delivery."
⚙ Are distinct from: "Their learning environments are distinct from one another."
⚙ In opposition to: "In opposition to the traditional approach, the alternative emphasizes learner independence."
EASIER: All of these mean "but this one is different".
Added example: "However", "In contrast", "On the other hand", "Conversely", and "Nevertheless" usually start a new sentence and take a comma, while "whereas" and "while" join two contrasting ideas inside one sentence.
Difference: "Similarly" shows likeness; "Conversely" shows the opposite. Do not swap them.

EMPHASIZING MAJOR DIFFERENCES
★ Expressions for emphasizing major differences: help highlight important distinctions between subjects.
⚙ One major difference is...
⚙ The most noticeable difference is...
⚙ Unlike A, B...
⚙ A significant distinction between the two is...
⚙ A differs from B in terms of...
⚙ The primary distinction lies in...
⚙ A is characterized by..., whereas B...
⚙ One is more..., while the other is...
⚙ A places greater emphasis on..., while B focuses on...
⚙ The two subjects vary considerably in terms of...
EXAMPLE: MAJOR DIFFERENCE
"One major difference between traditional and online learning is the mode of instruction. Traditional learning emphasizes face-to-face interaction, whereas online learning relies on digital communication and technological resources."
Difference: "However" just shows a difference. "The most noticeable difference is" or "The primary distinction lies in" tells the reader this difference is the most important one.

COMPARING AND CONTRASTING AT THE SAME TIME
★ Expressions for comparing and contrasting at the same time: allow discussing similarities and differences within the same sentence or paragraph.
⚙ Although A and B share similarities, they differ in...
⚙ While both A and B..., they differ in terms of...
⚙ Despite their similarities, A and B...
⚙ A is similar to B in terms of..., but different in...
⚙ Both approaches..., although...
⚙ Whereas A emphasizes..., B focuses on...
⚙ A and B have common objectives; however, their methods differ.
⚙ Despite having similar purposes, the two differ in their implementation.
⚙ Although they share certain characteristics, their outcomes may vary.
⚙ A resembles B in some aspects, yet they are fundamentally different in others.
EXAMPLE: SAME-TIME COMPARISON
"Although traditional and online learning share the same educational objectives, they differ in terms of instructional delivery, classroom interaction, and learning environment."
EASIER: These sentences have two halves: one half says how they are alike, the other half says how they differ.

SHOWING DEGREES OF SIMILARITY AND DIFFERENCE
★ Expressions for showing degrees of similarity and difference: help communicate how closely two subjects resemble or differ from each other.
⚙ Almost identical to: "The two approaches are almost identical in their objectives."
⚙ Very similar to: "Their teaching strategies are very similar to each other."
⚙ Slightly different from: "The second method is slightly different from the first."
⚙ Significantly different from: "Their learning outcomes are significantly different."
⚙ More...than: "Online learning is more flexible than traditional learning."
⚙ Less...than: "Traditional learning may be less accessible than online learning for remote students."
⚙ As...as: "Both approaches are as important as each other in certain learning contexts."
⚙ To a greater extent: "Online learning provides flexibility to a greater extent."
⚙ To a lesser extent: "Some students experience classroom interaction to a lesser extent in online settings."
⚙ In many respects: "The two systems are similar in many respects."
EASIER: Degree means "how much". Think of a scale: almost identical, very similar, slightly different, significantly different.

COMPARING ADVANTAGES AND DISADVANTAGES
★ Expressions for comparing advantages and disadvantages: useful when evaluating the strengths and weaknesses of two subjects.
Each advantage expression is paired with its disadvantage partner:
⚙ Advantage: One advantage of A is... Disadvantage: One disadvantage of A is...
⚙ Advantage: A offers several benefits... Disadvantage: A presents several challenges...
⚙ Advantage: A provides opportunities for... Disadvantage: A may create difficulties in...
⚙ Advantage: A is beneficial because... Disadvantage: A is problematic because...
⚙ Advantage: A is more effective in terms of... Disadvantage: A is less effective when it comes to...
⚙ Advantage: A has a positive impact on... Disadvantage: A may negatively affect...
⚙ Advantage: A is more convenient than... Disadvantage: A is less practical than...
⚙ Advantage: A offers greater flexibility... Disadvantage: A may have certain limitations...
Added example: "One advantage of online learning is its flexibility. One disadvantage of online learning is that it may limit face-to-face interaction."

CONCLUDING A COMPARISON AND CONTRAST ESSAY
★ Expressions for concluding a comparison and contrast essay: help summarize the similarities and differences and present an overall evaluation.
⚙ In conclusion,...
⚙ To sum up,...
⚙ Overall,...
⚙ In summary,...
⚙ After examining both subjects,...
⚙ Based on the similarities and differences presented,...
⚙ The comparison demonstrates that...
⚙ The discussion reveals that...
⚙ Although both subjects share common characteristics,...
⚙ Despite their differences, both approaches...
⚙ Ultimately, the choice between the two depends on...
⚙ Both approaches have their own strengths and limitations.
EXAMPLE: COMPARISON CONCLUSION
"In conclusion, traditional and online learning share the common goal of providing quality education. However, they differ in terms of accessibility, instructional delivery, and student interaction. Ultimately, the effectiveness of either approach depends on students' needs, learning preferences, and available resources."

QUICK REFERENCE: COMPARISON AND CONTRAST EXPRESSIONS
⚙ Introducing subjects: both A and B; A and B can be compared in terms of...
⚙ Showing similarities: similarly; likewise; in the same way; equally.
⚙ Adding similarities: furthermore; moreover; another similarity; in addition.
⚙ Showing differences: however; in contrast; whereas; unlike.
⚙ Emphasizing differences: one major difference; a significant distinction; differs from.
⚙ Comparing simultaneously: although; despite; while both; nevertheless.
⚙ Showing degrees: more than; less than; as...as; almost identical.
⚙ Evaluating advantages: one advantage; offers benefits; provides opportunities.
⚙ Evaluating disadvantages: one limitation; presents challenges; may negatively affect.
⚙ Concluding: in conclusion; overall; ultimately; based on the comparison.
Note: "Nevertheless" is a showing-differences expression in the main list but is placed under comparing simultaneously in the quick reference, because it admits one point and then turns to a contrasting one.

WRITING REMINDERS FOR COMPARISON AND CONTRAST ESSAYS
⚙ Reminder 1: Identify the subjects clearly. Introduce the two subjects being compared and contrasted.
⚙ Reminder 2: Use appropriate transitional expressions. Select expressions that accurately show similarities or differences.
⚙ Reminder 3: Maintain a balanced discussion. Discuss both subjects fairly rather than focusing only on one.
⚙ Reminder 4: Organize ideas logically. Use either the point-by-point method or the block method.
⚙ Reminder 5: Avoid repetitive expressions. Vary transitional words and phrases to improve academic writing.
⚙ Reminder 6: Provide specific examples. Support each similarity or difference with relevant evidence.

TWO COMMON ORGANIZATIONAL PATTERNS
★ Block method: discuss all the characteristics of Subject A first, followed by all the characteristics of Subject B.
⚙ Paragraph 1: Introduction.
⚙ Paragraph 2: Subject A, Traditional Learning.
⚙ Paragraph 3: Subject B, Online Learning.
⚙ Paragraph 4: Conclusion.
★ Point-by-point method: discuss both subjects according to specific points of comparison.
⚙ Paragraph 1: Introduction.
⚙ Paragraph 2: Accessibility, A vs. B.
⚙ Paragraph 3: Interaction, A vs. B.
⚙ Paragraph 4: Flexibility, A vs. B.
⚙ Paragraph 5: Conclusion.
EASIER: Block method is like finishing one whole plate before starting the next. Point-by-point is like taking one bite from each plate, topic by topic.
Difference: the block method has one body paragraph per subject (4 paragraphs in the sample). The point-by-point method has one body paragraph per point of comparison (5 paragraphs in the sample).

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Using "As a result" (introduces an effect) when you mean "As a result of" (introduces a cause).
⚙ Using a similarity word such as "Likewise" before a sentence that actually shows a difference.
⚙ Treating "whereas" like "however": "whereas" joins two ideas in one sentence.
⚙ Confusing multiple effects (one cause, many consequences) with a chain of effects (each effect causes the next).
⚙ Repeating the same transition, such as "Furthermore" in every sentence.
⚙ In a block essay, mixing points about both subjects in one body paragraph.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Know which group each expression belongs to, for example "owing to" introduces a cause and "hence" introduces an effect.
⚙ Know the paired partners: beneficial impact and detrimental impact, promotes and discourages, improves and undermines, strengthens and weakens.
⚙ Know the paragraph order of the block method and the point-by-point method.
⚙ Know the five guide questions: cause, effect, multiple causes, multiple effects, chain of effects.
