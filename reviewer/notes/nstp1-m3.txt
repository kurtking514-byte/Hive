Lesson 4 Drug Prevention and Rehabilitation
What drugs are, misuse versus abuse versus dependence, kinds of drugs, causes, effects, the school's role, and RA 9165

KEY TERMS
★ Drug: a substance used as a medication, for the cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease.
⚙ A drug alters the function of living organisms.
⚙ A drug changes or enhances the physical and psychological functions of the body.
EASIER: A drug is anything taken into the body that changes how the body or mind works. Medicine is a drug used the right way.
★ Mitigation: making something less severe, such as easing pain.
★ Drug misuse: when a person uses a drug to fulfill a need that the drug cannot pharmacologically fulfill.
EASIER: Pharmacologically means "by its chemical action in the body". Misuse is expecting a drug to do something it was never made to do.
★ Drug abuse: when a person uses a drug excessively and indiscriminately, beyond its medical purpose, so that it interferes with the person's health or social behavior.
★ Drug dependence: a state of psychological and/or physical dependence on a dangerous drug, following periodic or continuous use of the drug to achieve the desired effect.
★ Tolerance: the user becomes "immune" to the drug, so the same amount no longer gives the same effect.
★ Habituation: a continued desire for a drug.
Difference: misuse is using a drug for the wrong need. Abuse is using it too much and without care, beyond its medical purpose. Dependence is when the body or mind already needs the drug.
Difference: tolerance means the drug works less and less. Habituation means the person keeps wanting it.

DRUG MISUSE
Examples from the lesson:
⚙ Athletes use steroids or amphetamines to improve their skills.
⚙ A prostitute uses marijuana or "shabu" for confidence. The rest of this slide is cut off.
EASIER: A drug cannot give real skill or real confidence, so using it for that is misuse.
Drugs that can be misused:
⚙ Legal drugs: prescribed drugs and over-the-counter (OTC) drugs.
⚙ Illegal drugs.
⚙ Psychoactive substances.
★ OTC (over-the-counter) drugs: medicines that can be bought without a doctor's prescription.
★ Psychoactive substances: substances that modify a person's perception, mood, cognition, behavior, and motor functions.
⚙ The five things a psychoactive substance can change: perception, mood, cognitive function, behavior, and motor functions.
EASIER: Psychoactive means it acts on the mind. Perception is how you sense things, cognition is how you think, and motor functions are how you move.
★ Substance abuse: the harmful use of psychoactive substances, including alcohol and other drugs (AOD).

CLASSIFICATION OF DANGEROUS DRUGS
★ Prohibited drugs: drugs that produce the psychological effects of narcotics, which relieve pain and induce sleep.
★ Regulated drugs: self-inducing sedatives like barbiturates, drugs similar to amphetamines or hypnotic drugs, and compounds producing the same physiological effects.
EASIER: Prohibited drugs are banned outright. Regulated drugs can be legal medicine, but only under strict control, such as with a prescription.
Difference: prohibited means not allowed at all. Regulated means allowed only under controlled conditions.

DRUGS COMMONLY ABUSED
★ Stimulants: drugs that increase alertness and physical disposition.
⚙ Stimulant examples: amphetamine, methamphetamine, cocaine, caffeine, and nicotine.
★ Hallucinogens: drugs that affect sensation, thinking, self-awareness, and emotion.
⚙ Hallucinogen examples: LSD, mescaline, and marijuana.
★ Sedatives: drugs that may reduce anxiety and excitement, and can sedate and induce sleep.
⚙ Sedative examples: barbiturates, non-barbiturates, tranquilizers, alcohol, and Valium.
★ Narcotics: drugs that relieve pain and often induce sleep. In large doses they may produce stupor, coma, and death.
⚙ Narcotic examples: opium and its derivatives, such as morphine, codeine, and heroin.
★ Inhalants: volatile chemical substances with psychoactive vapors that produce a state of intoxication. They are usually inhaled or sniffed through the nose.
⚙ Inhalant example: Rugby or other solvents.
★ Anabolic steroids: chemical substances taken orally or injected into the body that temporarily enhance physical and mental performance and develop muscles.
⚙ Anabolic steroids have serious side effects on internal organs like the liver and kidneys.
⚙ Anabolic steroid example: Dianabol.
EASIER: Stimulants speed you up, sedatives slow you down, narcotics kill pain and bring sleep, hallucinogens change what you sense, inhalants are sniffed fumes, and steroids build muscle.
Difference: sedatives calm and bring sleep. Narcotics mainly relieve pain, and an overdose can cause coma and death.
Difference: caffeine and nicotine are legal, but the lesson still lists them as stimulants.
★ Volatile: evaporates easily into a vapor, like glue or paint thinner fumes.
★ Stupor: a state of near unconsciousness.

Difference: personality dynamics are traits inside the person. Common causes are situations or reasons, such as friends, boredom, or easy access.

HARMFUL EFFECTS OF DRUGS
★ Physical effects: malnutrition, skin infections, fetal deformity, impaired perception and judgment, and secondary psychosis.
★ Psychological effects: amotivational syndrome and lowering of IQ.
★ Social effects: being socially non-functional, deterioration of interpersonal relationships, and being socially maladjusted.
★ Spiritual and moral effects: becoming a pathological liar and manipulative; having no relationship with God, forgetting God's existence so that drugs become one's god; lowering of moral values; and indulging in prostitution.
★ Economic effect: expensive treatment and rehabilitation of drug dependents.
★ Amotivational syndrome: loss of drive and interest in doing things. The slide writes "motivational syndrome", but the condition is called amotivational syndrome.
★ Fetal deformity: harm to an unborn baby when a pregnant woman uses drugs.
★ Secondary psychosis: losing touch with reality because of drug use.
EASIER: Drugs harm five areas of life: body, mind, relationships, morals, and money.

ROLE OF THE SCHOOL IN THE FIGHT AGAINST DRUGS
⚙ Include drug education in the curriculum and impress upon students the evil effects of drugs.
⚙ School and home should cooperate so that children and siblings do not become victims of drug addiction.
⚙ School authorities should have surveillance against dope pushers.
⚙ Report drug pushers loitering on school premises to the proper authority.
⚙ A student who is a dope addict should not be treated like a criminal but like a patient who needs treatment.
⚙ Stiffer penalties should be given to dope pushers and drug dealers.

⚙ Love yourself.
⚙ Talk.
⚙ Look for alternatives.
⚙ Be pro-active.
⚙ Seek advice and help.
★ Dope pusher: a person who sells illegal drugs.

REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9165
★ Republic Act No. 9165: the Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act of 2002, dated June 7, 2002.
★ Use of dangerous drugs: a person apprehended or arrested who is found positive for use of any dangerous drug after a confirmatory test shall get a penalty of a minimum of six (6) months rehabilitation in a government center for the first offense, subject to Article VIII of the Act.
⚙ First offense for drug use: at least 6 months of rehabilitation in a government center.
⚙ It applies only after a confirmatory test is positive.
★ Confirmatory test: a second, more accurate drug test that confirms the first result.

FACTS AND MYTHS ABOUT DRUGS
Facts:
⚙ Some drugs create addiction much faster than others. Addiction depends on the what, who, why, where, and how of drug use.
⚙ Some people have problems the first time they use a drug; others develop problems as they use more often.
⚙ Different drugs harm in different ways. Heroin is seen as more dangerous because of a higher risk of addiction and overdose, or because it is injected.
⚙ Many possible signs, such as mood swings or losing interest in hobbies or study, are also normal teenage behavior. It is better to talk than to jump to conclusions.
⚙ Most young people are introduced to illegal drugs by a friend or someone they know. Drugs are usually "pulled" (the person asks for them, often out of curiosity) rather than "pushed".
Myths:
⚙ "All drugs are addictive."
⚙ "Only drug addicts have a problem."
⚙ "All illegal drugs are equally harmful."
⚙ "My teenager is moody and losing interest in school; they must be on drugs."
⚙ "Young people are tempted to try drugs by pushers."
EASIER: Each myth is the opposite of a fact above. Young people usually get drugs from friends, not pushers.

STAGES OF DRUG USE
⚙ Free of use (drug free), experimental use, recreational use, addiction.
★ Drug free: a status that is aspirational rather than actual (a goal to aim for).
★ Experimental (exploratory) use: a short-term learning phase influenced by culture and availability, marked by peer group activity and random choice of drugs.
⚙ Curiosity and risk-taking are the main motives. The mood-altering effect is secondary to the "adventure".
⚙ Young people may try more than one substance, but usually not more than a few times.
★ Recreational (social) use: regular use, as a group activity, over a longer period of time.
⚙ Social acceptance is the main motive. The setting is social: parties, drinking, smoking with friends.
⚙ Drugs are freely shared or sold at cost; the aim is to fit in and "loosen up".
★ Addiction (habitual use): use starts to affect the whole of the young person's life.
⚙ Relationships, peers, and activities become drug-related.
⚙ Sleep and concentration problems; withdrawal symptoms after long use.
⚙ Tolerance may increase, cravings appear, and the user keeps thinking about the next time.
⚙ Behavior problems increase and school performance is seriously affected.
EASIER: Experimental is trying out of curiosity, recreational is using to fit in, and addiction is when drugs take over your life.

HOW TO AVOID DRUG USE
⚙ Understand how alcohol and drug addiction develops.
⚙ Get help if you have an alcohol or drug problem.
⚙ Avoid temptation and peer pressure.
⚙ Find the support you need.
⚙ Maintain physical wellness and mental health.
⚙ Use drugs properly.
⚙ Accept yourself.
⚙ Develop your potential and engage in productive and fulfilling activities.
⚙ Communicate your issues and concerns.
⚙ Learn how to cope with your problems.
⚙ Seek professional help if you cannot cope.
⚙ Develop strong moral and spiritual foundations.

RA 9165 AND THE SCHOOLS
⚙ The reading includes the sections of RA 9165 on the participation of the family, students, teachers, and school authorities.
★ Sec. 41, Involvement of the Family: the family is the basic unit of society; parents are primarily responsible for teaching their family about the ill effects of drugs and watching closely over its members.
★ Sec. 42, Student Councils and Campus Organizations: they must include in their activities a program for preventing drug use and for referring students for treatment and rehabilitation.
★ Sec. 43, School Curricula: drug prevention and control must be taught in elementary, secondary, and tertiary curricula, including non-formal, technical-vocational, and agricultural schools.
★ Sec. 44, Heads, Supervisors, and Teachers of Schools: they are deemed persons in authority and may apprehend or arrest anyone violating the Act inside the school or its immediate vicinity.
★ Sec. 45, Publication and Distribution of Materials: DepEd, CHED, and TESDA, with the Dangerous Drugs Board, make and distribute information materials on dangerous drugs to students, faculty, parents, and the community.
★ Sec. 46, Special Drug Education Center: a center in each province for drug education and youth programs, run with the DILG, DSWD, and local government.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Mixing up misuse and abuse. Misuse is using a drug for a need it cannot meet; abuse is excessive, careless use beyond medical purpose.
⚙ Mixing up tolerance and habituation. Tolerance is becoming "immune"; habituation is the continued desire.
⚙ Putting marijuana under stimulants. The lesson lists it as a hallucinogen.
⚙ Putting alcohol under stimulants. The lesson lists it as a sedative.
⚙ Mixing up sedatives and narcotics. Narcotics are the pain relievers from opium, like morphine, codeine, and heroin.
⚙ Saying RA 9165 sends a first-time user to prison. The lesson's provision is a minimum of 6 months rehabilitation in a government center.
⚙ Mixing up experimental and recreational use. Experimental is curiosity and risk-taking; recreational is regular use for social acceptance.
⚙ Believing most young people get drugs from pushers. Most are introduced by a friend or someone they know.
