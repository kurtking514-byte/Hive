Lesson 4 Register and Tone in Spoken and Written Communication
Course ENG100, taught by Kristine Castillo MA-ESL

CLASS OBJECTIVES
⚙ Objective 1: Understand the concepts of register and tone in communication.
⚙ Objective 2: Differentiate between formal and informal registers and tones for an effective communication.
⚙ Objective 3: Apply appropriate register and tone in spoken and written communication.
EASIER: In simple words, first learn what register and tone are, then learn how formal and informal differ, then use the right one when you speak and write.

EXAMPLE: HOW WE GREET DIFFERENT PEOPLE
The lecture opens with three greetings, each used with a different person.
It is an honor to meet you Mr. President. This is very respectful because the listener is the President.
My pleasure to meet you, Sir. This is polite and respectful, shown in a handshake between two people.
Hey sis! This is relaxed and friendly because the listener is a sister.
EASIER: The idea (greeting someone) stays the same, but the words change because the listener changes.

LANGUAGE REGISTERS
★ Language register: the level of formality and style of our speech and writing.
★ A register should be appropriate to the situation we are in.
★ The parts of a situation are audience, topic, purpose, and location.
★ Different situations call for different registers.
EASIER: Register is like choosing clothes. You wear different clothes to a funeral, a classroom, and a beach, and you choose different words for each place and person too.

AUDIENCE
★ Audience is one of the four parts of a situation that decide the register.
EASIER: Audience means the people you are speaking or writing to.
EXAMPLE: AUDIENCE
Ms. Castillo, may we know our exam scores? This is polite and is said to a teacher.
Hey! Dude/bro. This is casual and is said to a friend.

TOPIC
★ Topic is one of the four parts of a situation that decide the register.
EASIER: Topic means the subject being talked or written about.
Added example: Talking about an illness with a doctor uses more careful words than talking about a game with friends.

PURPOSE
★ Purpose is one of the four parts of a situation that decide the register.
★ The author's purposes shown in the lecture are inform, entertain, persuade, and describe.
EASIER: Purpose means why you are speaking or writing.

LOCATION
★ Location is one of the four parts of a situation that decide the register.
EASIER: Location means the place where the communication happens.
Added example: A school library, a coffee shop, and a mall each call for different language.

QUESTIONS A WRITER ASKS
⚙ Step 1: Ask "Why am I writing this?" This question is about purpose.
⚙ Step 2: Ask "Who am I writing this for?" This question is about audience.
⚙ Step 3: Ask "So what sort of writing will suit this purpose?" This question is about form.
⚙ Step 4: Ask "So what style do I need to write in?" This question is about style.
EASIER: Memory trick: purpose first, then audience, then form, then style.

KINDS OF LANGUAGE REGISTERS
★ The five kinds of language registers, in lecture order, are static, formal, consultative, casual, and intimate.
EASIER: Memory trick: the list goes from the most fixed language (static) to the most private language (intimate).

STATIC REGISTER
★ Static register rarely or never changes.
★ Static register is frozen in time in form and content.
★ Static register is extremely formal language that must be said the same each time we say it.
★ Examples of static register: National Anthem, The Lord's Prayer, Pledge of Allegiance, Preamble of the Philippine Constitution, and Law.
EASIER: Static means not moving. You cannot change even one word when you recite the Pledge of Allegiance.

FORMAL REGISTER
★ Formal register is used in a formal setting and is one way in nature.
★ The use of language in formal register usually follows a commonly accepted format.
★ Formal register is usually impersonal and formal, and it includes academic language.
★ Examples of formal register: sermons, rhetorical statements and questions in speeches, pronouncement by the judges, and announcement.
EASIER: One way means one person speaks and the audience listens, like a pastor giving a sermon.
EASIER: Impersonal means it does not sound like a private chat between two close people.

RULES OF THE FORMAL REGISTER
⚙ Rule 1: Do not use contractions.
⚙ Rule 2: Avoid using slang, idioms, and exaggerations.
⚙ Rule 3: Avoid using too much passive voice.
EASIER: A contraction is a shortened word such as "don't". In formal register write "do not" instead.
EASIER: Slang is very casual word use such as "dude". An idiom is a phrase whose meaning is not literal. An exaggeration says something is bigger or worse than it really is.
EASIER: Passive voice puts the receiver of the action first. Active voice is more direct.
Added example: Passive: "The report was written by me." Active: "I wrote the report."

CONSULTATIVE REGISTER
★ Consultative register is the standard form of communications.
★ In consultative register, users engage in a mutually accepted structure of communications.
★ In consultative register, formal and societal expectations accompany the users of the speech.
★ Consultative register is professional discourse or acceptable speech used in professional settings.
★ Pairs that use consultative register: superior and subordinate, doctor and patient, lawyer and client, and teacher and student.
EASIER: Consultative comes from the idea of consulting a professional. Both sides follow polite, accepted rules when they talk.

CASUAL REGISTER
★ Casual register is informal language used by peers and friends.
★ In casual register, slang, vulgarities, and colloquialisms are normal during communication.
★ Examples of casual register: friends or buddies, team mates, group chats, message to friends, blogs, and advertisements.
EASIER: A colloquialism is an everyday spoken expression. A vulgarity is a rude or crude word. These are normal among friends but not in formal situations.

INTIMATE REGISTER
★ Intimate register is private communication.
★ Intimate register is reserved for close family members or intimate people.
★ In intimate register, intonation is more important than wording or grammar.
★ Pairs that use intimate register: husband and wife, boyfriend and girlfriend, siblings, and parent and children.
EASIER: Intonation is the rise and fall of your voice. Between very close people, how you say something matters more than the exact words.

SIMILAR TERMS STUDENTS CONFUSE
Difference: Static register never changes at all, while formal register follows a common format but the wording can still change.
Difference: Formal register is one way in nature, while consultative register is an exchange in which the users engage with each other in a professional setting.
Difference: Casual register is for peers and friends, while intimate register is private and reserved for close family members or intimate people.
Difference: In casual register the wording (slang and colloquialisms) is normal, while in intimate register intonation matters more than wording or grammar.
Difference: Register is the level of formality and style of language, while tone is the attitude or emotion conveyed.
Difference: Formal register is a kind of register, while formal tone is a kind of tone. They are two different ideas.

TONE
★ Tone: the attitude, emotion, or style conveyed in communication.
★ Tone is described as the way a person speaks to someone.
EASIER: The words "Come here" can sound angry or kind. The difference is the tone.

TONE IN SPOKEN COMMUNICATION
★ There are six kinds of spoken tone: formal, informal, authoritative, friendly, expressive, and neutral.
★ Spoken formal tone: polite, respectful, following proper etiquette. Example: giving a presentation in a professional setting.
★ Spoken informal tone: casual, relaxed, conversational. Example: chatting with friends or family.
★ Spoken authoritative tone: confident, assertive, commanding. Example: giving instructions or directions.
★ Spoken friendly tone: warm, approachable, amicable. Example: talking to colleagues during a break.
★ Spoken expressive tone: emotionally charged, passionate. Example: delivering a speech on a topic of personal significance, advertisements or commercials.
★ Spoken neutral tone: unbiased, objective, without emotional emphasis. Example: reporting facts or information without personal opinion.
EASIER: Amicable means friendly and peaceful. Assertive means saying what you want with confidence. Objective means based on facts, not feelings.

TONE IN WRITTEN COMMUNICATION
★ There are seven kinds of written tone: formal, informal, sympathetic, instructive, humorous, critical, and persuasive.
★ Written formal tone: proper grammar, professional language, and structured writing. Examples: business emails and academic papers.
★ Written informal tone: conversational language, relaxed structure, may include colloquialisms. Examples: personal emails and text messages to friends.
★ Written sympathetic tone: compassionate, understanding, empathetic. Example: writing a condolence letter.
★ Written instructive tone: clear instructions and step-by-step guidance. Examples: manuals and how-to guides.
★ Written humorous tone: playful, funny, lighthearted. Examples: comic strips and some advertisements.
★ Written critical tone: analytical, evaluative, may include constructive criticism. Examples: academic critiques and performance reviews.
★ Written persuasive tone: convincing, compelling, using rhetorical strategies. Examples: persuasive essays and advertising copy.
EASIER: A condolence letter is a letter that comforts someone who lost a loved one. Empathetic means you understand and share the other person's feelings.
EASIER: Rhetorical strategies are speaking or writing techniques that help convince people.

TONES THAT STUDENTS CONFUSE
Difference: Authoritative tone is spoken and commanding, while instructive tone is written and gives step-by-step guidance.
Difference: Expressive tone is emotionally charged and passionate, while neutral tone has no emotional emphasis.
Difference: Sympathetic tone comforts and understands, while critical tone analyzes and evaluates.
Difference: Humorous tone aims to be playful, while persuasive tone aims to convince.
Difference: Advertisements appear in several places: casual register, spoken expressive tone, written humorous tone, and written persuasive tone.

COMMON MISTAKES
Mixing up register and tone. Register is the level of formality and style, and tone is the attitude or emotion.
Thinking static register can be reworded. It must be said the same each time.
Using contractions, slang, or idioms in formal register.
Placing consultative register under casual. Teacher and student, doctor and patient, lawyer and client, and superior and subordinate are consultative.
Forgetting that written tone has seven kinds, while spoken tone has six.

EXAM TIPS
Memorize the five registers in order: static, formal, consultative, casual, intimate.
Memorize the three formal register rules: no contractions, no slang or idioms or exaggerations, not too much passive voice.
Memorize the four parts of a situation: audience, topic, purpose, location.
Memorize each tone with its key words and its example.
