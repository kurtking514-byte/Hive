Module 7 The Math of Money
Simple interest, compound interest, and how they compare

KEY TERMS
★ Interest: the "rent" that a borrower pays a lender to use the lender's money.
EASIER: If you borrow a friend's bike, you might give something back for using it. Interest is what you pay for using someone else's money.
★ Principal (P): the present value, the amount of money first borrowed or deposited.
★ Interest rate (r): the percent of the principal charged or earned per year. Always change it to a decimal before using it in a formula (6% becomes 0.06).
★ Time (t): how long the money is borrowed or saved, in years.
★ Future value or accumulated value (A): the total amount at the end, principal plus interest.
★ Simple interest: interest paid only on the original amount deposited, not on past interest.
★ Compound interest: "interest on interest". The interest is added to the principal, and this new total becomes the principal for the next period, so past interest also earns interest.
★ Compounding period: how often interest is added to the principal (annually, semi-annually, quarterly, monthly, or daily).
★ Effective rate of interest: the equivalent simple rate of interest that would give the same amount as the compounding did.
★ Time value of money: money you have now is worth more than the same amount later, because money you have now can earn interest.
Difference: simple interest grows by the same amount every year. Compound interest grows by a bigger amount every year, because each year's interest is computed on a bigger total.
There are two types of interest: simple interest and compound interest.

SIMPLE INTEREST FORMULAS
★ Simple interest: $I = Prt$
★ Future value: $A = P + I = P(1 + rt)$
⚙ I is the amount of interest, P is the principal, r is the yearly rate as a decimal, t is the time in years, and A is the future value.
EASIER: Prt just means principal times rate times time. Multiply the three, and that is the interest. Add it to the principal to get the total.
⚙ Months must be changed to years: 4 months is $\frac{4}{12}$ of a year, and 18 months is $\frac{18}{12} = 1.5$ years.

EXAMPLE: SIMPLE INTEREST ON P3000
P3000 earns simple interest at 6% per year for 2 years. Find the interest and the total.
⚙ Step 1: $P = 3000$, $r = 0.06$, $t = 2$.
⚙ Step 2: $I = Prt = 3000 × 0.06 × 2 = 360$
⚙ Step 3: $A = P + I = 3000 + 360 = 3360$
★ The interest is P360 and the total after 2 years is P3360.
⚙ Check with the other formula: $A = 3000(1 + 0.06 × 2) = 3000(1.12) = 3360$.

EXAMPLE: FINDING THE PRINCIPAL
How much must be in an account to earn P100 simple interest in 4 months at 6.4%?
⚙ Step 1: $I = 100$, $r = 0.064$, $t = \frac{4}{12}$ year.
⚙ Step 2: $100 = P × 0.064 × \frac{4}{12}$
⚙ Step 3: $0.064 × \frac{4}{12} = 0.021333...$, so $P = \dfrac{100}{0.021333...}$
⚙ Step 4: $P = 4687.50$
★ You need P4687.50.
⚙ Check: $4687.50 × 0.064 × \frac{4}{12} = 100$.

EXAMPLE: FINDING THE TIME
How long must P200 stay in an account at 4% simple interest to earn P10?
⚙ Step 1: $I = 10$, $P = 200$, $r = 0.04$.
⚙ Step 2: $10 = 200 × 0.04 × t$, so $10 = 8t$.
⚙ Step 3: $t = \dfrac{10}{8} = 1.25$
★ It takes 1.25 years (1 year and 3 months).
⚙ Check: $200 × 0.04 × 1.25 = 10$.

EXAMPLE: SIMPLE INTEREST FOR 18 MONTHS
P9500 at 7% simple interest for 18 months (from the pair work).
⚙ Step 1: $t = \frac{18}{12} = 1.5$ years.
⚙ Step 2: $I = 9500 × 0.07 × 1.5 = 997.50$
⚙ Step 3: $A = 9500 + 997.50 = 10497.50$
★ The interest is P997.50 and the total is P10,497.50.

COMPOUND INTEREST FORMULA
★ Compound interest: $A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$
⚙ A is the amount accumulated, P is the principal, r is the yearly rate as a decimal, n is the number of times interest is added each year, and t is the number of years.
EASIER: Divide the yearly rate by n to get the rate for one period. Multiply n by t to count how many periods there are. Then grow the principal by that small rate, that many times.
The slide labels n as "compoundings per period" and t as "number of periods". When the rate is a yearly rate, n is the number of compoundings per year and t is the number of years.
★ Compound interest earned: $I = A - P$
⚙ Annually: $n = 1$. Semi-annually: $n = 2$ (twice a year, every 180 days). Quarterly: $n = 4$ (every 90 days). Monthly: $n = 12$ (every 30 days). Daily: $n = 360$ in the lecture's solutions.
Note: one slide says daily means 365 times a year and another uses 360. The worked solution uses 360, so follow your teacher. For P1000 at 7% for 10 years, both give P2013.62.
★ The more compounding periods, the greater the compound interest.
⚙ To evaluate: first work out $\frac{r}{n}$, add 1, raise to the power nt, then multiply by P. Do not round until the last step.

EXAMPLE: P1000 AT 7% FOR 10 YEARS
$P = 1000$, $r = 0.07$, $t = 10$. Only n changes.
⚙ Annually, $n = 1$: $A = 1000(1 + 0.07)^{10} = 1000(1.967151) = 1967.15$
⚙ Semi-annually, $n = 2$: $A = 1000\left(1 + \frac{0.07}{2}\right)^{20} = 1000(1.035)^{20} = 1989.79$
⚙ Quarterly, $n = 4$: $A = 1000\left(1 + \frac{0.07}{4}\right)^{40} = 1000(1.0175)^{40} = 2001.60$
⚙ Daily, $n = 360$: $A = 1000\left(1 + \frac{0.07}{360}\right)^{3600} = 2013.62$
★ More compounding periods give more money: P1967.15, then P1989.79, then P2001.60, then P2013.62.

EXAMPLE: P500 AT 8% COMPOUNDED QUARTERLY FOR 2 YEARS
⚙ Step 1: $P = 500$, $r = 0.08$, $n = 4$, $t = 2$.
⚙ Step 2: $\frac{r}{n} = \frac{0.08}{4} = 0.02$ and $nt = 4 × 2 = 8$.
⚙ Step 3: $A = 500(1.02)^{8} = 500(1.171659) = 585.83$
★ The amount is P585.83, so the interest made is $585.83 - 500 = 85.83$.

EFFECTIVE RATE OF INTEREST
★ The effective rate is found by taking the interest made with compounding and putting it into the simple interest formula, then solving for r.
⚙ For the P500 example: $I = Prt$ becomes $85.83 = 500 × r × 2$.
⚙ $85.83 = 1000r$, so $r = 0.08583 = 8.583\%$.
EASIER: The bank said 8%, but because of compounding you really earned as much as a simple 8.583% would give.

SIMPLE VERSUS COMPOUND INTEREST
P1000 at 7% per year, both kinds side by side.
⚙ Year 0: simple 1,000.00 and compound 1,000.00
⚙ Year 1: simple 1,070.00 and compound 1,070.00 (the same after one year)
⚙ Year 2: simple 1,140.00 and compound 1,144.90
⚙ Year 3: simple 1,210.00 and compound 1,225.04
⚙ Year 7: simple 1,490.00 and compound 1,605.78
⚙ Year 23: simple 2,610.00 and compound 4,740.53
⚙ Year 30: simple 3,100.00 and compound 7,612.26
★ Simple interest grows in a straight line, by P70 each year. Compound interest grows faster and faster, like a curve bending upward.
EASIER: Simple interest after 30 years: $1000(1 + 0.07 × 30) = 3100$. Compound: $1000(1.07)^{30} = 7612.26$. Same rate, more than double the money.
★ If you are earning, choose compound interest. If you are paying, simple interest costs less.
★ Time is on your side: the longer you save, the greater the effect of compound interest. But the longer you borrow, the quicker your debts grow.
"Money makes money, and the money that money makes, makes more money." (Benjamin Franklin)

LOANS IN DAILY LIFE
⚙ Common loans: home loans, education loans, car loans, and business loans.
⚙ Installment buying is paying for something in parts over time, which usually means paying interest.
★ A loan's total cost is higher than the principal because the borrower also pays interest for using the lender's money.

EXIT TICKET ANSWERS
Q: Which formula should be used for P15,000 at 4% compounded quarterly for 2 years? Why?
★ The compound interest formula, because the interest is compounded quarterly. $A = 15000(1.01)^{8} = 16242.85$.
Q: In one sentence, why can a loan's total cost be higher than the principal?
★ Because the borrower pays interest on top of the principal for using the lender's money.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Using 6 instead of 0.06 for 6%. Always write the rate as a decimal.
⚙ Using months as years. 4 months is $\frac{4}{12}$ of a year, not 4.
⚙ Forgetting to divide r by n in compound interest.
⚙ Using t instead of nt as the exponent.
⚙ Giving A when the question asks for the interest. Interest is $A - P$.
⚙ Rounding $1 + \frac{r}{n}$ too early. Keep all the digits until the end.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ First decide: simple or compound? "Compounded" means use $A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$.
⚙ Write down P, r, n, and t before you substitute.
⚙ Check that more compounding periods gave a bigger answer.

SELF-TEST
Q: Find the simple interest on P5000 at 5% for 3 years.
★ $I = 5000 × 0.05 × 3 = 750$, so the interest is P750.
Q: Find A for P2000 at 6% compounded semi-annually for 3 years.
★ $\frac{0.06}{2} = 0.03$ and $nt = 6$, so $A = 2000(1.03)^{6} = 2388.10$.
Q: What is n for quarterly compounding?
★ $n = 4$.
