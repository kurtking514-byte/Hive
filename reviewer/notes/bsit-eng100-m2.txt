Module 2 Communication and Globalization
Local and global communication in multicultural settings

GLOBALIZATION

People can move freely from one country to another, communicate with people in any part of the world, move products quickly, and get information anytime and anywhere because we live in a globalized world.
★ Ledbetter (2015) states that globalization represents a new, post-traditional order, forging new identities, institutions, and ways of life.
★ Ledbetter (2015) calls globalization "the way we live now": a worldwide network of social relations, seemingly unfettered by the constraints of geography.
EASIER: Distance matters much less now; your friends, work, and news can come from anywhere in the world.
★ Globalization has increased the economic, political, and cultural interdependence of diverse cultures.
EASIER: Interdependence means countries rely on each other, like neighbors who borrow from and help one another.
★ Globalization is related to two other concepts: diversity and glocalization.
★ Diversity is the recognition and valuing of difference, encompassing factors such as age, ethnicity, ability, religion, education, marital status, sexual orientation, and income.
EASIER: Diversity means noticing that people are different and treating those differences as valuable.
★ Glocalization is a newer concept describing how globalization affects and merges with local interests and environments.
EASIER: Glocalization is global plus local: something worldwide blending with a local place.
Diversity is about differences between people; glocalization is about global things merging with local interests.

CAUSES OF GLOBALIZATION

★ Garret (2000) names three causes of globalization.
⚙ Cause 1: technological innovations lowering the costs of moving goods and, more notably, information around the world.
⚙ Cause 2: growing international economic activity.
⚙ Cause 3: the liberalization of foreign economic policies.
EASIER: Cheaper technology, more international business, and looser rules on foreign trade and investment pushed the world closer together.
EASIER: Liberalization means a government loosens its rules and restrictions.

ECONOMIC GLOBALIZATION

★ In economic globalization, nations are economically interdependent.
Example: Filipino workers are found across the globe, and their income helps boost the Philippine economy.
EASIER: Money earned by Filipinos working abroad and sent home helps the economy here.
★ Communication between and among countries is essential to solve unemployment, poverty, unending consumerism, economic exploitation of workers in developing and developed countries, and deterioration of the environment.
★ The economic welfare of member nations of international organizations such as the WTO, World Bank, and IMF may also be controlled by these organizations.
EASIER: WTO is the World Trade Organization and IMF is the International Monetary Fund.
★ The growth of multinational companies such as Coca-Cola, Nike, and McDonald's makes products available in almost all countries.
EASIER: A multinational company operates in many countries, so you can buy the same soda or burger in Manila and in New York.

GLOBALIZATION OF GOVERNMENT POLICIES

★ Government policies of developed countries are also globalized.
★ Examples are privatization of state enterprises and trade liberalization.
EASIER: Privatization means businesses owned by the government are turned over to private owners.
★ Trade liberalization is the removal of world tariffs, which decreased trade barriers.
EASIER: A tariff is a tax on imported goods; removing it makes trade between countries easier and cheaper.
★ Liberalization of capital accounts allows people to invest overseas and allows foreign funds to be invested in the home country.
EASIER: Investment money can flow out of and into the country more freely.
Trade liberalization is about goods crossing borders; capital account liberalization is about investment money crossing borders.

GLOBALIZATION OF CULTURE

★ Globalized popular cultures include rock or pop music, television, K-dramas and programs, movies, fast food, dance, and sports.
★ Language is part of culture.
★ English has become the "world language" or the lingua franca of the modern world.
EASIER: A lingua franca is a shared language that people with different first languages use to talk to each other.
★ As English dominates, second-language and foreign speakers of English have developed their own varieties of English, and the study of World Englishes emerged.
Added example: Philippine English and Indian English are varieties studied under World Englishes.
★ Globalization has resulted in diversity in the workplace, hence the need for effective communication skills in a diverse work environment.
EASIER: Your future coworkers may come from many countries, so you must be able to communicate clearly with all of them.

GLOBALIZATION AND BUSINESS COMMUNICATION

★ Language is part of culture and is closely bound to the principles, rules, and values formed in a given society.
★ As society becomes more globally connected, the ability to communicate across cultural boundaries has gained increasing prominence.
★ Global businesses must understand how to communicate with employees and customers from different cultures to fulfil the organization's mission and build value for stakeholders.
★ Technology has had a profound impact on how businesses communicate globally and market their products and services.
★ Even with advanced technology, organizations must stay discerning of cultural nuances that can present obstacles to increasing profits and market share.
EASIER: A nuance is a small, subtle difference; missing one can offend customers and cost sales.
★ Genevieve Hilton: cultural proficiency does not mean memorizing every cultural nuance of every market; it is knowing when to listen, when to ask for help, and when, finally, to speak.
EASIER: Being culturally skilled is less about memorizing facts and more about listening first and speaking last.
★ Culture can affect how we perceive the actions of others, and our perception directly affects how we interpret their behaviour and actions.
★ Effective cross-cultural communication requires basing perceptions on facts, not merely on personal biases and prejudices.
★ Successful business leaders must balance organizational objectives with external global challenges.
★ As organizations become more interconnected, the role of leaders in managing global teams becomes increasingly important.
★ Navigating different cultural nuances is a key skill for global leaders (Matthews and Thakkar, 2012).

CROSS-CULTURAL COMMUNICATION

★ Cross-cultural communication is communication between people who differ in any one of the following: styles of working, age, nationality, ethnicity, race, gender, sexual orientation, and so on.
★ It also refers to attempts to exchange, negotiate, and mediate cultural differences by means of language, gestures, and body language.
EASIER: It is simply how people from different cultures communicate with each other.
★ Each individual can practice culture at varying levels: the culture of the community he grows up in, the work culture at his workplace, and other cultures he actively joins or slowly withdraws from.
★ An individual is constantly confronted with the clash between his original culture and the majority culture he is exposed to daily.
★ Cultural clashes occur as a result of individuals believing their culture is better than others.
Added example: A student from a province who studies in a big city lives with home culture, school culture, and city culture at the same time.
★ Cross-cultural communication is necessary to avoid misunderstandings that can lead to conflicts between individuals or groups.
★ Cross-cultural communication creates a feeling of trust and enables cooperation.
★ Its focus is on providing the right response rather than providing the right message.
EASIER: What matters most is reacting well to the other person, not just delivering a perfect speech.

DIALOGUE IN CROSS-CULTURAL COMMUNICATION

★ Successful cross-cultural communication creates a dialogue, a continuous transfer of information.
⚙ The exchange of information addresses our assumptions.
⚙ It clarifies points we do not understand.
⚙ It gives the opportunity to ask questions and confirm the information received.
★ Having a dialogue reduces conflict because cultural misunderstandings can be dealt with when they arise.
★ Dialogue only occurs when both parties agree to share information and ensure that the transfer of information is not blocked.
EASIER: Dialogue is a two-way street; if one side shuts down, there is no dialogue.

LOCAL COMMUNICATION

★ Communities develop their own ways of exchanging information, conversation, ideas, or messages with other people using words, signs, writings, and verbal or non-verbal means.
★ Communities use their own terms depending on their environment and social context.
★ People identify themselves with a group or community whose members share the same language and culture.
Added example: Friends in Cebu chatting in Cebuano with local expressions that outsiders may not understand.

GLOBAL COMMUNICATION

★ People engage in global communication as they become more interdependent and as technology greatly advances.
★ Mattelart (2014): communication globalizes but it also remains local.
⚙ Role 1: global communication is a crucial source for our perceptions of the world and our sense of belonging to this world.
⚙ Role 2: global communication is a key player in the global economy.
⚙ Role 3: global communication is essential to global politics.
⚙ Role 4: military operations depend upon global communication.
⚙ Role 5: global communication is a carrier of cultural expressions.
EASIER: Memory trick for the five roles: Perception, Economy, Politics, Military, Culture.

GLOCAL COMMUNICATION

★ According to Mattelart (2014), global and local belong together; we do not live in the globe but in specific locations.
★ However cosmopolitan one may be, one's identity is primarily defined by locality: the locus of birth, family, language, and jokes.
EASIER: Cosmopolitan means at home in many parts of the world; even well-traveled people still feel rooted in where they came from.
★ Cultural proximity is the attachment to the place where you experience the greatest cultural comfort; it is an essential experience.
★ We are global and local citizens, so our communication could best be termed "glocal".
★ Glocal connects the global (for example, a product for global marketing) with the local (for example, local tastes and experiences).
★ Glocalization in business is communication that conveys the essential core message and spirit of a brand globally while integrating, at the local level, the particularities of regional markets.
Added example: A global fast-food brand keeps the same logo and slogan everywhere but adds rice meals to its menu in the Philippines.
Local communication stays within one community's language and culture; global communication crosses borders; glocal communication keeps a global core message but adapts it to local tastes.

INTERCULTURAL COMMUNICATION

★ Intercultural communication is the exchange of information between individuals who are unalike culturally.
★ It is essential because of our increasing exposure to people of other cultures and co-cultures.
EASIER: A co-culture is a smaller group with its own ways of living inside a larger culture.
★ Exposure comes through vacation travel, transnational jobs, international conflicts, military and humanitarian service, and the presence of immigrants, refugees, and new citizens.
★ Intercultural communication includes better understanding of cultural and co-cultural friends and enemies (Pearson, Nelson, Titsworth, and Harter, 2011).
★ Being an effective communicator means interacting positively with people from various racial, ethnic, and cultural backgrounds.
★ You can communicate better with people from other cultures if you know something about theirs.

HUMAN UNIQUENESS AND CULTURE

★ According to science, each person is genetically unique, and individual experiences make this uniqueness even more heightened.
★ Factors that shape human identities: family background, religious affiliations, educational achievements, sociocultural forces, economic conditions, emotional states, and other factors.
★ Because of this, no two people can ever be exactly the same.
★ Culture is the ever-changing values, traditions, social and political relationships, and worldview created and shared by a group of people bound together by a combination of factors.
★ These factors can include a common history, geographic location, language, social class, and/or religion.
EASIER: Culture is a group's shared way of life, and it keeps changing over time.

INTERCULTURAL COMMUNICATION PROBLEMS

★ Intercultural communication is subject to all the problems that can hamper effective interpersonal communication.
★ Ethnocentrism is the belief that your own group or culture is superior to all other groups or cultures.
★ In ethnocentrism, you use your own culture as the measure that others are expected to meet.
★ Cultural relativism is the belief that another culture should be judged by its own context rather than measured against your culture.
EASIER: Ethnocentrism says "my way is the standard"; cultural relativism says "judge their way by their own setting".
★ To communicate effectively, you need to accept people whose values and norms may differ from your own; an effective communicator avoids ethnocentrism and embraces cultural relativism.
★ Stereotyping is a perceptual trap as dangerous as ethnocentrism.
★ Rogers and Steinfatt (1999) define a stereotype as "a generalization about some group of people that oversimplifies their culture."
★ Example: the stereotype of a gay male is an effeminate fellow, but gay people are just as likely to be truckers, physicians, and athletes.
★ Stereotyping sometimes occurs because people have had a negative or positive experience with a person from another culture or co-culture.
★ In one investigation, people stereotyped black people after only one observation of a negative behaviour; in another, simply hearing about an alleged crime was enough (Henderson-King and Nisbett, 1996).
★ People are willing to stereotype with little evidence.
★ Prejudice is a negative attitude toward a group of people just because they are who they are.
★ Groups receiving prejudice are often marginalized groups: people in poverty, people of colour, people who speak a language other than English, gay men, and lesbian women.
★ Sometimes the group receiving the prejudice is actually larger than the group that exhibits the prejudice.
Ethnocentrism is thinking your culture is better than others; stereotyping is acting as if all members of a group are alike; prejudice is a negative attitude toward a group just because of who they are.
EASIER: Memory trick: ethnocentrism is "we are better", stereotyping is "they are all the same", prejudice is "I dislike them".

CHARACTERISTICS OF DIFFERENT CULTURES

★ Five characteristics: individualistic versus collectivist, M-time versus P-time, high-context versus low-context, affective versus neutral, and non-verbal cultural differences.

INDIVIDUALISTIC VERSUS COLLECTIVIST CULTURE

★ Individualistic cultures value individual freedom, choice, uniqueness, and independence.
★ Individualistic cultures place "I" before "we" and value competition over cooperation, private property over public or state-owned property, personal behaviour over group behaviour, and individual opinion over what anyone else might think.
★ Collectivist cultures value the group over the individual and place "we" before "I".
★ Collectivist cultures value commitment to family, tribe, and clan; their people tend to be loyal to spouse, employer, community, and country.
★ Collectivist cultures value cooperation over competition, and group-defined social norms and duties over personal opinions.
★ An ancient Confucian saying captures collectivism: "If one wants to establish himself, he should help others to establish themselves first."
EASIER: Individualistic is "me first, stand out"; collectivist is "us first, help the group".

M-TIME VERSUS P-TIME

★ M-time, or monochronic time schedule, compartmentalizes time to meet personal needs, separates task and social dimensions, and points to the future (Ting-Toomey, 1997).
★ M-time is dominant in Canada, the United States, and Northern Europe.
★ M-time cultures see time as something that can be compartmentalized, wasted, or saved.
★ P-time, or polychronic time schedule, views time as contextually based and relationally oriented (Ting-Toomey, 1997, p. 395).
★ For P-time cultures, time is not saved or wasted; it is only one factor in a much larger and more complicated context.
EASIER: Memory trick: M is for monochronic and "mono" means one, so time is cut into separate blocks; P is for polychronic and "poly" means many, so time mixes with people and situations.

HIGH-CONTEXT VERSUS LOW-CONTEXT

★ All international communication is influenced by cultural differences; even the choice of communication medium can have cultural overtones.
★ According to Goman (2011), the determining factor may not be the degree of industrialization but whether the country is a high-context or low-context culture.
★ High-context cultures leave much of the message unspecified, to be understood through context, nonverbal cues, and between-the-lines interpretation of what is actually said.
★ Low-context cultures expect messages to be explicit and specific.
EASIER: High-context means "read between the lines"; low-context means "say exactly what you mean".

AFFECTIVE VERSUS NEUTRAL

★ In international business practices, reason and emotion both play a role; which dominates depends on whether people are affective or emotionally neutral.
★ Affective means readily showing emotions.
★ In cultures with high affect, people show their feelings plainly by laughing, smiling, grimacing, scowling, and sometimes crying, shouting, or walking out of the room.
★ Members of neutral cultures do not telegraph their feelings but keep them carefully controlled and subdued.
EASIER: To telegraph feelings means to show them openly; neutral people keep a calm, unreadable face.

NON-VERBAL CULTURAL DIFFERENCES

★ Knowledge of nonverbal communication is invaluable because it helps us be conscious of messages we may send in face-to-face settings.
★ Benny Lewis is a polyglot who has lived in more than 23 countries and seriously invested time in studying their languages and cultures.
EASIER: A polyglot is a person who speaks many languages.
Added example: Nodding the head means yes in most countries, but in Bulgaria a nod can mean no.

TIPS FOR DIVERSE WORKPLACE AUDIENCES

⚙ Tip 1: Understand the value of differences.
⚙ Tip 2: Don't expect conformity.
⚙ Tip 3: Practice focused, thoughtful, and open-minded listening.
⚙ Tip 4: Invite, use, and give feedback.
⚙ Tip 5: Make fewer assumptions.
⚙ Tip 6: Learn about your cultural self.
⚙ Tip 7: Learn about other cultures and identity groups.
⚙ Tip 8: Seek common grounds.
EASIER: Conformity means making everyone think and act the same way; a diverse workplace should not demand it.

STRATEGIES FOR IMPROVING INTERCULTURAL COMMUNICATION

Having strategies in advance prepares you for new situations with people from other cultures and co-cultures and increases your confidence in communicating with a variety of people.
⚙ Strategy 1: Conduct a personal self-assessment; ask how your own attitudes toward different cultures and co-cultures influence your communication with them.
⚙ Strategy 2: Practice supportive communication behaviors; supportive behaviors such as empathy encourage success in intercultural exchanges, while defensive behaviors tend to hamper effectiveness.
⚙ Strategy 3: Develop sensitivity toward diversity; you can learn something from all people, so take time to learn about other cultures and co-cultures before a communication situation.
⚙ Strategy 4: Avoid stereotypes; avoid making assumptions about another's culture and get to know individuals for themselves.
⚙ Strategy 5: Avoid ethnocentrism; familiarity does not make your culture superior, and learning about other cultures teaches you the strengths and weaknesses of your own.
⚙ Strategy 6: Develop code sensitivity.
★ Code sensitivity is the ability to use the verbal and nonverbal language appropriate to the cultural or co-cultural norms of the individual with whom you are communicating.
EASIER: The more you know about another's culture, the better you will be at adapting your words and gestures.
⚙ Strategy 7: Seek shared codes; be open-minded about differences while you determine which communication style to adopt.
⚙ Strategy 8: Use and encourage descriptive feedback; effective feedback encourages adaptation, and both participants should accept feedback and show supportive behaviours.
★ Feedback should be immediate, honest, specific, and clear.
⚙ Strategy 9: Open communication channels; intercultural communication can be frustrating, so be patient as you seek mutual understanding.
⚙ Strategy 10: Manage conflicting beliefs and practices; think ahead about how you might handle minor and major differences, from everyday behaviour to seriously different practices like punishments, realities, and beliefs.
Code sensitivity is adapting to the other person's language norms; shared codes are a common communication style both sides can use.

BIAS-FREE LANGUAGE

★ Language plays an important role in achieving more effective communication, and using appropriate terms avoids conflicts and misunderstanding.
★ Gender-biased terms: female doctor, woman attorney, cleaning woman.
★ Bias-free terms: doctor, attorney, cleaner.
Added example: chairman becomes chairperson, policeman becomes police officer, and mankind becomes humankind.

COMMON MISTAKES

★ Diversity is not glocalization: diversity values differences between people, while glocalization is globalization merging with local interests.
★ Ethnocentrism, stereotyping, and prejudice are three different traps; do not use them interchangeably.
★ Cultural relativism is the opposite attitude of ethnocentrism, not a type of it.
★ P-time is polychronic and M-time is monochronic.
★ High-context messages are implied; low-context messages are explicit; do not reverse them.
★ Affective cultures show emotion openly; neutral cultures control and subdue it.

★ Ethnocentrism judges other cultures by your own; cultural relativism judges a culture by its own context.
Q: Which time schedule sees time as something that can be compartmentalized, wasted, or saved?
★ M-time, the monochronic time schedule.
Q: What is code sensitivity?
★ The ability to use verbal and nonverbal language appropriate to the cultural or co-cultural norms of the person you are communicating with.

★ Technological innovations lowering the costs of moving goods and information, growing international economic activity, and liberalization of foreign economic policies.
