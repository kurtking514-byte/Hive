WARM-UP: ONE TOPIC, THREE PURPOSES
Imagine convincing your friends to eat healthy:
⚙ Telling them facts about vegetables is informing.
⚙ Making them feel inspired is persuading.
⚙ Proving they are harming their bodies is arguing.

INFORMATIVE COMMUNICATION
★ Informative communication: communication intended to provide knowledge, explain concepts, describe events, or present factual information to an audience.
⚙ Its primary purpose is to increase the audience's understanding of a subject, rather than directly convince them to accept a position.
Common forms:
⚙ News reports
⚙ Academic presentations
⚙ Research reports
⚙ Educational lectures
⚙ Documentary presentations
⚙ Instructions and informational announcements
EXAMPLE: INFORMATIVE SAMPLE
⚙ "According to the Department of Health, maintaining proper hand hygiene helps prevent the spread of infectious diseases. Regular handwashing with soap and water is one of the simplest ways to protect ourselves and others."
★ It is informative because it states facts from a source and does not push the audience to take a side.

PERSUASIVE COMMUNICATION
★ Persuasive communication: communication intended to influence the audience's beliefs, attitudes, opinions, or behavior.
⚙ Unlike informative communication, which emphasizes understanding, persuasive communication encourages the audience to consider a viewpoint or take action.
Common forms:
⚙ Advertisements
⚙ Campaign speeches
⚙ Product promotions
⚙ Advocacy campaigns
⚙ Motivational speeches
⚙ Fundraising appeals
EXAMPLE: PERSUASIVE SAMPLE
⚙ "Instead of spending your free time scrolling through social media, consider reading a book. Reading improves vocabulary, strengthens comprehension, and develops critical thinking. Start reading today!"
★ It is persuasive because it urges the audience to change a behavior and ends with a call to action ("Start reading today!").

THE THREE PERSUASIVE APPEALS
★ Ethos (appeal to credibility): establishes the speaker's trustworthiness, expertise, or authority.
⚙ Example: "As a licensed environmental scientist, I have studied the effects of improper waste disposal for several years."
★ Pathos (appeal to emotion): connects with the audience's feelings, values, and experiences.
⚙ Example: "Imagine a community where children cannot safely play because the surroundings are filled with garbage."
★ Logos (appeal to logic): uses facts, statistics, examples, and logical reasoning.
⚙ Example: "A waste segregation program can help communities reduce the amount of garbage sent to landfills."
EASIER: Ethos is "trust me", pathos is "feel this", logos is "here is the proof".
Difference: "As a doctor..." is ethos (who is speaking). "Imagine your child sick..." is pathos (feelings). "Studies show 80 percent..." is logos (facts).

EXAMPLE: KEEP OUR CAMPUS CLEAN
A sample persuasive essay that uses all three appeals and ends with a call to action.
⚙ Logos: "A clean school environment is everyone's responsibility... proper waste disposal helps protect our health and the environment."
⚙ Ethos: "As an environmental advocate who has participated in community clean-up activities, I believe that small actions can create meaningful changes."
⚙ Pathos: "Imagine walking into a school surrounded by garbage, unpleasant smells, and polluted surroundings. Would you feel comfortable studying in such an environment?"
⚙ Call to action: "Let us start practicing proper waste segregation, avoid littering, and encourage others to do the same."
★ Call to action: the closing part of a persuasive message that tells the audience exactly what to do.

ARGUMENTATIVE COMMUNICATION
★ Argumentative communication: communication in which a speaker or writer presents a position on a debatable issue and supports it through logical reasoning and credible evidence.
⚙ Its purpose is to establish the validity of a claim, defend a position, examine opposing perspectives, and show why the argument is reasonable.
EXAMPLE: ARGUMENTATIVE SAMPLES
⚙ Mental health: "Universities should provide more mental health support services for students. Academic pressure can negatively affect students' well-being, and accessible counseling services can help address these concerns. Therefore, investing in student mental health services should be an institutional priority."
⚙ Topic "Should College Attendance Be Mandatory?": the speaker claims attendance policies should allow reasonable flexibility, gives reasons (students balance work or family duties), admits the other side ("Although regular attendance encourages participation..."), then concludes that universities should set flexible policies while keeping academic standards.
★ They are argumentative because they state a position on a debatable issue, give reasons, address the opposing view, and reach a conclusion ("Therefore...").
Difference: persuasive communication leans on emotion and calls the audience to act. Argumentative communication leans on reasons and evidence, and it examines the opposing side.
Difference: informative communication explains; it does not take a side.

PUBLIC SPEAKING
★ Public speaking: a type of oral communication viewed as a form of art because it involves styles and techniques in facing a group of people or an audience.

SEVEN ELEMENTS OF SPEECH DELIVERY
⚙ Element 1: Voice and articulation.
⚙ Element 2: Gestures.
⚙ Element 3: Posture and attire.
⚙ Element 4: Body movement.
⚙ Element 5: Facial expressions.
⚙ Element 6: Eye contact.
⚙ Element 7: Diction.
Added example: diction is the choice and pronunciation of words; articulation is how clearly the sounds are formed.

EXTEMPORANEOUS SPEECH
★ Extemporaneous speech: a speech that has been carefully planned and researched.
Four guidelines:
⚙ Guideline 1: Develop a positive mental attitude.
⚙ Guideline 2: Be well-prepared.
⚙ Guideline 3: Practice your speech.
⚙ Guideline 4: Get enough sleep.

IMPROMPTU SPEECH
★ Impromptu speech: a speech presented without any advance preparation or planning. It is also called "spur of the moment".
Five guidelines:
⚙ Guideline 1: Listen actively.
⚙ Guideline 2: Prepare to be called.
⚙ Guideline 3: Don't worry about small mistakes.
⚙ Guideline 4: Use basic principles of speech organization.
⚙ Guideline 5: Speak briefly and concisely.
Difference: extemporaneous is planned and researched. Impromptu has no advance preparation.

IMPROMPTU SPEECH TEMPLATES
★ Point, Reason, Example, Point (PREP): state your point, give a reason, give an example, then restate your point.
⚙ Point: My favorite hobby is photography.
⚙ Reason: it is a powerful form of self-expression and a way to capture meaningful moments.
⚙ Example: a road trip last summer where I photographed a sunset over the mountains.
⚙ Point: photography lets me see the world through a different lens and appreciate everyday beauty.
★ Past, Present, Future: describe what happened before, what is true now, and what you plan next.
⚙ Past: in sophomore year I struggled with time management.
⚙ Present: I now use a planner, prioritize, and break projects into steps.
⚙ Future: I plan to keep refining these skills and build lifelong learning habits.
★ Cause, Effect, Remedy: explain why a problem happens, what it leads to, and how to fix it.
⚙ Cause: procrastination, driven by heavy workloads, poor time management, and lack of motivation.
⚙ Effect: stress, lower-quality work, missed deadlines, and poor performance.
⚙ Remedy: the Pomodoro Technique, breaking tasks into smaller parts, and setting realistic goals.
EXAMPLE: MISS UNIVERSE Q AND A
⚙ The class video shows a Miss Universe question and answer as an impromptu speech: the candidate answers a question she has not seen before, live on stage.
⚙ The question shown: "What is the most important lesson you've learned in your life and how would you apply it to your time as Miss Universe?"
Added example: the candidate is Catriona Gray of the Philippines, who won Miss Universe 2018.

PERSUASIVE SPEECH
★ Persuasive speech: its purpose is to convince your audience to agree with an idea or opinion that you present.
⚙ Step 1: Choose a side on a controversial topic.
⚙ Step 2: Write a speech to explain your position.
⚙ Step 3: Convince the audience to agree with you.
⚙ Class video: "Stop Body-Shaming", an Ignite talk by Vanndy ChanRachna, urging the audience to stop judging people's bodies ("My body, my choice").

ARGUMENTATIVE SPEECH
★ Argumentative speech: a speech that aims to secure the agreement of the audience. The speaker uses arguments, proofs, evidence, facts, and statistics.
⚙ Its objective is to win the audience and provide a proposition with reasons and evidence.
⚙ Class video: "Why should we reimpose the Death Penalty?" by Gwynn Arabilla, on whether the death penalty should be reinstated in the Philippines.
How the video builds its argument:
⚙ States a position: the death penalty should be reimposed for heinous crimes such as murder, rape, kidnapping, and arson.
⚙ Acknowledges opposing views: no justice system is perfect, and an innocent person might be executed.
⚙ Rebuts with evidence: DNA analysis, which the speaker says is roughly 95 percent accurate, makes wrongful execution unlikely.
⚙ Uses a real case: Cho Doo-soon, a Korean offender released on December 12, 2020, to show that jail does not always reform criminals.
⚙ Gives reasons: retribution (punishment that reflects the crime) and deterrence (discouraging others from committing violent acts).
⚙ Concludes by restating the position.
Difference: an argumentative speech states the opposing view and answers it with evidence. A persuasive speech mainly moves the audience to agree or act.

HUMOROUS OR ENTERTAINING SPEECH
★ Humorous or entertaining speech: a speech intended to amuse and share enjoyment with listeners, often blending humor with a lighthearted message.
⚙ Examples: an after-dinner speech or a stand-up comedy routine.
⚙ Class video: "Making Fire", a performer who demonstrates making fire by rubbing sticks while joking with the audience.

FOUR PURPOSES OF SPEECHES
⚙ To inform: informative speech.
⚙ To persuade: persuasive speech.
⚙ To argue: argumentative speech.
⚙ To entertain: humorous or entertaining speech.

COMMON MISTAKES
⚙ Calling a factual news report persuasive. If it only explains facts, it is informative.
⚙ Mixing up ethos and logos. Credentials ("As a licensed scientist...") are ethos; facts and statistics are logos.
⚙ Treating persuasive and argumentative as the same. Argumentative examines opposing views and relies on evidence.
⚙ Confusing extemporaneous (planned and researched) with impromptu (no preparation).
⚙ Forgetting the order of PREP: Point, Reason, Example, Point.
⚙ Listing diction and articulation as the same element. Voice and articulation is element 1; diction is element 7.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Be ready to classify a sample passage as informative, persuasive, or argumentative.
⚙ Be ready to label a sentence as ethos, pathos, or logos.
⚙ Memorize the 7 elements of speech delivery, the 4 extemporaneous guidelines, and the 5 impromptu guidelines.
⚙ Know the three impromptu templates: PREP; Past, Present, Future; Cause, Effect, Remedy.
