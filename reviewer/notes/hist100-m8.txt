Module 8 Other Issues in Philippine History
Peace treaties with Muslim Filipinos, key issues in education, and issues in Philippine geography (summarized)

HOW TO ANALYZE AN ISSUE
⚙ Context: what was happening?
⚙ Root causes: what long-term conditions produced the issue?
⚙ Actors: who had power and who was affected?
⚙ Evidence: treaties, laws, events, data, reports, maps.
⚙ Consequences: what changed and what continued?
⚙ Communication: how can it be explained clearly?

PART 1: PEACE TREATIES WITH MUSLIM FILIPINOS
★ Muslim Filipinos: Filipino citizens who are Muslims; diverse in ethnicity, language, region, and politics.
★ Moro: a colonial term for Muslim peoples of Mindanao and Sulu, later reclaimed as a marker of identity and resistance.
★ Bangsamoro: a collective identity and political community tied to self-determination and self-government.
⚙ Mindanao is not uniform: Indigenous peoples and settler communities also live there.
⚙ Main actors: the government, the MNLF (Moro National Liberation Front), the MILF (Moro Islamic Liberation Front), communities, and international facilitators.

ROOT CAUSES OF THE MINDANAO CONFLICT
⚙ Identity: recognition of a distinct history, religion, and culture.
⚙ Territory: ancestral homeland and land disputes.
⚙ Governance: demand for real self-rule.
⚙ Marginalization: poverty and uneven development.
⚙ Security: armed conflict, private armies, displacement.
⚙ Trust: broken agreements and competing memories of injustice.
★ The conflict cannot be explained by religion alone. Its roots are political, territorial, economic, historical, and cultural.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
⚙ Before colonization: the Sulu and Maguindanao sultanates had their own law, trade, and diplomacy.
⚙ Spanish period: Spain never fully controlled Mindanao and Sulu (the Moro Wars).
⚙ American period: land titling and settlement programs brought migrants; ancestral land claims were disadvantaged.
⚙ Postwar: poverty and land conflict; the Jabidah Massacre became a major symbol in Moro political consciousness.
⚙ Marcos era: the MNLF became the major armed movement; the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) became involved.
⚙ 1987 Constitution: recognized autonomous regions in Muslim Mindanao and the Cordilleras, within Philippine sovereignty.

PEACE AGREEMENT TIMELINE
⚙ 1976 Tripoli Agreement: government and MNLF; made autonomy the center of the peace framework.
⚙ 1987 Jeddah Accord: reopened talks with the MNLF after EDSA.
⚙ 1996 Final Peace Agreement: implemented the Tripoli framework; created the Southern Philippines Council for Peace and Development.
⚙ 2008 MOA-AD controversy: the Memorandum of Agreement on Ancestral Domain, which proposed a Bangsamoro Juridical Entity; it was challenged at the Supreme Court over territory, consultation, and constitutional limits.
⚙ 2012 Framework Agreement on the Bangsamoro (FAB): proposed a new Bangsamoro political entity.
⚙ 2014 Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro (CAB): combined the agreements on powers, wealth sharing, normalization, and transition.
⚙ 2018 to present: the Bangsamoro Organic Law and BARMM.
★ Lesson from Tripoli: signing an agreement is easier than building institutions people trust.
★ Lesson from MOA-AD: peace agreements need sound legal design, consultation, and constitutional compatibility.

ARMM AND BARMM
★ ARMM: the Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao, an autonomy experiment.
⚙ RA 6734 (1989): the Organic Act that established ARMM.
⚙ RA 9054 (2001): strengthened and expanded ARMM.
⚙ ARMM struggled with poverty, capacity, conflict, and legitimacy, which led to calls for a new arrangement.
★ RA 11054, the Bangsamoro Organic Law (BOL): created BARMM (Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao), which replaced ARMM.
⚙ The Bangsamoro Transition Authority governs during the transition.
⚙ RA 12317 reset the first regular BARMM parliamentary election to the second Monday of September 2026.
★ Normalization: moving from armed life to civilian life, including decommissioning of combatants, socio-economic help, transitional justice, and healing.
⚙ Continuing challenges: inclusive governance, security, development, justice, trust, and political transition.

PART 2: KEY ISSUES IN PHILIPPINE EDUCATION
⚙ Spanish period: schooling was limited, unequal, and tied to religion.
★ Education Decree of 1863: a milestone that organized public primary schools and teacher preparation under Spain.
⚙ The Propaganda Movement used education and writing to challenge colonial rule.
⚙ American period: mass public schooling expanded, with English as the medium of instruction.
★ Act No. 74: centralized the public-school system under the Americans.
★ Thomasites: American teachers who became the symbol of the new public-school system.
⚙ Japanese occupation: curriculum redirected to "Asia for Asians" themes; schooling disrupted.
⚙ Postwar: education expanded as a path to social mobility, but quality struggled to keep up.
⚙ The 1987 Constitution affirms the right to quality education.

EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS
⚙ DepEd: basic education.
⚙ CHED: higher education.
⚙ TESDA: technical-vocational education and training.
⚙ LGUs: local facilities and support.
⚙ Families and communities: learner support and accountability.

EDUCATION LAWS AND REFORMS
⚙ RA 9155 (2001): Governance of Basic Education Act.
⚙ RA 10533 (2013): Enhanced Basic Education Act, or K to 12.
⚙ RA 10931 (2017): Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act (free tuition in public colleges).
⚙ RA 12028 (2024): ARAL Program, the Academic Recovery and Accessible Learning Program, for learning loss in reading, math, and science.
★ MATATAG curriculum: decongests the K to 10 curriculum and strengthens foundational learning (literacy, numeracy, values).
Difference: Access means learners can enroll and stay in school. Quality means they actually learn. Being in school is not the same as learning.

CURRENT EDUCATION ISSUES
⚙ Learning crisis: PISA 2022 showed Filipino 15-year-olds below the OECD average in math, reading, and science. This should not be used to blame learners; it reflects system problems.
⚙ Language of instruction: balancing English, Filipino, and mother tongues.
⚙ Teachers: heavy workload, non-teaching tasks, pay, and training.
⚙ Infrastructure: classroom, water, electricity, and internet shortages.
⚙ Digital divide and AI: unequal access to devices and connectivity.
⚙ Inclusion: learners with disabilities, Indigenous and Muslim learners, and remote learners. Equity means removing barriers, not treating everyone identically.
⚙ Skills mismatch: graduates not matching labor needs.
⚙ Disasters and heat: class suspensions and schools used as evacuation centers.

PART 3: ISSUES IN PHILIPPINE GEOGRAPHY
★ Maps are historical sources, not just pictures. They reflect who made them and for what purpose.
TERRITORY TIMELINE
⚙ 1898 Treaty of Paris: Spain ceded the Philippines to the United States.
⚙ 1900 Treaty of Washington: added islands not covered in 1898.
⚙ 1930 boundary arrangements: clarified southern maritime boundaries.
⚙ 1961 and 2009 baselines laws (RA 3046 and RA 9522): defined and updated archipelagic baselines.
⚙ 2024 maritime laws: updated maritime zones and sea lanes.
★ Archipelagic doctrine: the islands and the waters connecting them are treated as one geographic, political, and legal unit.
★ UNCLOS: the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, which defines the territorial sea, EEZ, continental shelf, and navigation rights.
★ The Philippines has 7,641 islands.

MARITIME ZONES
★ Territorial sea: next to the baselines; the state has sovereignty.
★ Contiguous zone: where customs, fiscal, immigration, and sanitary laws can be enforced.
★ Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ): sovereign rights over resources, but not the same as full land territory.
★ Continental shelf: rights over the seabed and subsoil.
⚙ RA 9522: updated archipelagic baselines in line with UNCLOS.
⚙ RA 12064: the Philippine Maritime Zones Act.
⚙ RA 12065: the Philippine Archipelagic Sea Lanes Act.
⚙ The West Philippine Sea involves sovereignty, fisheries, energy, and security; use evidence, not slogans.

OTHER GEOGRAPHY ISSUES
⚙ Uneven development: center-periphery gaps between Manila and remote areas.
⚙ Transport and connectivity: islands make transport, internet, and services costly.
⚙ Urban primacy: Metro Manila concentrates jobs and services, causing congestion and flooding.
★ A hazard is not automatically a disaster. Disaster happens when exposure combines with vulnerability, poverty, and weak planning.
⚙ Climate change: heat, extreme rain, sea-level rise.
⚙ Resource conflicts: mining, forests, fisheries, and Indigenous ancestral domains.
⚙ Food security depends on climate, land, transport, and markets.
⚙ Migration and the diaspora: internal migration and overseas work.
⚙ Borderlands: southern links with Borneo, Indonesia, and Malaysia through the Sulu-Sulawesi seas.

COMMON ROOT CAUSES ACROSS ALL THREE ISSUES
⚙ Colonial legacies, centralization, inequality, governance capacity, recognition, and future risk.

COMMUNICATION TECHNIQUES
⚙ Cause-Effect: how earlier conditions led to a present issue.
⚙ Continuity-Change: what changed and what stayed (ARMM to BARMM; K to 12 to MATATAG).
⚙ Compare-Contrast: comparing agreements or reforms (Tripoli vs CAB; access vs quality).
⚙ Stakeholder Map: who decides, who benefits, who bears costs.
⚙ Issue Brief structure: Title, Background, Root Causes, Present Impact, Future Scenario, Recommendation.

EXAM TIPS
⚙ Peace timeline: Tripoli 1976, Jeddah 1987, FPA 1996, MOA-AD 2008, FAB 2012, CAB 2014, BOL 2018.
⚙ RA 6734 = ARMM; RA 11054 = Bangsamoro Organic Law (BARMM).
⚙ RA 10533 = K to 12; RA 12028 = ARAL; RA 10931 = free tertiary education; RA 9155 = basic education governance.
⚙ Treaty of Paris 1898; 7,641 islands; RA 12064 = Maritime Zones; RA 12065 = Sea Lanes.
