LESSON 1: DEFINITIONS OF PHYSICAL EDUCATION
★ Physical Education (PE) is a multidimensional discipline, far more than organized sports or exercise routines, that integrates movement science, pedagogy, health promotion, and character development.
EASIER: Multidimensional means many sides. Think of PE as a four-ingredient recipe: movement science (how the body moves), pedagogy (the art and science of teaching), health promotion (helping people live healthier), and character development (building values such as discipline).
★ Intentional: at its core, PE is the intentional study, practice, and appreciation of human movement as a vehicle for personal growth, social interaction, and lifelong wellness.
EASIER: Intentional means done on purpose and with a plan, not by accident. A vehicle is something that carries you somewhere. Movement is the vehicle, and growth, friendship, and lifelong health are the destinations.
EASIER: Running around at recess is plain activity. PE is planned learning through movement.

EXPANDED DIMENSIONS OF PE
★ The four expanded dimensions of PE are cognitive, psychomotor, affective, and social.
EASIER: Memory trick: head, hands, heart, and group. Cognitive is the head (thinking), psychomotor is the hands and body (moving), affective is the heart (feelings and values), and social is the group (people).
Cognitive dimension: PE cultivates understanding of anatomy, physiology, biomechanics, and health literacy. Students learn how the body works, how to prevent injuries, and how to make informed decisions about their physical well-being.
EASIER: Anatomy is the body parts. Physiology is how the body parts work. Biomechanics is how forces and levers act when the body moves. Health literacy is the ability to understand and use health information.
Psychomotor dimension: Through structured activities, learners develop motor skills, coordination, and physical competence, from basic locomotor movements to complex sport-specific techniques.
EASIER: Locomotor movements are movements that take you from place to place, such as walking, running, hopping, and skipping. Sport-specific techniques are skills used in one sport, such as a volleyball serve.
Affective dimension: PE nurtures values such as discipline, resilience, sportsmanship, and teamwork. It provides a space for emotional regulation, goal-setting, and self-confidence building.
EASIER: Affective means about feelings and values. Resilience is bouncing back after a setback. Sportsmanship is fair play and respect. Emotional regulation is managing your feelings.
Social dimension: Group activities and team sports foster collaboration, communication, and respect for diversity. PE becomes a microcosm of society where students learn to navigate roles, resolve conflicts, and lead with empathy.
EASIER: A microcosm is a small model of something bigger. A PE class is like a small society. Empathy is understanding how another person feels.
Added example: Learning why we warm up is cognitive. Hopping and skipping with control is psychomotor. Staying calm after losing a game is affective. Settling a disagreement with a teammate is social.

IMPORTANCE OF PE IN EDUCATION
★ PE is important in education because it supports holistic development, builds lifelong habits, promotes equity and inclusion, and aligns with global and national goals.
Supports Holistic Development: PE is a critical component of whole-child education. It complements academic subjects by promoting physical literacy, which is foundational to overall learning and development.
EASIER: Whole-child education develops body, mind, and character together. Physical literacy is having the skill, confidence, and knowledge to move well. Complements means completes, like a missing puzzle piece.
Builds Lifelong Habits: PE instills routines and mindsets that extend beyond the classroom, such as regular exercise, balanced nutrition, and stress management. These habits contribute to preventive health and quality of life.
EASIER: Preventive health means staying well so you do not get sick in the first place.
Promotes Equity and Inclusion: When designed thoughtfully, PE can be inclusive of all body types, abilities, and cultural backgrounds. It encourages participation and celebrates progress over perfection.
EASIER: Equity means fairness based on what each person needs. Inclusion means nobody is left out.
Aligns with Global and National Goals: PE supports Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly in health, education, and gender equality. Locally, it aligns with the Philippine Professional Standards for Teachers (PPST) and the Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF) by fostering 21st-century skills and civic responsibility.
EASIER: The SDGs are the global goals set by the United Nations for a better world by 2030. The PPST describes what good teachers should know and do. The PQF is the national ladder that describes what graduates at each level should know and be able to do. Civic responsibility means doing your part as a citizen.
Added example: Health matches SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-being), education matches SDG 4 (Quality Education), and gender equality matches SDG 5 (Gender Equality).

LESSON 2: LEGAL BASES OF PHYSICAL EDUCATION
Physical Education (PE) is not just a curricular requirement. It is a legal and constitutional mandate rooted in national and global frameworks that recognize movement, health, and physical literacy as fundamental human rights and educational priorities.
EASIER: A legal basis is a law or official document that gives PE its authority. A mandate is an official order. A framework is an organized set of rules and guidelines. Think of legal bases as the foundation under a house: they keep PE standing.

PHILIPPINE LEGAL FOUNDATIONS
★ The 1987 Philippine Constitution, Article XIV, Section 19: the State shall promote PE and encourage sports programs, league competitions, and amateur sports, including training for international competition, to foster self-discipline, teamwork, and excellence for the development of a healthy and alert citizenry.
EASIER: The Constitution is the highest law of the country. The State means the government. Amateur sports are played by people who are not paid professionals. Citizenry means the citizens.
★ Republic Act No. 5708 (1969), the Schools Physical Education and Sports Development Act, mandates an integrated PE and sports development program across all schools, emphasizes physical growth, social training, and personal discipline, and requires daily PE routines, competitive athletics, and intramural programs.
EASIER: A Republic Act (RA) is a law passed by Congress. Integrated means joined together as one program. Intramural programs are games among teams inside the same school.
★ RA 10533, the Enhanced Basic Education Act of 2013, strengthens PE within the K–12 curriculum, aligning it with global standards for health and movement across age groups.
EASIER: K–12 means kindergarten plus twelve years of basic education. Aligning means making it match.
★ CMO No. 39, s. 2021, the PATHFIT Guidelines: the implementation of the Physical Activity Towards Health and Fitness (PATHFIT) courses as the new standard for tertiary-level physical education in the Philippines.
EASIER: CMO means CHED Memorandum Order. CHED is the Commission on Higher Education. The s. 2021 means series of 2021. Tertiary level means college and university. This is the rule behind the course you are taking now.
EASIER: Memory trick by year: 1987 is the Constitution, 1969 is the schools law, 2013 is the K–12 law, and 2021 is the college PATHFIT memorandum.

GLOBAL LEGAL FOUNDATIONS
★ The UNESCO International Charter of Physical Education and Sport (1978) declares PE and sport a fundamental human right, equal in principle to food, shelter, and medical care, and calls for inclusive access and lifelong participation.
EASIER: UNESCO is the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization. A charter is a formal statement of principles. Inclusive access means open to everyone. Lifelong participation means staying active at every age.
★ The WHO Global Action Plan on Physical Activity (2018–2030) advocates national policies that promote physical activity across all age groups, emphasizing its role in preventing noncommunicable diseases and improving mental health.
EASIER: WHO is the World Health Organization. Noncommunicable diseases are illnesses that do not pass from person to person, such as heart disease and diabetes. The plan runs from 2018 to 2030.

IMPORTANCE OF THE LEGAL FOUNDATIONS IN PE
Legal frameworks do more than mandate PE. They legitimize its role, protect its integrity, and ensure its accessibility across diverse educational contexts.
EASIER: Legitimize means make official and respected. Integrity means kept honest and whole. Accessibility means everyone can reach it.
★ The six importance areas of legal foundations are institutional legitimacy and curriculum priority; equity, inclusion, and universal access; health promotion and public policy alignment; civic development and nation-building; accountability and quality assurance; and sustainability and policy continuity.
Institutional Legitimacy and Curriculum Priority: PE often competes with academic subjects for time and resources. Legal mandates, such as those in the Philippine Constitution and CHED memoranda, elevate PE from optional enrichment to essential curriculum.
This ensures PE is allocated sufficient instructional time.
This ensures PE is integrated into learning outcomes and assessment systems.
This ensures PE is supported by qualified personnel and facilities.
Without legal grounding, PE risks being sidelined or inconsistently implemented.
EASIER: Enrichment means a nice extra. Essential curriculum means a must-have subject. Sidelined means pushed aside.
Equity, Inclusion, and Universal Access: Legal foundations guarantee that all learners, regardless of gender, ability, socioeconomic status, or geographic location, have the right to participate in PE.
This supports inclusive design for learners with disabilities.
This supports gender-sensitive programming.
This supports culturally responsive movement practices.
This supports rural and urban parity in access to sports and wellness resources.
PE becomes a vehicle for social justice, not just physical development.
EASIER: Parity means equal. Rural means countryside. Urban means city. Social justice means fair treatment for everyone.
Health Promotion and Public Policy Alignment: PE is a frontline strategy in addressing national health challenges such as obesity, sedentary lifestyles, and mental health concerns.
Legal mandates align PE with preventive health goals, for example the WHO Global Action Plan on Physical Activity.
Legal mandates align PE with youth development policies.
Legal mandates align PE with national wellness campaigns.
This positions PE as a public health intervention, not merely a school-based activity.
EASIER: Sedentary means sitting most of the day. A public health intervention is an action that improves the health of a whole population.
Civic Development and Nation-Building: PE cultivates values like discipline, teamwork, and resilience, which are qualities essential for active citizenship.
Legal frameworks recognize PE's role in developing socially responsible and physically capable citizens.
Legal frameworks recognize PE's role in preparing youth for national and international competitions.
Legal frameworks recognize PE's role in promoting unity and pride through sports and movement.
PE becomes a platform for civic engagement and national identity.
EASIER: Civic means about being a citizen. National identity means the feeling of belonging to one country.
Accountability and Quality Assurance: Legal mandates provide benchmarks for curriculum standards (for example PATHFIT outcomes), teacher qualifications and professional development, and program evaluation and continuous improvement.
This ensures that PE is not only delivered but delivered well, with measurable impact.
EASIER: Accountability means someone is responsible for results. A benchmark is a standard to measure against, like a passing mark.
Sustainability and Policy Continuity: Legal foundations protect PE from being diluted by shifting political or institutional priorities.
They ensure long-term investment in facilities and programs.
They ensure continuity across administrations and leadership transitions.
They ensure integration into broader educational reforms (for example K–12, OBE, and PQF).
PE becomes a sustainable pillar of education, not a temporary initiative.
EASIER: Diluted means weakened. OBE means outcomes-based education, which focuses on what students can actually do at the end. Continuity means it keeps going even when leaders change.

LESSON 3: HEALTH VERSUS FITNESS
When we talk about living well, two words often come up: health and fitness. They are closely connected, but they are not the same.
Understanding the difference shows that being fit does not always mean being healthy, and being healthy does not always mean being fit.
The lesson explores how these concepts complement each other and why both are essential for lifelong wellness.
★ Health is a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease (World Health Organization, 1948).
EASIER: Health is not just not being sick. It also means your mind is well and your relationships are good.
★ Fitness is a set of attributes that relate to the ability to perform physical activities effectively.
EASIER: Fitness is what your body can do, such as run, lift, bend, and keep going.
★ Scope: health is holistic and includes emotional, mental, social, and physical well-being, while fitness is physical and focuses on performance and physiological capacity.
EASIER: Scope means how wide something is. Health is wide (the whole person). Fitness is narrower (the body's performance). Physiological capacity is what your body systems are able to do.
★ Measurement: health is often qualitative (for example mood, energy, absence of illness), while fitness is quantitative (for example VO2 max, strength tests, flexibility scores).
EASIER: Qualitative means described in words and quality. Quantitative means measured in numbers. VO2 max is the highest amount of oxygen your body can use during hard exercise, so it is a number for heart and lung fitness.
★ Goal: health aims at sustainable well-being and disease prevention, while fitness aims at enhanced physical performance and endurance.
★ Indicators: health shows in balanced nutrition, mental clarity, strong immunity, and social connectedness, while fitness shows in speed, strength, agility, cardiovascular endurance, and flexibility.
EASIER: Memory trick: health is how well your whole self is doing. Fitness is how well your body performs. Health is like the overall condition of a car. Fitness is like how strong and fast its engine is.
EASIER: Immunity is your body's defense against illness. Mental clarity is clear thinking.

INTERRELATIONSHIP OF HEALTH AND FITNESS
★ Health and fitness are interdependent but not synonymous, so a person can be fit but not healthy: a competitive athlete may have excellent strength and endurance but suffer from chronic stress, poor sleep, or disordered eating.
★ A person can be healthy but not fit: someone may have no illness and good mental health but lack stamina, flexibility, or muscular strength.
The ideal is integration, a lifestyle that supports both functional fitness and holistic health.
EASIER: Interdependent means each one depends on the other, like two legs. Synonymous means having the same meaning, which these two words do not. Chronic stress is stress that lasts a long time. Disordered eating means unhealthy eating patterns. Stamina is the ability to keep going without tiring quickly. Functional fitness means fitness that helps in daily life.

EDUCATIONAL IMPLICATIONS
Knowing the difference between health and fitness allows educators to design inclusive programs that prioritize well-being over competition.
It allows educators to promote lifelong habits like stress management, sleep hygiene, and balanced nutrition.
It allows educators to teach students to self-assess not just physical capabilities but also emotional and social wellness.
It allows educators to align with PATHFIT outcomes such as Active and Healthy Living and Advocacy and Action.
EASIER: Sleep hygiene means good sleep habits, such as a regular bedtime. Self-assess means to check yourself honestly.

HOW TO CHECK HEALTH VERSUS FITNESS FOR ONE PERSON
Added example: This is a simple way to apply the lesson to a real person.
⚙ Step 1: Check the fitness indicators: speed, strength, agility, cardiovascular endurance, and flexibility.
⚙ Step 2: Check the health indicators: balanced nutrition, mental clarity, strong immunity, and social connectedness.
⚙ Step 3: Compare the two results.
⚙ Step 4: Name the result: fit but not healthy, healthy but not fit, or both (the ideal integration).

EXAMPLE: FIT BUT NOT HEALTHY AND HEALTHY BUT NOT FIT
⚙ Added example, Person A: lifts heavy, runs fast, and has great endurance (strong fitness indicators), but sleeps four hours a night and is always stressed (weak health indicators). Result: fit but not healthy.
⚙ Added example, Person B: has no illness, a calm mind, good friends, and balanced meals (strong health indicators), but gets out of breath on one flight of stairs and cannot touch the knees when bending (weak fitness indicators). Result: healthy but not fit.
⚙ Added example, Person C: eats well, sleeps well, has supportive friends, and also jogs 30 minutes without trouble and lifts and stretches well. Result: both healthy and fit, which is the ideal integration.

LESSON 4: COMPONENTS OF FITNESS
Fitness is a multidimensional concept that goes beyond simply being active.
It is composed of several key components that together determine an individual's overall physical capability and well-being.
Understanding these elements helps in designing effective training programs and promotes lifelong health and performance.
EASIER: Think of fitness as a toolbox. Each component is a different tool, and a well-rounded person has many tools in working order.
★ Health-related fitness (HRF) covers exercises done with the intention of improving one's physical health and maintaining a healthy lifestyle. It is essential for overall well-being and disease prevention and supports daily function and long-term health.
★ Skill-related fitness (SRF) is the capacity to perform during games and sports. This level of fitness is needed to perform the more technical parts of a wide range of sports. It enhances athletic performance and movement efficiency and is often emphasized in sports and advanced training.
EASIER: HRF is like eating well and sleeping well so your body works for life. SRF is like practicing drills so you can play well in a game.
★ The five health-related fitness components are cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, and body composition.
★ The six skill-related fitness components are speed, agility, balance, coordination, power, and reaction time.
EASIER: Memory trick for SRF: S-A-B-C-P-R stands for Speed, Agility, Balance, Coordination, Power, Reaction time. Memory trick for HRF: heart, strong, long-lasting, bendy, build (cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, body composition).

HEALTH-RELATED FITNESS COMPONENTS
★ Cardiovascular endurance is the ability of the lungs, heart, and blood vessels to deliver enough oxygen to the cells to meet the needs of long-term physical activity.
EASIER: Cardio means heart and vascular means blood vessels. Picture the heart as a pump, the blood vessels as pipes, and oxygen as fuel delivered to the engines (your cells) so they can keep running.
Getting stronger in your heart and lungs can make it easier to carry out the tasks you need to do every day.
Jogging, running, cycling, and swimming can enhance cardiovascular endurance.
This supports aerobic capacity, stamina, and heart health.
EASIER: Aerobic means with oxygen. Aerobic capacity is how much oxygen-powered work your body can do.
★ Muscular strength is the capacity of the muscle to produce force during a relatively short period of time.
EASIER: Strength is how much force you can produce in one go, like lifting one heavy box.
Push-ups, sit-ups, lifting, squats, and lunges promote muscular strength.
It enables lifting, pushing, and functional movement.
★ Muscular endurance is the ability of muscles to perform repeated contractions over time.
EASIER: Endurance is how many times or how long you can keep going, like carrying many small boxes one after another. A contraction is a muscle tightening.
Circuit training and bodyweight exercises are good ways to build muscular endurance.
EASIER: Circuit training means moving through a series of exercise stations. Bodyweight exercises use your own body as the weight.
Muscular endurance promotes posture, injury prevention, and sustained effort.
EASIER: Strength versus endurance: strength is one heavy effort, endurance is many repeated efforts.
★ Flexibility is the ability of a joint or group of joints to move through their full range of motion without pain or restriction.
EASIER: Range of motion is how far a joint can move. A flexible joint is like a smooth hinge, and a stiff joint is like a rusty one.
It enhances mobility, reduces injury risk, and supports recovery.
Even though flexibility varies a lot from person to person, everyone needs to stay within certain minimum ranges to keep their joints and bodies healthy.
Squats, lunges, and stretching can enhance the body's flexibility.
★ Body composition is how much of your body is made up of fat, bone, and muscle.
EASIER: It is about what your body is made of, not only the number on the scale. Two people with the same weight can have different body composition.
It reflects one's nutritional status and physical health.
Body composition is a way for health professionals to figure out if a person is at a healthy weight for their body.
Nutrition and exercise are critical for improving body composition.
Burpees, push-ups, squat jumps, lunges, and planking can improve body composition.

SKILL-RELATED FITNESS COMPONENTS
★ Speed is the ability of a person to move quickly from one point to another, or the maximum rate at which a person can move or cover a distance in a certain amount of time.
EASIER: Speed is how fast you go from point A to point B, like a straight 100-meter sprint.
It supports agility, reaction time, and competitive performance.
★ Agility is the capacity to shift or change the orientation of the body rapidly from one point to another.
EASIER: Agility is quick changes of direction, like zigzagging around cones. Speed is straight-line quickness. Agility is quickness plus turning.
This enhances coordination and sport-specific movement.
A person who competes in track and field exhibits a high level of agility.
Added example: Basketball and football players also show agility when they quickly change direction to dodge an opponent.
★ Balance is the ability to keep your body in place, whether you are standing still (static balance) or moving (dynamic balance).
EASIER: Static balance is holding steady without moving, like standing on one foot. Dynamic balance is staying steady while moving, like walking along a narrow beam.
This is vital for posture, injury prevention, and motor control.
EASIER: Motor control is the brain directing your muscles to move correctly.
People who do gymnastics, yoga, and skiing as sports exhibit great balance.
★ Coordination is the ability to use different parts of the body together smoothly, efficiently, and under control.
EASIER: Dribbling a ball while running uses eyes, hands, and feet together. That teamwork is coordination.
Coordination is a difficult skill that also requires good balance, strength, and agility.
It supports skill acquisition and movement precision.
★ Power is the ability to perform one maximum effort in the shortest possible time. Strength and speed are the two components that combine to create power.
Power is the ability of your muscles to use as much force (strength) as possible in as little time (speed) as possible, like when you run or swim.
Power depends on muscular strength and speed, not on cardiovascular endurance.
This skill is crucial for explosive movements like jumping or sprinting.
EASIER: Think of power as strength plus speed. A sledgehammer is both heavy (strength) and swung fast (speed). Heavy but slow is not power, and fast but weak is not power.
★ Reaction time is the rate at which an individual reacts to an external stimulus.
EASIER: A stimulus is a signal such as a whistle, a light, or a flying ball. Reaction time is how fast you start responding to it.
Athletes can be exposed to different stimuli when they play sports, so reaction time is very crucial for any athlete.
Whether a player wins or loses in a sport is usually decided in a matter of milliseconds, and it depends on how fast the player can react.
Added example: A sprinter pushing off the starting block the instant the starting gun sounds is using reaction time.

HOW TO CLASSIFY AN ACTIVITY INTO A FITNESS COMPONENT
Added example: Use these steps whenever you must name the fitness component behind an activity.
⚙ Step 1: Describe exactly what the body is doing and what limits the performance.
⚙ Step 2: Match that description to the definition of one component.
⚙ Step 3: Decide whether that component is health-related or skill-related.
⚙ Step 4: Check the answer against its look-alike component to avoid confusion.

EXAMPLE: CLASSIFYING EIGHT ACTIVITIES
⚙ Added example, jogging for 20 minutes without stopping: the heart and lungs must deliver oxygen for a long time, so it is cardiovascular endurance, a health-related component.
⚙ Added example, lifting the heaviest weight you can manage once: maximum force in one effort, so it is muscular strength, a health-related component.
⚙ Added example, doing 30 bodyweight squats in a row: the same muscles contract repeatedly, so it is muscular endurance, a health-related component.
⚙ Added example, bending forward and reaching as far as possible: the joints move through their range of motion, so it is flexibility, a health-related component.
⚙ Added example, running a 100-meter sprint in a straight line: moving quickly from one point to another, so it is speed, a skill-related component.
⚙ Added example, zigzagging around cones as fast as possible: quickly changing body direction, so it is agility (not plain speed), a skill-related component.
⚙ Added example, a standing jump for height: force (strength) delivered fast (speed), so it is power, a skill-related component.
⚙ Added example, a volleyball player moving the instant the opponent's spike leaves the hand: responding to an external stimulus, so it is reaction time, a skill-related component.

COMMON MISTAKES
Mistake: Thinking that fit means healthy. A person can be fit but not healthy, and healthy but not fit.
Mistake: Saying power depends on cardiovascular endurance. Power depends on strength and speed.
Mistake: Mixing up the two lists. Body composition and flexibility are health-related, while power and reaction time are skill-related.
Mistake: Confusing muscular strength (one heavy effort) with muscular endurance (many repeated efforts).
Mistake: Confusing speed (straight-line quickness), agility (quick change of direction), and reaction time (how fast you respond to a signal).
Mistake: Confusing static balance (still) with dynamic balance (moving).
Mistake: Mixing up RA 5708 (1969, schools) with CMO No. 39, s. 2021 (PATHFIT for college).
Mistake: Counting the components wrongly. Learn the lists: five health-related and six skill-related.
